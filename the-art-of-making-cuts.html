✂️ The Art of Making Cuts

Heads, hearts and tails: how to separate them by percentage, smell and temperature — the core skill of any distiller.

🛑 The heads fraction contains methanol and acetone. Never drink it and never blend it back into the drinking fraction.

The three fractions

  • Heads: first 8–15% of pure alcohol (12–16% for high-pectin fruit like currant, Isabella, rowan). Sharp solvent smell. Discard.
  • Hearts: the clean drinking fraction; collect until stream strength falls to ~40–45%.
  • Tails: fusel oils and water; cloudy and unpleasant; redistil on a column or discard.

Judging the cuts

  • Percentage: calculate pure alcohol (L × ABV% ÷ 100) and take heads as a percent of that number.
  • Smell: heads smell of solvent/nail polish; hearts smell clean; tails smell oily and damp.
  • Temperature & strength: track stream strength with a hydrometer; hearts end near 40–45%.
  • When in doubt: cut more into heads and stop hearts earlier — safety over yield.
