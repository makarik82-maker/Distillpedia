🍑 Peach Moonshine (Wash Recipe & Distillation)

Peach moonshine is one of the most aromatic distillates with a soft fruit taste in which tones of freshly picked fruit and light astringency are discernible. To fully reveal the potential of the raw material, you need to choose the right yeast, not overdo it with sugar, and distill the wash twice competently.

Category
Fruit
ABV
40–45%
Difficulty
Medium
Fermentation
5–45 days
Yield
~10 kg peaches
⚠️ Legal & Safety Disclaimer: This recipe is provided for educational purposes only. Distilling alcohol at home is illegal in many countries without a proper licence. Always check your local laws. Read our Distillation Safety Guide before attempting any distillation.

Choosing the Ingredients

Peaches

Peaches of any condition are suitable: overripe, picked from the ground, bruised, too small, etc. The color of the skin and pulp does not matter; different varieties can be mixed in one wash. From quality fruit you can make homemade peach wine, and if the drink does not appeal, distill it into moonshine according to this recipe.

Unripe peaches have a high pectin content, which yeast converts into harmful methanol. Although fruit moonshine cannot cause poisoning even with a gross error in distillation technology, the methanol concentration should still be minimized at the raw material processing stage. Unripe peaches should be left for 5–6 days under direct sunlight so that the fruit becomes soft and without green spots on the skin.

Amygdalin in peach pits is safe after distillation, but adds a harsh bitter almond taste to the moonshine that spoils the soft fruit tones, so the pits are necessarily removed before fermentation. The skin also brings light bitterness and spicy notes; if possible, this part is cut off, but it is important to consider that "wild" yeast lives precisely on the skin.

Yield: depending on variety and growing region, peaches contain 5–10% sugar (usually 7–8%). This sugar content allows counting on 60–120 ml of 40° peach moonshine from 1 kg of fruit, but in practice the yield will be 15–20% lower due to losses of ethyl alcohol during double fractional distillation. This means making peach moonshine without sugar is advisable only if there is more than 15–20 kg of raw material.

Sugar, Water, Acid, Yeast

  • Sugar (optional): 1 kg gives an additional 1.2 L of 40% moonshine, but partially levels the fruit organoleptics. Empirically, up to 200 g of sugar per 1 kg of peaches can be added (optimally 100–150 g). Better to replace with neutral analogs — fructose or dextrose.
  • Water: most peach varieties are not juicy enough; thick wash ferments slowly and scorches during distillation. Water also controls sugar content (should not exceed 20%). Any drinking water, preferably chlorine-free.
  • Citric acid: peaches have few natural acids for stable fermentation; easiest to balance with citric acid.
  • Yeast (optional): if fruit was not treated with chemicals and picked at least a day after rain, wild yeast can be used. Cultured wine yeast for white grape juice or universal strains are best. Spirit yeast finishes in 4–10 days but organoleptics become noticeably worse. Fruit-wash yeast is justified for unripe peaches. Baker's yeast is the worst choice.

Ingredients

  • Peaches — 10 kg (weight with pits)
  • Sugar (optional) — up to 200 g per 1 kg of peaches
  • Water — 0.6 L per 1 kg of peaches, plus 4 L per 1 kg of sugar
  • Citric acid — 6 g per 1 kg of peaches
  • Yeast or starter (optional) — per label instructions

Baker's yeast: 100 g pressed or 20 g dry per 1 kg of total sugar (in fruit + added). Approximate calculations suffice: extra yeast won't harm but won't speed up fermentation.

Step-by-Step Process

1
Prepare the fruit. Cut out darkened, moldy, and rotten parts from the peaches. Divide the fruit in half, then remove the pits (they give strong almond bitterness) without damaging the integrity of the hard shell. Ideally the skin should also be cut off, but this is a laborious process, and moreover "wild" yeast lives on the skin.
2
Crush by hand. Crush the pulp by hand to a puree state. Do not use a meat grinder, blender, or other mechanical methods, otherwise the wash will be difficult to filter before distillation.
3
Transfer to vessel. Put the peach puree into a wide-neck fermentation vessel.
4
Activate yeast. Activate yeast per manufacturer's instructions, or use the universal starter method.
5
Make the wash. Dissolve sugar and citric acid in water. To speed up fermentation, you can preheat the water to 32–35 °C, but no higher. Pour the resulting solution into the fermentation vessel. Mix until homogeneous. If the wort turned out thick, add more water: the wash should mix easily. Optionally add yeast or starter. Fill the vessel to a maximum of 75%, leaving room for foam and carbon dioxide.
6
Seal and ferment. Install an airlock. Leave the peach wash in a dark room at 18–28 °C. Within the first 36 hours, signs of fermentation will appear: gas bubbles from the airlock and foam near the surface. If this did not happen, find the cause and fix the situation urgently.
7
Monitor the cap. Watch the wash for the first 3–5 days so that the floating pomace (particles of skin and pulp) does not block the carbon dioxide outlet from the airlock. If necessary, periodically open and stir the wash, submerging the pomace in the liquid. Short contact with oxygen will not harm. When almost all the pulp settles at the bottom, leave the wash alone.
8
Confirm completion. Depending on the yeast used and room temperature, peach wash ferments for 5–45 days. Signs of a completed process: the airlock releases fewer than 5 bubbles per minute; almost no foam on the surface (sometimes "islands" up to 3 cm in diameter remain); the liquid part became noticeably lighter, and a layer of loose sediment accumulated at the bottom; the wash smells of alcohol, is not sweet to taste, and has a light bitterness (yeast converted all sugar into ethyl alcohol).

Distillation

9
Filter. Drain the fermented peach wash from the sediment through a tube (like gasoline from a tank). Squeeze the pomace dry. Filter the wash through cheesecloth or a fine sieve to remove pulp residues that could scorch. Clarifying with bentonite or other methods is desirable. If after all manipulations the wash remains thickish, it must be diluted with water to a liquid state, otherwise scorching is inevitable.
10
Fill the still. Pour the wash into the distillation pot, filling 30–75% of the total volume.
11
Assemble equipment. Assemble the equipment in distillation mode: column height up to 60 cm with no packing inside, dephlegmator off, leaving only a coil or shell-and-tube condenser in the system, plus one bubble trap if provided by design. This mode is called pot still in factory columns.
12
First distillation. Distill the wash at maximum speed: moonshine may flow cloudy and warm, but there must be no steam. When the strength in the stream drops below 25%, end the distillation. Running "to water" is acceptable if the additional energy and time costs seem justified by a slight increase in drink quantity.
13
Calculate. Measure the alcohol content of the resulting distillate at 20 °C, or make a correction for another temperature in an online moonshine calculator. Calculate the amount of pure (absolute) alcohol: strength in percent multiplied by volume in liters, divided by 100.
14
Dilute. Dilute the moonshine with water to 18–20 degrees. Do not clean with milk, charcoal, or other methods, preserving the peach organoleptics.
15
Second distillation — heads. Make the second distillation: after the raw spirit boils, reduce the power so the distillate flows in small drops and is cold like water in the apparatus. Collect the first 15% of output from the amount of pure alcohol separately. This is the "head" — the initial harmful fraction that is not suitable for drinking due to high concentration of harmful impurities.
16
Collect the hearts. Smoothly raise the apparatus power, achieving "fast" drops or a thin stream of moonshine, while the drink must remain cold. Collect the "body" (drinkable part of peach moonshine) until the strength in the stream drops below 43%. Then turn off the equipment or accumulate "tails" separately to rectify them on a column in the future.
17
Dilute and rest. Dilute peach moonshine ("body") with softened drinking water to 40–45% strength. Pour into glass bottles or jars, close tightly, and leave for at least 4 days in a dark cool place to stabilize. Shelf life is unlimited.
💡 Editor's tip: To preserve peach tones to the maximum, it is better to reassemble the column in pot still mode for the second run and follow this recipe. For the second distillation, it is desirable to place one section of copper RPN or SPN packing in the column to increase the efficiency of removing sulfur compounds. Stainless packing is useless and only slows the process.
