🍒 Compote Moonshine (Old, Fermented, or Slightly Mouldy)

Compote moonshine is slightly milder than regular sugar moonshine, with almost no aroma or taste of the original raw material. First of all, this is a good way to process unwanted compote, obtaining an excellent alcohol base for tinctures and liqueurs. The distillate can also be consumed neat if desired.

Category
Fruit
ABV
40–45%
Difficulty
Medium
Fermentation
4–50 days
Yield
~5 L compote
⚠️ Legal & Safety Disclaimer: This recipe is provided for educational purposes only. Distilling alcohol at home is illegal in many countries without a licence. Always check your local laws. Read our Distillation Safety Guide before attempting any distillation.

Ingredients

  • 5 L compote — any homemade compote: old (stored more than 3 years), fermented, or slightly mouldy (but with pleasant aroma, no mustiness)
  • Sugar (optional): up to 100 g per 1 L compote
  • Water (if sugar exceeds 20%): 1 L per 1 kg sugar
  • Yeast: spirit, wine, or baker's yeast depending on wash volume

Spirit and wine yeast per package instructions. Baker's yeast: 20 g dry or 100 g pressed per 1 kg sugar (added + natural in compote).

🛑 Important: Do NOT use sour compote with vinegar or acetone smell. Avoid compotes where sugar was replaced with synthetic sweeteners (aspartame, sucralose, etc.) — yeast cannot ferment them. Store-bought compotes often contain preservatives and chemical additives that worsen the organoleptic properties. Always discard the "heads" fraction. See Distillation Safety Guide.

Step-by-Step Process

1
Remove the bloom. If using mouldy compote, remove the mould layer (bloom). Bring to a boil and simmer for 2–3 minutes. Remove from heat, cover, and cool to a temperature comfortable for yeast — below 30 °C.
2
Activate the yeast. Spirit or fruit yeast per package instructions. Pressed yeast can be added directly from the refrigerator, crumbled with clean hands. Dry baker's yeast: sprinkle evenly over the surface of sugar syrup (10 ml water and 1 g sugar per 1 g yeast) at 26–30 °C, leave for 5 minutes, then stir to remove lumps, transfer to a dark place at room temperature, and wait 10–20 minutes until stable foam appears.
3
Strain the compote. Strain through cheesecloth or a kitchen sieve, removing fruit or berry residues that could worsen the moonshine with rotten tones.
4
Add sugar and water (optional). Add sugar and water to the compote. Stir until homogeneous — crystals should not settle at the bottom. Total sugar content of the wash — up to 20%. Check with a hydrometer, refractometer, or household vinometer.
5
Transfer to fermentation vessel. Pour the compote into the fermentation vessel, filling up to 75% of volume — free space is needed for foam and CO₂. Add the diluted yeast.
6
Ferment. Fit an airlock and move to a dark room (or cover) at 18–28 °C. Compote wash on spirit or baker's yeast ferments for 4–15 days, on wine yeast or starter — up to 50 days. Signs of completion: airlock bubbles fewer than 5 times per minute or glove deflates, almost no foam near the surface, wash clarified with sediment at bottom, smells like alcohol and tastes dry (all sugar converted by yeast).
7
Siphon off sediment. Siphon the fermented wash off sediment (preferably through a tube), optionally clarify with bentonite. Transfer to the still, filling 30–70% of volume.
8
Assemble the equipment. Set up in pot still mode: column height up to 65 cm with no packing, disable all auxiliary units, leave only one thump keg (if included in the design) and a direct-flow cooler (coil or shell-and-tube). In factory column equipment, the required mode is usually called "pot still."
9
First distillation. Distill at maximum speed: low wines may come out warm and cloudy, but must be without steam. Turn off the equipment when the ABV in the stream drops below 25%. It is permissible to distill almost "to water" if additional energy costs seem justified by a slight increase in quantity.
10
Measure and calculate. Measure the ABV of the low wines at 20 °C, or adjust the value for actual temperature using an online calculator. Determine the amount of pure (absolute) alcohol: multiply ABV% by volume in litres and divide by 100.
11
Dilute the low wines. Dilute with water to 18–20%. If there are pronounced fusel oil tones, you can clean with activated charcoal, but then not even a hint of the fruit or berry origin of the drink will remain.
12
Second distillation. After the liquid boils, reduce the heating power so the moonshine drips slowly and is cold as water in the system. Collect the first 14% of the pure alcohol volume (calculated at step 10) separately. This is the "heads" — the initial fraction with high concentration of harmful substances, unsuitable for consumption.
13
Collect the hearts. Smoothly increase the power, achieving fast drops or a thin stream, but the drink must remain cold. Collect the drinking portion ("hearts") until the ABV in the stream drops below 43%. Then turn off the equipment or collect "tails" separately to rectify them later on a column.
14
Dilute and rest. Dilute the compote moonshine ("hearts") with softened water to drinking strength — 40–45%. Transfer to glass bottles or jars, seal tightly. Leave for at least 3 days in a cool place to stabilize. Shelf life is unlimited.
💡 Editor's tip: Different compotes can be mixed in one wash (e.g., apple, strawberry, cherry) since unique berry and fruit notes are lost during boiling anyway. The result will be a good neutral base for tinctures.
