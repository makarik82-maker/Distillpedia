🧫 Fermentation Science

How yeast converts sugar to alcohol: yeast types, temperature, pH, hydromodule, oxygen — and how to rescue a stuck wash.

Fermentation is the biological stage that feeds your still: yeast eats simple sugars and produces ethanol, CO₂ and flavor compounds (esters). A healthy, calm fermentation gives a clean wash; a stressed one loads the distillate with fusel oils and off-flavors. Basic equation: C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2 C₂H₅OH + 2 CO₂ + heat. From 1 kg of sugar you theoretically get ~640 ml of pure alcohol (~1.2 L of 40% spirit); real yield is ~1 L after losses.

Phases of fermentation

  1. Adaptation (0–12 h): yeast reproduces, consumes dissolved oxygen; foam and hissing appear.
  2. Active phase (1–7 days): vigorous CO₂ release, sugar drops fast, temperature rises.
  3. Slow phase: sugar depletes, activity fades, yeast settles.
  4. Finished wash: not sweet, slightly bitter, smells of alcohol, clear layer over loose sediment.

Yeast types

  • Spirit yeast: fast (4–8 days), low impurities, high alcohol tolerance; the default for sugar and grain washes.
  • Wine yeast: slow (20–50 days) but produces esters that soften fruit distillates; best for fruit washes.
  • Fruit-wash yeast: spirit yeast + pectinase enzymes — less methanol from pectin; good for unripe fruit.
  • Baker’s yeast: cheap and available, but adds kvass/bread tones and more foam; a last resort.
  • Wild yeast: free, but unpredictable and slow; verify with a starter 3–5 days ahead.
  • Koji / enzymes A & G: for cold saccharification of starch-based washes.

Temperature

  • Optimal: 20–26 °C; acceptable 18–30 °C.
  • Below 18 °C yeast slows or sleeps; above 32–33 °C most strains die.
  • Large volumes self-heat by 3–4 °C; above 200 L monitor constantly — yeast dies at 35 °C+.

Acidity (pH)

  • Target: 4.0–4.5 pH. Neutral (7–8) raises infection risk; alkaline (9–10) accumulates glycerin and acetic acid.
  • Adjust with citric acid: ~5 g per 1 kg of sugar (one teaspoon without a heap).
  • Lemon juice works too: one medium lemon contains 5–6 g of acid.

Sugar & hydromodule

  • Hydromodule 1:3–1:5 (sugar:water). 1:3 = 20% sugar — the maximum for ordinary yeast.
  • 1:4 = 15%, 1:5 = 12%. Above 30% sugar acts as a preservative and stops fermentation.
  • Most strains tolerate only 12–14% ABV in the wash; a fully fermented 20% sugar syrup yields ~12% ABV wash.
  • Keep fruit-wash sugar additions modest (see each recipe) to preserve organoleptics.

Oxygen & the airlock

  • Initial stage needs a little oxygen (unboiled water provides it).
  • Constant air access later = vinegar bacteria = sour wash. Always fit an airlock.
  • During active fermentation CO₂ pushes oxygen away; when activity slows, the airlock becomes critical.

Signs the wash is ready

  • Airlock bubbles <5 per minute, or the glove deflated.
  • Foam gone (small “islands” up to 3 cm may remain).
  • Wash lightened; loose sediment layer at the bottom.
  • Not sweet (the key sign), slightly bitter, smells of alcohol.

Troubleshooting a stuck wash

🛑 Checklist if fermentation never starts or stops early:
  • Temperature: bring to 20–26 °C; if yeast died above 33 °C, pitch fresh yeast.
  • Sugar too high (>20%): dilute with water.
  • Chlorinated water: use settled/filtered water next time.
  • Preservatives in store juice/jam/candy: boil the wort to break them down.
  • Dead yeast: re-pitch; for wild-yeast failures use a starter or cultured yeast.
  • Infection (vinegar smell, mold): boil, cool, re-pitch in a sanitized vessel; discard moldy batches.
💡 Faster is not better: a calm fermentation at 20–26 °C with proper acidity and nutrition accumulates fewer impurities than any “turbo” shortcut.
