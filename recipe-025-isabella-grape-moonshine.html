🍇 Isabella Grape Moonshine (Proper Wash Recipe and Distillation)

Isabella grape moonshine has an original strawberry-like aftertaste that you won't find in distillates from other grape varieties. However, the preparation technology has nuances due to the high pectin content in the berries. The proposed wash recipe takes into account the variety's characteristics, and the distillation method removes harmful impurities to acceptable concentrations while preserving substances responsible for aroma and taste.

Category
Fruit
ABV
38–45%
Difficulty
Advanced
Fermentation
5–45 days
Yield
~20 kg grapes
⚠️ Legal & Safety Disclaimer: This recipe is provided for educational purposes only. Distilling alcohol at home is illegal in many countries without a licence. Always check your local laws. Read our Distillation Safety Guide before attempting any distillation.

Why Isabella is Special

Isabella grape wash contains elevated methanol concentration — 150–200 mg/L, while other varieties have only 50–120 mg/L. This is due to the high pectin content, which yeast converts to methanol. Unripe berries contain several times more pectin than fully ripe ones. Although even 600 mg/L concentration cannot cause poisoning from homemade Isabella wine, proper fractional distillation removes almost all methanol, and it is more correct to avoid this harmful impurity already at the wash preparation stage.

Ingredients

  • Isabella grapes — 20 kg (weight of berries without stems)
  • Sugar (not recommended) — up to 4 kg (maximum 1 kg per 5 kg berries)
  • Water — 3 L per 1 kg sugar
  • Yeast (wine or spirit) — per package instructions

Wine starter requires 3% of wash volume. Baker's yeast: 100 g pressed or 20 g dry per 1 kg of calculated sugar (sum of natural and added). Approximate dosages are sufficient: excess yeast won't harm, but won't accelerate fermentation either.

🛑 Important: Isabella grapes contain 16–20% sugar (up to 20–22% in southern regions). This means 190–264 ml of 40% moonshine per 1 kg of berries, but about 20% of ethanol will be lost after two distillations. If you have sufficient raw material (at least 15–20 kg), it is desirable to make Isabella moonshine without sugar, preserving the rich berry organoleptics. Always discard the "heads" fraction — 16% in this recipe (higher than usual due to elevated methanol). See Distillation Safety Guide.

Step-by-Step Process

1
Prepare the grapes. Remove berries from clusters (stems). Reject rotten, mouldy, and unripe fruits. If using "wild" yeast for fermentation, do not wash the grapes. Stems add astringency, which is good for Isabella chacha (based on pomace), but undesirable for classic grape moonshine when a "soft" brandy taste is required.
2
Crush the berries. Mash berries by hand or another method, without damaging most seeds, otherwise the moonshine will have a bitter almond aftertaste.
3
Place in container. Put the berry mass in a pot or bucket.
4
Activate yeast and prepare wash. Activate yeast per manufacturer's instructions (if using). Dissolve sugar (optional) in water, then add syrup to the grapes. Add yeast or starter to the must. Stir until consistency is homogeneous. For normal fermentation, grape wash should be liquid: easily stirrable. If necessary (usually only for low-juice grapes), add up to 150 ml water per 1 kg berries. Water won't affect the quality of the finished moonshine.
5
Initial fermentation (combined scheme). Tie the bucket (pot) neck with cheesecloth. Leave the wash in a dark place at 18–25 °C for 3 days. Stir every 12 hours, sinking floating pulp and skin particles into the juice. Wash without stirring may sour, worsening the moonshine organoleptics. Foam should appear within the first 36 hours, indicating fermentation has started.
6
Filter the wash. Strain the wash through cheesecloth or a kitchen sieve, squeezing the pulp dry (no longer needed). The better the filtration, the better.
7
Continue fermentation. Pour the liquid part into the fermentation vessel, filling max 75% of volume. Close the wash with any airlock design. Leave in a dark place at 20–26 °C. The "combined" fermentation scheme proposed in this recipe (first with pulp and skin, then pure juice) sufficiently saturates the wash with dry matter and increases yield even from mediocre quality berries, while simultaneously allowing control of the strawberry aftertaste intensity so it doesn't become intrusive, and minimizes methanol. Classic grape moonshine is usually made by the "red" scheme (wash not filtered before distillation), and homemade cognac by the "white" scheme (only pure juice ferments, but raw material quality must be excellent).
8
Confirm fermentation is complete. Depending on the chosen yeast, sugar content, and temperature, Isabella grape wash ferments for 5–45 days. Signs of completion: gas release from airlock stopped (glove deflated); almost no foam remains on the surface; wash smells like alcohol and is not sweet (all sugar converted by yeast).
9
Filter before distillation. Siphon the finished wash off sediment, then strain through a fine sieve or several layers of cheesecloth to remove solid particles that could burn.
10
Assemble the equipment. Assemble the column or moonshine apparatus in simple distillation mode: minimal column height (up to 60 cm), with shell-and-tube or coil cooler, disable dephlegmator. You can leave only one thump keg if provided in the design. In factory apparatuses, the appropriate mode is usually called pot still.
11
First distillation. Pour the wash into the still, filling 30–75% of volume. Distill the first time at maximum speed: moonshine may come out slightly cloudy and warm, but must be without steam. Finish distillation when the ABV in the stream drops below 20%, or continue almost to zero ABV if time, energy, and water costs seem justified.
12
Measure and calculate. Measure the ABV of the first distillation moonshine. Calculate the amount of absolute (anhydrous) alcohol: ABV percentage multiplied by volume in litres divided by 100. Dilute the distillate with water to 18–20%. Do not clean with wood charcoal or other methods, preserving the grape-strawberry organoleptics.
13
Second distillation with cuts. Distill the second time: after boiling, adjust power so the moonshine flows slowly in small drops and is cold, almost like water in the apparatus. Collect separately the first 16% of liquid from the amount of pure alcohol. This fraction (called "heads") contains high concentration of harmful impurities, so it is not suitable for drinking. The large percentage of "heads" in Isabella grape moonshine is due to the need to get rid of elevated methanol concentration.
14
Collect the hearts. Smoothly raise the power so the moonshine flows in a thin stream or drips quickly, but remains cold. Collect the drinking part ("body") until the ABV in the stream drops below 42%. Then finish distillation or collect "tails" separately to process this fraction on a rectification column in the future.
15
Dilute and rest. Dilute the drinking part of Isabella moonshine with softened water to 38–45% ABV. Pour into glass bottles or jars, close tightly, and leave in a cool place for 5 days to stabilize the taste. Shelf life is unlimited.
💡 Editor's tip: For the second distillation, you can add one section of copper packing (SPN or RPN) to the column to better remove sulfur impurities. Stainless steel packing is useless in this case. The unique strawberry-like aftertaste of Isabella is what makes this moonshine special — don't lose it by over-cleaning or using a full rectification column.
