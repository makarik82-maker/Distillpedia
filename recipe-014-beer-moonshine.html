🍺 Beer Moonshine (Proper Distillation Technology)

Beer is distilled into moonshine in two cases: when you have expired store-bought beer, or when the taste of homemade beer doesn't satisfy for some reason. During double distillation, the main goal is to remove hop essential oils as much as possible, otherwise the moonshine will have a rather specific taste. The entire technology is built around this feature.

Category
Beer
ABV
40–45%
Difficulty
Medium
Fermentation
N/A (ready-made)
Yield
~10 L beer
⚠️ Legal & Safety Disclaimer: This recipe is provided for educational purposes only. Distilling alcohol at home is illegal in many countries without a licence. Always check your local laws. Read our Distillation Safety Guide before attempting any distillation.

Ingredients

  • 10+ L beer — homemade or expired (any type: barley, wheat, light, dark, live, pasteurized, canned, bottled)
  • Bottled water — for diluting the finished moonshine

Different beer types can be mixed since the final taste will be approximately the same. Less hops (bitterness, IBU) and more malt (dry matter percentage) is better — richer bread-malt tones and fewer inappropriate hop notes.

🛑 Important: Do not distill beer with additives (cherry, lemon, orange flavor) — at high temperatures, these unnatural ingredients saturate the moonshine with an irremovable "chemical" taste. Do not distill sour beer with vinegar tones — the yield will be minimal and the taste disgusting. Always discard the "heads" fraction. See Distillation Safety Guide.
💡 Note: It's better to distill beer outdoors or in a non-residential premises with good ventilation, as during degassing and especially the first distillation, a specific smell will appear.

Step-by-Step Process

1
Degas the beer. Method 1 (mechanical): pour into a plastic bucket or pot (fill max 2/3 volume), then stir for 3–7 minutes with a drill or hammer drill with a construction attachment (plaster mixer) until foam and characteristic smell disappear. Method 2 (thermal): heat the beer to 50 °C in a metal pot, bucket, or directly in the still (leave at least 25% volume free). Maintain this temperature for 15–20 minutes, stirring every 3–5 minutes, while foam is visible, hissing is heard, and CO₂ bubbles rise from the bottom. Mechanical degassing is more effective than thermal.
2
Transfer to the still. Pour degassed beer into the still, filling 30–70% of volume. Dilute with regular water if necessary.
3
Assemble the equipment. Set up in pot still mode: minimal column height, no packing, disable dephlegmator, leave only the cooler (shell-and-tube or coil).
4
First distillation. Achieve stable drop-by-drop output, then collect the first 5 ml from each liter of beer separately and discard — this fraction contains excess aromatic hop substances that worsen organoleptic properties. Increase heating power; distillate may come out in a stream and be warm (up to 40 °C), but must be without steam. Stop when ABV drops below 25%.
5
Measure and calculate. Determine the amount of pure alcohol: ABV% × volume in litres ÷ 100.
6
Dilute and clean. Dilute the first-run distillate to 18% ABV. If possible, clean with birch or coconut activated charcoal to reduce intrusive hop tones. Other cleaning methods are not advisable.
7
Second distillation with cuts. Adjust the apparatus so the moonshine comes out in small drops and stays cool. Collect the first 10% of pure alcohol volume separately and discard — this is the "heads" fraction with high concentration of harmful substances.
8
Collect the hearts. Smoothly increase heating power: moonshine may flow into the receiving vessel in drops or a thin stream, but must remain cool. Collect the drinking portion ("hearts") until ABV drops below 42%, then stop distillation.
9
Dilute and rest. Dilute the finished "beer" moonshine with soft water to 40–45% ABV, transfer to glass containers, seal tightly, and leave for 5–7 days in a cool place to stabilize the taste. Organoleptic properties depend on the initial properties of the beer — the result is difficult to predict.
💡 Editor's tip: For a neutral spirit without malt and hop tones, the second distillation must be done on a rectification column. For simple stills, the beer character will remain, which is not necessarily bad — it gives a bread-malt profile.
