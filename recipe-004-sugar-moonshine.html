🍬 Sugar Moonshine (Classic Beginner's Recipe)

The classic starting point for most home distillers. Simple ingredients, straightforward process, and a clean neutral spirit when done correctly. Master this recipe before moving to fruit or grain distillates.

Category
Sugar
ABV
40–45%
Difficulty
Beginner
Fermentation
3–10 days
Yield
~5 L (from 6 kg sugar)
⚠️ Legal & Safety Disclaimer: This recipe is provided for educational purposes only. Distilling alcohol at home is illegal in many countries without a licence. Always check your local laws. Read our Distillation Safety Guide before attempting any distillation.

Ingredients (for ~5 L of 40% moonshine)

  • 6 kg sugar — pure cane or beet sugar, no sweeteners (avoid E420, E951, E954)
  • 24 L water — chlorine-free, medium hardness (3–7 °dH)
  • 120 g dry yeast or 600 g pressed yeast (or per spirit yeast instructions)
  • 30 g citric acid + 60 g for sugar inversion
  • Optional: nutrient (tomato paste, black bread, or fresh berries), bentonite clay, activated charcoal

Theoretical yield: 1.2 L of 40% moonshine per 1 kg sugar. Practical yield: ~1 L per kg due to losses.

🛑 Safety reminder: The "heads" fraction contains methanol and other harmful compounds. Always discard it — never consume. See Distillation Safety Guide.

Step-by-Step Process

1
Sanitize everything. Sterilize all tools and vessels with boiling water, then wipe dry with a clean cloth. Wash hands thoroughly before handling the wash.
2
Prepare the water. If using tap water, let it sit for 24–48 hours in an open container to allow chlorine to evaporate. Do not use the bottom 5–7 cm layer (sediment). Do not use boiled or distilled water without aeration.
3
Invert the sugar (optional but recommended). Heat 0.5 L water per 1 kg sugar to 70–80 °C. Add sugar gradually, bring to a boil, simmer 10 minutes. Add citric acid (10 g per 1 kg sugar) slowly — the syrup will foam. Cover and simmer at 80+ °C for 60 minutes. Cool below 30 °C before adding to the wash. Inversion speeds up fermentation and reduces harmful byproducts.
4
Activate the yeast. For dry yeast: mix 50 g sugar with 300 ml water at 26–30 °C in a jar. Sprinkle yeast on the surface, cover, and wait 10–40 minutes until foamy. For pressed yeast: crumble directly into the wash. For spirit yeast: follow package instructions.
5
Mix the wash. Combine sugar (inverted or regular) with water in the fermentation vessel. Add citric acid (5 g per 1 kg sugar). Stir until fully dissolved. Fill the vessel no more than 75% full. Water can be pre-warmed to 27–30 °C.
6
Add nutrients (recommended). For every 10 L of wash, add one of: 100 g tomato paste, 10 crushed fresh berries (grape, strawberry, raspberry), or ⅓ loaf of natural black bread. Stir to distribute evenly. This speeds up fermentation by ~2 days.
7
Pitch the yeast and seal. Add activated yeast to the wash. Fit an airlock. Move to a dark place with stable temperature 18–30 °C (optimal 20–26 °C). Avoid temperature fluctuations.
8
Ferment. Normal fermentation time: 3–10 days (usually 4–7). Signs of completion: airlock stops bubbling (fewer than 5 bubbles/min), wash clarifies with sediment at bottom, no foam on surface, smells like alcohol, tastes dry (not sweet) with slight bitterness.
9
Degas and clarify (recommended). Siphon the wash off sediment, heat to 50 °C (no higher) while stirring until foaming stops. Return to the vessel. Add bentonite clay (20 g per 10 L, pre-mixed with warm water to a cream consistency). Let sit 24 hours, then siphon off the clarified wash. Do not pour bentonite residue down the drain — it clogs pipes.
10
First distillation. Filter the wash through a fine sieve or cheesecloth. Fill the still 30–75% full. Distill at maximum speed. Collect the first 50 ml per 1 kg of sugar separately (300 ml for this recipe) — this is the "heads" fraction, discard it. Stop when the ABV in the stream drops below 40%.
11
Intermediate cleaning (optional). Dilute the distillate to 18–20% ABV. Add activated charcoal (30 g/L for BAU-A or BAU-LV, or 10 g/L for coconut charcoal KAU-A). Stir, wait a few minutes, seal, and let sit 2–3 hours, shaking every 20–30 minutes. Do not exceed 6 hours. Filter through cheesecloth, then through cotton to remove charcoal dust.
12
Second distillation with cuts. Dilute the distillate to 18–20% ABV. Heat slowly. Collect the first 50–60 ml per 1 kg of sugar as "heads" — discard this fraction. Increase heat slightly and collect the "hearts" until the ABV in the stream drops below 41–42%. Optionally collect "tails" separately for future redistillation.
13
Dilute and rest. Dilute the "hearts" with quality bottled water to 40–45% ABV. The moonshine may turn slightly cloudy at first but will clear within 24 hours in the cold. Bottle in glass, seal tightly, and rest for at least 3–4 days (preferably in a fridge or cellar) to stabilize the flavor.
💡 Editor's tip: Double distillation with proper cuts is the single most important step. It can compensate for almost any other mistake in the process. Do not skip it, and do not rush the cuts.
