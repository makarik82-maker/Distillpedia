📖 What is Home Distilling?

Introduction to the craft: distillation first, brewing second. Terminology, still types, fractions and the beginner path.

Home distilling is the art and science of producing spirits — moonshine, brandy, whiskey, vodka — by fermenting sugar-rich raw materials and then separating alcohol through controlled evaporation and condensation. On this wiki distillation comes first: it is the skill that turns a simple fermented wash into a refined drink. Brewing (making the wash) is the essential second step that feeds your still.

⚠️ Educational Purposes Only: distilling alcohol at home is illegal in many countries without a proper licence. Always check your local laws before starting.

Part 1: Distillation — the heart of moonshine

What is distillation?

Distillation is a physical separation process based on different boiling points: ethanol boils at 78.3 °C, water at 100 °C. Heating the fermented wash vaporizes alcohol first; the vapor is then cooled and condensed back into liquid — this is your distillate.

Types of stills

  • Pot still (alembic): simple vessel + direct-flow condenser. Keeps maximum flavor and aroma — the right choice for fruit and grain distillates.
  • Reflux / rectification column: vertical column with packing. Produces clean, high-proof neutral spirit — the right choice for vodka and tincture bases.
  • Hybrid systems: pot still base with an adjustable column; flexibility for different spirit styles.

The three fractions of every run

  1. Heads (foreshots): first 8–15% of pure alcohol. Contains methanol, acetone and other harmful volatiles. Never drink.
  2. Hearts (body): the middle, drinkable fraction — your moonshine.
  3. Tails (feints): last fraction with fusel oils and water; redistil or discard.
🛑 Double fractional distillation (two runs with proper cuts) is the single most important safety practice. It removes almost all harmful impurities even if other steps were imperfect.

Part 2: Brewing — making the wash

Brewing is the biological stage: yeast converts sugars into ethanol and CO₂. The resulting liquid — the wash (or mash for grains) — is the raw material for your still. A poorly fermented wash produces a harsh distillate no matter how good your still is.

  • Sugar wash: sugar + water + yeast. Neutral base, beginner-friendly.
  • Fruit / berry wash: natural fructose; aromatic brandy-style distillates.
  • Grain wash: starch must first be converted to sugar (saccharification) with malt or enzymes.
  • Honey, juice, jam washes: specialty bases with their own nuances.

For the deep dive into yeast, temperature, pH and hydromodule see the Fermentation Science guide.

Essential terminology

Distillation terms

  • ABV — alcohol by volume, %.
  • Absolute (pure) alcohol — volume in litres × ABV% ÷ 100.
  • Pot still mode — column assembled without packing and with the dephlegmator off.
  • Thump keg (bubble trap) — splash protector; can also hold aromatics.
  • Copper packing (SPN/RPN) — removes sulfur compounds; stainless packing does not.

Brewing terms

  • Wash / mash — fermented liquid ready for distillation.
  • Wort — sweet liquid before yeast is pitched.
  • Hydromodule — sugar-to-water ratio (typically 1:4).
  • Saccharification — converting starch to fermentable sugars (malt or enzymes).
  • Airlock — lets CO₂ out, keeps oxygen and bacteria out.

Getting started: the beginner path

  1. Learn the safety rules — Distillation Safety Guide.
  2. Assemble your kit — Equipment Checklist.
  3. Make your first sugar wash and distil it twice with proper cuts.
  4. Move to fruit washes, then grain washes with saccharification.
  5. Experiment: oak aging, vapor infusion, zero-proof distillates.
💡 Do not chase speed. A calm, controlled fermentation plus two careful runs beats any “turbo” shortcut.
