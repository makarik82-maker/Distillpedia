🎃 Pumpkin Moonshine

An original vegetable-fruit aroma; the taste reveals banana, melon and honey-nut shades. Almost impossible to guess the raw material — especially when the wash is set correctly.

Category
Fruit / Vegetable
ABV
40–45%
Difficulty
Medium
Fermentation
5–45 days
Yield
36–180 ml/kg (40%)
⚠️ Educational purposes only. Home distillation is illegal in many countries without a licence. Check your local laws first.

Choosing the Ingredients

Pumpkin

Any ripe pumpkin of any variety works. Unripe fruits contain much pectin (more methanol and a heavier hangover) and give pronounced vegetable tones. After harvest, let pumpkins rest for 2–3 months so part of the starch splits into sugar.

Sugar content ranges 3–15% depending on variety, region and weather: theoretically 36–180 ml of 40° spirit per 1 kg, with up to 20% lost in two fractional runs. Table varieties (Melonnaya, Sakharnaya, Atlant, Nadezhda and similar) are sweeter; the most aromatic spirit comes from muscat varieties (Vitaminaya, Butternut, Arabskaya, Kroshka). Fodder pumpkin has low sugar (8–10% at best) and weak organoleptics.

Starch note: pumpkin pulp holds only 2–6% starch (less when ripe). Converting it would raise yield by a couple of percent but needs malt (hot) or enzymes A+G (cold); with less than 100 kg of pumpkin the extra effort is not worth it. For comparison: potato holds 12–20%, corn up to 70%. This recipe does not use saccharification.

Sugar, water, yeast

  • Sugar (optional): 1 kg adds 1.2 L of 40% but adds kvass/molasses tones. Empirically up to 150 g per 1 kg of pumpkin (less for sweeter pulp). Dextrose or fructose in the same proportion affect the spirit less.
  • Water: thick wash ferments slowly and sours easily, so water is added. If you use pure juice without extra sugar, no dilution is needed. Non-chlorinated water preferred; stand tap water 3 days in an open vessel. Dilute the finished spirit only with softened water; boiled water is unsuitable.
  • Yeast: the skin lacks enough wild yeast, so cultured strains are required. Spirit yeast is best for speed and organoleptics; for unripe pulp use fruit-wash yeast (pectinase reduces methanol). Wine yeast or a raisin starter give only slightly more aroma but ferment up to 1.5 months — worthwhile only for quality table varieties without added sugar. Baker's yeast ferments in up to 12 days but adds bread/kvass tones; use only when nothing else is available.

Ingredients

  • Pumpkin — 20 kg (weight of unpeeled fruits)
  • Water — 20 L, plus 4 L per 1 kg of sugar
  • Sugar — up to 150 g per 1 kg of pumpkin
  • Yeast — per package instructions (baker's: 20 g dry or 100 g pressed per 1 kg of total sugar)

Wash Preparation

1
Wash the pumpkins thoroughly — soil residues give an earthy taste. Halve them, remove seeds, the soft core, rotten and mouldy parts, the "tail" and the brown circle on the opposite side, then cut off the skin.
2
Cut the pulp into 3–4 cm pieces. Put them into a cooking pot, add the sugar (optional) and 75% of the total water. Bring to a boil and cook on medium heat for 15–20 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the pumpkin is soft. Do not let it scorch on the walls and bottom.
3
Mash the pulp into a puree, transfer to the fermentation vessel and add the remaining 25% of raw water (it supplies oxygen and microelements yeast need for multiplication). Stir until homogeneous; if the wort is thick, dilute with more water — this does not affect quality. Fill the vessel to max 70%, leaving room for foam and CO₂.
4
Alternative to boiling: extract pure pumpkin juice and mix it with sugar syrup (1 kg sugar per 4 L water), keeping total sugar below 20%. However, heat treatment raises yield by a few percent and removes the vegetable taste via the Maillard reaction; with pure juice the finished spirit shows grassy shades.
5
Dilute the yeast per the package or activate by the universal method. Pitch the yeast or starter into the wort cooled to 30 °C. Fit any airlock and keep the vessel in a dark place (or covered) at 20–27 °C. Fermentation must start within the first 36 hours — bubbles from the airlock (glove inflates). If not, find and fix the cause urgently.
6
For the first 3–4 days check the pumpkin wash once every 8–10 hours so the pulp layer on the surface does not block the airlock. Open and stir as needed. Fermentation lasts 5–14 days (up to 45 on wine yeast or starter). It is finished when: gas stops (glove deflates); the wort partly lightens with a loose sediment layer; foam has disappeared; the wash tastes bitter without sweetness.
7
Rack the finished wash off the sediment. The remaining pulp can be pressed through cheesecloth. Strain the liquid part through a fine sieve or cheesecloth to remove solids that could scorch.

Distillation

8
Assemble the equipment as a simple pot still: column up to 65 cm with no packing, auxiliary units off, only a shell-and-tube or coil condenser and one bubble trap if the design has one. Factory columns: pot still mode.
9
Fill the boiler to 30–70% and run the first distillation at maximum speed: the distillate may run warm and cloudy but never steamy. Stop when the stream strength falls below 25%. Running "to water" is acceptable if the small gain justifies the extra energy and time.
10
Measure the raw spirit strength at 20 °C (or correct in a calculator) and calculate the pure alcohol: volume (L) × strength (%) ÷ 100. Dilute to 18–20%. Do not clean with activated charcoal or other means — keep the baked-pumpkin aroma and taste.
11
Second run: after boiling, reduce power so the spirit flows in small drops and stays cold. Collect the first 14–15% of the pure-alcohol amount separately as "heads" — a technical fraction unfit for drinking.
12
Raise power smoothly to fast drops or a thin stream, keeping the spirit cold, almost like the water in the apparatus.
13
Collect the "body" until the stream strength falls to 43%, then stop or collect "tails" separately for future rectification on a column.
14
Dilute the finished pumpkin moonshine with softened water to 40–45%, bottle in glass, seal tightly and rest for 4–5 days in a cool place. Shelf life is unlimited.
💡 To keep the pumpkin organoleptics, run the second distillation in pot-still mode and add a few sections of copper packing (SPN/RPN) to the column; stainless packing gives no result and only slows the run.
