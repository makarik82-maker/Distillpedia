🌾 Wheat Moonshine (Simple Recipe with Sugar)

A recipe for wheat moonshine without saccharification and enzymes, which significantly simplifies the technology of preparing wash, while the finished drink has pronounced grain aroma and taste. The disadvantage is lower yield, since starch in the grain will not be converted by yeast into ethyl alcohol. This method can be recommended to beginner distillers who are not yet ready to make classic malt wash (hot saccharification), or work with Koji yeast or enzymes (cold saccharification).

Category
Grain
ABV
40–45%
Difficulty
Medium
Fermentation
4–12 days
Yield
~5 kg wheat
⚠️ Legal & Safety Disclaimer: This recipe is provided for educational purposes only. Distilling alcohol at home is illegal in many countries without a licence. Always check your local laws. Read our Distillation Safety Guide before attempting any distillation.

Choosing the Right Wheat

Well-dried whole grains capable of germination are required — at least 2 months must have passed since harvest, but no more than 3 years. It is desirable that the wheat has a pleasant smell: without musty and stale tones.

Instead of wheat, you can take barley or rye without changing the method of preparing wash, but in terms of aroma and taste, wheat moonshine turns out the best, since this grain gives its tones well even without saccharification.

One batch of wheat can be used three times, adding only new sugar and water, and optionally yeast to speed up fermentation.

Ingredients

  • Wheat — 5 kg
  • Sugar — 5 kg
  • Water — 40 L (4 L per 1 kg wheat and sugar)
  • Yeast — 100 g dry or 500 g pressed baker's, or spirit yeast per instructions for 45 L of must

Proportions: for each kilogram of sugar and wheat add 4 L of water. The ratio between wheat and sugar can be changed, however it has been practically established that the best moonshine is obtained with equal proportions. For 1 kg of sugar you need 20 g dry or 100 g pressed baker's yeast. Spirit varieties should be added according to package instructions. Wheat is not considered in the yeast dosage.

🛑 Important: It is advisable to treat the grain with disinfecting solution before adding yeast to destroy pathogenic microorganisms on the surface. At home, it is easiest to use iodine or 6% hydrogen peroxide. Always discard the "heads" fraction during distillation — 12% in this recipe. See Distillation Safety Guide.

Step-by-Step Process

1
Wash the wheat. Pour wheat with water at room temperature, stir, leave for 5 minutes, then remove debris from the surface and drain the dirty water. Repeat the procedure 2-3 more times until the water is clear and without debris.
2
Disinfect. Make disinfecting solution: dissolve iodine (10 ml or 1 tablespoon of iodine per 20 L water). Pour solution over wheat 5-6 cm above, cover with lid and leave for 30 minutes, then drain the liquid and rinse the grains under running water. Disinfection is optional but highly recommended stage of wheat wash preparation, minimizing the risk of mould and souring of the must.
3
Sprout the wheat. Spread wheat in an even layer up to 2 cm thick on flat trays, add water at room temperature (should cover the grain by about half a centimeter). Leave wheat to germinate in a dark place at 18-25 °C. Stir the grain twice a day, adding water if necessary (wheat should not dry out). Finish germination when sprout length reaches about 2 cm and they start intertwining. Germination releases aromatic and flavor substances.
4
Disinfect fermentation vessel. Disinfect fermentation vessel with iodine solution (10 ml per 20 L water, 30 minutes).
5
Activate the yeast. Dilute yeast. Spirit: according to manufacturer's instructions. Dry baker's: sprinkle in an even layer over the surface of sugar syrup (10 ml water and 4 g sugar per 1 g yeast) at 24-30 °C, then cover with lid and leave in a dark place for 10-40 minutes until foam appears. Pressed yeast can be added directly from the refrigerator.
6
Add sugar and water. Add sugar and water from the wash recipe to the fermentation vessel. Stir until sugar is completely dissolved in the liquid.
7
Add yeast and wheat. Add yeast, stir again. Add wheat, trying not to separate sprouts. Fill the vessel maximum 75% of volume to leave enough space for foam and carbon dioxide.
8
Ferment. Install any design of airlock on the fermentation vessel. Transfer the wash to a dark place (cover with dense fabric) with stable temperature 18-28 °C. Depending on yeast and temperature, wheat wash according to this recipe ferments for 4-12 days. Signs of readiness for distillation: airlock stopped releasing gas (glove deflated); must became lighter; almost no foam on surface (individual small "islands" possible); no sweetness in wash taste (yeast converted all sugar).
9
Filter the wash. Drain the fermented wheat wash from sediment, strain through cheesecloth or sieve to remove grain residues that can burn during distillation. Optionally set up a new batch of wash on the wheat: add water and sugar in the same amount as the first time. You can skip yeast, but then fermentation duration may increase by a couple of days. It is permissible to store grain for up to 3 days in refrigerator or cellar, necessarily submerged in water and in hermetically sealed container.
10
First distillation. Pour wash into the still, filling 30-75% of volume. Distill the first time at maximum speed — moonshine may come out warm, but necessarily without steam. Finish distillation when strength in stream drops below 25% on regular apparatuses, and at 5% on columns over 50 cm high. If this seems advisable in terms of time and energy costs, you can distill low wines to almost zero strength.
11
Measure and calculate. Determine the amount of pure alcohol in first distillation moonshine (low wines): amount of distillate in liters multiplied by its strength in percentages (at 20 °C), then divide by 100. For calculations of all second distillation parameters, there is a moonshine calculator.
12
Dilute and second distillation. Dilute low wines with water to 18-20% and distill again. To preserve pronounced grain tones, it is advisable not to clean with charcoal or other methods. After boiling, adjust power — moonshine should come out in small drops and be cold. Collect 12% of the amount of pure alcohol in a separate container, this is "heads" — harmful fraction that cannot be drunk.
13
Collect the hearts. Increase power so moonshine comes out in a stream but remains cold. Collect "body" (main product) until strength in stream drops below 42%. Then finish distillation, or collect "tails" separately to add them to a new batch of wash before distillation, trying to extract remaining alcohol.
14
Dilute and rest. Dilute finished wheat moonshine ("body") with water to 40-45%, pour into glass bottles, seal tightly and leave for 5-7 days in a cool dark place to stabilize taste.
💡 Editor's tip: The main advantage of this recipe is simplicity — no saccharification or enzymes needed. However, the yield is lower than classic malt wash because starch in the grain is not fermented. The sprouted wheat adds grain aroma and flavor. You can reuse the same wheat up to 3 times, just adding fresh sugar and water.
