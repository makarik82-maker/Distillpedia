🌴 Date Moonshine

Light smoky notes with a fruity shade. Given the price of dates, distillers usually process expired or substandard fruit — even mouldy dates are acceptable because boiling kills the fungus; only rotten fruit (which adds bitterness) must be removed.

Category
Fruit
ABV
40–45%
Difficulty
Medium
Fermentation
4–55 days
Yield
up to 0.75 l/kg (40%)
⚠️ Educational purposes only. Home distillation is illegal in many countries without a licence. Check your local laws first.

Theory & Ingredients

Dates contain 50–66% sugar, so 1 kg of fruit yields up to 0.75 l of 40% spirit. Adding beet sugar (1 kg gives up to 1.2 l of 40%) increases the yield but dulls the date aroma — with enough raw material, run the wash without sugar.

On baker's or spirit yeast the wash is ready in 4–9 days, but these strains worsen the aroma; if time allows, use a homemade raisin/fruit starter or store-bought wine yeast — fermentation takes 30–50 days but the organoleptics improve noticeably.

The key technological feature: the fructose in the pulp must first be dissolved in water, so a "compote" is boiled, cooled, and only then pitched with yeast.

Ingredients

  • Dates — 3 kg
  • Water — 18 l, plus 4 l per 1 kg of sugar
  • Sugar (optional) — 0.5–2 kg
  • Yeast — baker's (20 g dry or 100 g pressed) or wine yeast for 20 l of wort
  • Citric acid — 10 g (sets the acidity that supports fermentation)

Wash Preparation

1
If using a starter, prepare a raisin or berry starter 3–5 days before working with the raw material.
2
Remove the pits from the dates, keeping as much pulp as possible. The process is labour-intensive but very desirable: pits noticeably worsen the spirit with an unpleasant aftertaste.
3
Put the pulp into a cooking pot, add the sugar (optional) and water at 4 l per 1 kg of raw material (counting the sugar). Stir.
4
Bring to a boil and simmer on low heat with the lid open for 25–30 minutes, stirring occasionally so the pulp does not stick to the bottom and scorch.
5
Take the pot off the heat. Add the remaining water (2 l per 1 kg of dates) and the citric acid. Stir.
6
When the wort cools to 25–28 °C, pitch the starter or the yeast diluted per the package instructions.
7
Pour the date wash into a fermentation vessel, fit an airlock (or a medical glove with a pinhole in one finger) and move it to a dark place at 18–28 °C.
8
Depending on yeast, sugar and temperature, fermentation lasts 4–55 days. It is finished when the airlock stops bubbling (the glove deflates), a sediment layer forms, and the wash tastes bitter without sweetness.

Distillation

9
Strain the finished wash through several layers of gauze and press the pomace well, otherwise pulp residues will scorch and spoil the distillate. Pour it into the still.
10
First run at maximum speed without fraction separation. Stop collection when the stream strength falls below 30%.
11
Measure the distillate strength and compute the pure alcohol: strength (%) × volume (l) ÷ 100.
12
Dilute to 18–20% and run the second distillation. Collect the first 10–15% of the pure-alcohol amount separately as "heads" — a harmful fraction for technical use only.
13
Collect the "body" until the stream strength falls below 45%. Then stop, or collect the "tails" separately.
14
Dilute the finished date moonshine to the desired strength (usually 40–45%), bottle in glass, seal tightly and rest for at least 2–3 days before tasting.
💡 With enough raw material, skip the sugar entirely: the date aroma stays much richer, and 50–66% natural sugar already gives an excellent yield.
