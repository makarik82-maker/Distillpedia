🧪 Fructose & Dextrose Moonshine (Clean Sugar Alternative)

Fructose or dextrose (glucose) moonshine has a clean organoleptic profile — no specific smell or harsh aftertaste typical of regular sugar moonshine. Even on simple stills, these distillates make an excellent base for tinctures and homemade gin, replacing rectified spirit. The trade-off: fructose and glucose cost 3–6 times more than sugar, and yield is about 5% lower.

Category
Sugar
ABV
40–45%
Difficulty
Beginner
Fermentation
2–7 days
Yield
~5 L (from 5 kg)
⚠️ Legal & Safety Disclaimer: This recipe is provided for educational purposes only. Distilling alcohol at home is illegal in many countries without a licence. Always check your local laws. Read our Distillation Safety Guide before attempting any distillation.

Why Fructose/Dextrose Instead of Sugar?

Yeast can only ferment four carbohydrates into ethanol: fructose, glucose (dextrose), sucrose, and maltose. When fermenting regular sugar (sucrose), yeast must first split it into fructose and glucose using the enzyme invertase. This process:

  • Changes the chemical composition of the wash
  • Consumes yeast energy and time, slowing fermentation
  • Produces kvas-like and fusel oil tones in the distillate
  • Can release toxic furfural during thermal processing

Fructose and dextrose skip this step entirely — yeast ferments them directly, resulting in a cleaner, smoother distillate.

Ingredients (for ~5 L of 40% moonshine)

  • 5 kg fructose or dextrose (glucose) — food-grade or technical-grade, minimum 99% pure, no additives
  • 20 L water — chlorine-free, medium hardness (3–7 °dH)
  • Citric acid (optional): 25 g (5 g per kg)
  • Yeast: spirit yeast or baker's yeast per manufacturer's instructions

Theoretical yield: 0.647 L pure alcohol per 1 kg fructose/dextrose (vs 0.681 L from sugar). Practical yield: ~1 L of 40% moonshine per kg.

🛑 Safety reminder: The "heads" fraction contains methanol and other harmful compounds. Always discard it — never consume. See Distillation Safety Guide.

Step-by-Step Process

1
Prepare the water. If using tap water, let it sit for 24–48 hours in an open container to allow chlorine to evaporate. Do not use the bottom 5–7 cm layer (sediment). Do not use boiled or distilled water without aeration.
2
Activate the yeast. For spirit yeast: follow package instructions. For dry baker's yeast: sprinkle evenly over syrup at 27–30 °C (10 ml water and 2 g sugar per 1 g yeast), wait 5 minutes for granules to swell, stir, and leave for 10–15 minutes in a dark place until foamy. For pressed baker's yeast: crumble directly from the fridge into the wash.
3
Dissolve the sugar. Mix all fructose/dextrose and citric acid with room-temperature water, or warm to 35–37 °C to speed up dissolution. If the syrup turns cloudy, it likely contains starch, dextrins, or other impurities. Boil the liquid, simmer for 5 minutes while skimming foam, then cool.
4
Transfer to fermentation vessel. Pour the syrup into the fermentation vessel, filling no more than 80% of volume. Leave headspace for foam and CO₂. If using regular spirit or baker's yeast, add nutrients: raisins, dried apricots, tomato paste, or black bread.
5
Pitch the yeast. When the wash temperature drops below 30 °C, add the activated yeast. Seal with an airlock. Move to a dark place with stable temperature 20–28 °C. Cover with dense fabric if needed.
6
Monitor for self-heating. For vessels over 50 L, fermentation raises temperature by 3–4 °C. For vessels over 200 L, monitor temperature constantly and use forced cooling if needed. Yeast dies or stops reproducing at 35 °C and above.
7
Confirm fermentation is complete. Dextrose/glucose wash ferments in up to 5 days (usually 48–72 hours); fructose in up to 7 days (usually 4–6 days). Signs: airlock bubbles fewer than 5 times per minute (or glove deflates), wash clarifies with sediment at bottom, smells like alcohol, tastes dry (not sweet) with slight bitterness.
8
First distillation. Siphon the wash off sediment into the still, filling 30–75% of volume. Assemble in simple pot-still mode (column height up to 65 cm, no packing, no dephlegmator). Distill at maximum speed. Stop when the ABV in the stream drops below 25%.
9
Measure and dilute. Measure the ABV at 20 °C and calculate the total pure alcohol (ABV% × volume in litres ÷ 100). Dilute with water to 18–20% ABV. Optional: clean with activated charcoal to partially remove unwanted impurities.
10
Second distillation with cuts. After boiling, reduce heat so the distillate drips slowly and stays cool. Collect the first 10–12% of pure alcohol volume as "heads" — discard this fraction, never consume it. Increase heat slightly and collect the "hearts" until the ABV in the stream drops below 43%.
11
Dilute and rest. Dilute the moonshine with softened water to 40–45% ABV. Bottle in glass, seal tightly, and rest for at least 3 days in a cool place to stabilize the flavor. Shelf life is unlimited.
💡 Editor's tip: Fructose/dextrose moonshine is the ideal base for homemade gin, tinctures, and liqueurs. Its clean profile doesn't compete with botanical flavors the way regular sugar moonshine does.
