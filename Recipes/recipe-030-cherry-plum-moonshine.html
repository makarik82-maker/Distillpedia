🍑 Cherry Plum Moonshine (with sugar, almond tone control)

Cherry plum moonshine has a pleasant berry aroma and soft taste with almond tones (if you leave the pits in the wash). Other organoleptic properties depend on the variety. The disadvantage is the low sugar content of the berries, which is why sugar has to be added.

Category
Fruit
ABV
40–45%
Difficulty
Medium
Fermentation
5–45 days
Yield
~10 kg cherries
⚠️ Legal & Safety Disclaimer: This recipe is provided for educational purposes only. Distilling alcohol at home is illegal in many countries without a licence. Always check your local laws. Read our Distillation Safety Guide before attempting any distillation.

Choosing the Right Cherry Plums

Ripe cherry plums of any color are suitable for preparing wash: yellow, red, dark, etc. You can use overripe, ground-collected, and crushed berries, as long as there is no rot or mould. Moonshine from yellow cherry plum has a subtle apricot aftertaste, while red and dark varieties give plum tones, sometimes with tart-spicy notes. It is not recommended to mix berries of different colors, as their organoleptic properties do not always combine well.

Incompletely ripe cherry plums contain a lot of pectin, which breaks down into methanol during fermentation. Although it is impossible to be poisoned by methanol from cherry plum moonshine even with a gross error in distillation technology, an increased pectin concentration in the wash is a sure way to a hangover.

Due to amygdalin, cherry plum pits bring tones of roasted almond nuts to the moonshine. To completely eliminate this aftertaste, you need to extract all the pits, or ferment the wash using the combined fermentation scheme. The first option is very labor-intensive and leads to significant loss of pulp, since in most varieties it separates poorly from the pits. In the second case, cherry plum moonshine loses some of the aroma and taste. Therefore, the pits are usually left, the main thing is not to damage their shell when crushing the berries. After distillation, amygdalin will not be able to convert into harmful hydrocyanic acid, since the enzymes required for this will be destroyed by high temperature and strong ethyl alcohol.

Ingredients

  • Cherry plums — 10 kg (weight with pits)
  • Sugar — up to 200 g per 1 kg of berries
  • Water — 1.5 L per 1 kg of berries, plus 4 L per 1 kg of sugar
  • Yeast (optional) — for 30 L of wash

Baker's yeast requires 100 g pressed or 20 g dry per 1 kg of total sugar (in cherry plums and added). Exceeding the recommended amount will not harm, but will not speed up fermentation either.

🛑 Important: Cherry plums contain only 5-7% sugar. In theory, 60-84 ml of 40-degree moonshine can be obtained from 1 kg of raw material, but in practice the losses of alcohol during double fractional distillation will be up to 20%. Therefore, the average yield is about 0.5 L of cherry plum moonshine (without sugar) per 10 kg of berries, which is very little given the high labor intensity of preparing the wash. Always discard the "heads" fraction during distillation — 15% in this recipe. See Distillation Safety Guide.

Step-by-Step Process

1
Prepare the cherry plums. Reject unripe, rotten, and mouldy cherry plums. If fermentation is on "wild" yeast, do not wash the berries; in other cases, rinse with running water. Pay special attention to washing ground-collected fruits so that no earthy aftertaste appears in the moonshine.
2
Crush the berries. Crush the berries by hand or by other means, leaving the pit shell intact, otherwise the almond aftertaste will "drown out" all other tones. Ideally, you need to extract the pits, but often they separate poorly from the pulp, which leads to large losses of raw material.
3
Transfer to fermentation vessel. Place the cherry plum mass in a fermentation vessel. Dilute the yeast (if used) according to the label instructions.
4
Add sugar and water. Dissolve sugar in water. Add the resulting syrup (or just water) to the cherry plums. Stir until homogeneous consistency. The wash should stir freely by hand; if necessary, add more water. Add yeast or starter (optional). Fill the vessel maximum 75% of volume, leaving enough free space for foam and carbon dioxide.
5
Seal and ferment. Close the wash with an airlock, leave in a dark room with temperature 18-28 °C. For the first 3-5 days, check the vessel every 8-12 hours so that the floating pulp (remains of pulp and skin) does not clog the airlock outlet tube. If necessary, open and stir the wash, destroying the dense layer of pulp. After a few days, most of the solid particles will sink to the bottom and stop interfering with the release of carbon dioxide.
6
Optional: filter to remove almond taste. If the almond aftertaste is undesirable, after 3 days strain the wash and squeeze the pulp dry, then put the already clean wash under an airlock to finish fermentation. However, the moonshine will lose a significant part of the fruit aroma and taste, and the yield will be smaller.
7
Confirm fermentation is complete. Depending on the chosen yeast, amount of sugar, and temperature, fermentation of cherry plum wash takes 5-45 days. The end of the process is determined by the following signs: carbon dioxide stopped being released from the airlock (less than 5 bubbles per minute) or the glove deflated; almost no foam remained; the wash became lighter, and a layer of loose sediment fell out at the bottom; the wash is not sweet to taste (the most important sign: yeast converted all the sugar).
8
Filter the wash. Remove the fermented wash from the sediment, then strain through cheesecloth or a kitchen sieve, removing the remains of pulp that can burn during distillation. It is desirable to clarify the wash with bentonite or another method.
9
Transfer to the still. Pour the wash into the distillation pot. Fill 30-75% of volume.
10
Assemble the equipment. Assemble the equipment for distillation in simple distiller mode: column height up to 60 cm and no packing inside, disable the dephlegmator, leave only the coil or shell-and-tube cooler, and optionally one thump keg to protect against splashing. In factory columns, the required mode is usually called pot still.
11
First distillation. Make the first distillation at maximum cooling capacity: the distillate can flow warm and cloudy, but there should be no steam. Finish the process when the ABV in the stream drops below 25%. You can also distill "to water" if a small increase in quantity seems justified considering the additional costs of time and energy.
12
Measure and calculate. Measure the ABV of the resulting low wines at 20 °C, or make a correction for the actual temperature in the moonshine calculator. Determine the concentration of pure (absolute) alcohol: multiply the ABV percentage by the volume of moonshine in liters, then divide by one hundred.
13
Dilute. Dilute the low wines with water to 18-20 degrees. Do not clean with activated charcoal or other means, preserving the aroma and taste of cherry plums.
14
Second distillation. Distill the second time. After boiling, reduce the heating power so that the distillate slowly drips and is necessarily cold.
15
Collect the heads. Collect separately the first 15% of output from the amount of absolute alcohol (calculated at step 12). This is the "heads" — the upper fraction with a high concentration of harmful impurities.
16
Collect the hearts. Gradually add power: the moonshine can flow in a thin stream or drip quickly, but should remain cold. Collect the middle drinking fraction ("body") until the ABV in the stream drops below 44%.
17
Finish or collect tails. Turn off the apparatus or collect the "tails" separately to rectify them on a column in the future.
18
Dilute and rest. Dilute the cherry plum moonshine ("body") with softened water to drinking strength — 40-45%, transfer to glass containers and seal tightly. Leave for at least 3 days in a dark cool place for stabilization. Shelf life is unlimited.
💡 Editor's tip: The main challenge with cherry plum moonshine is the very low sugar content of the berries (5-7%). This means you will get very little moonshine unless you add sugar. However, adding too much sugar will ruin the fruit character. The optimal balance is 100-150 g of sugar per 1 kg of berries. The almond tone from the pits is a matter of personal preference — some distillers love it, others find it intrusive.
