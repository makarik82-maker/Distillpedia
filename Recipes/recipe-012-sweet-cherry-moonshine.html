🍒 Sweet Cherry Moonshine (with and without sugar)

Sweet cherry moonshine has a subtle aroma and soft berry flavor with a slight bitterness. During wash preparation, it is important to handle the pits and sugar correctly, then properly separate fractions during distillation — otherwise the delicate organoleptic profile will be overwhelmed by fusel oils and almond bitterness.

Category
Fruit
ABV
40–45%
Difficulty
Medium
Fermentation
5–45 days
Yield
~10 kg cherries
⚠️ Legal & Safety Disclaimer: This recipe is provided for educational purposes only. Distilling alcohol at home is illegal in many countries without a licence. Always check your local laws. Read our Distillation Safety Guide before attempting any distillation.

Ingredients

  • 10 kg sweet cherries — weight with pits
  • Sugar (optional): up to 120 g per 1 kg cherries, recommended up to 85 g/kg
  • Water: 2 L per 5 kg cherries, plus 3 L per 1 kg sugar
  • Yeast: per manufacturer's instructions for total wash volume

Cherries contain 8–20% natural sugars (average 10–14%). Theoretical yield: 170–215 ml of 40% moonshine per 1 kg sweet cherries, 96–120 ml per 1 kg sour cherries.

🛑 Safety reminder: Cherry pits contain amygdalin, which gives a bitter almond taste. If pit integrity is violated during crushing, the moonshine will be bitter with pronounced almond tones. To guarantee removal of almond flavor, remove almost all pits from the wash. Always discard the "heads" fraction during distillation. See Distillation Safety Guide.

Step-by-Step Process

1
Sort the cherries. Remove rotten, mouldy, and unripe berries, as well as leaves and stems. If fermenting on wild yeast, do not wash the cherries.
2
Crush the cherries. Mash the berries by hand or by other means, without damaging the pits. Transfer the resulting mass to a plastic, glass, or enameled fermentation vessel. Critical: If pit integrity is violated, the moonshine will be bitter with pronounced almond tones. To guarantee removal of almond flavor, remove almost all pits from the wash.
3
Activate the yeast (if using). Prepare yeast in a separate container according to package instructions.
4
Prepare the wash. Add water and sugar (if using) to the cherry pulp. The wash should become liquid and easy to stir. If needed (depending on pulp juiciness), add more water than specified. Fill the vessel no more than 75% of volume to leave room for foam and CO₂.
5
Add yeast (optional). Pitch the activated yeast into the wash.
6
Seal and ferment. Fit an airlock and move to a dark place with temperature 18–28 °C. Signs of fermentation should appear within 8–20 hours: gas from the airlock and foam.
7
Monitor early fermentation. For the first 3–5 days, ensure floating pomace doesn't block the airlock. Open and stir if needed, pushing pulp back into the juice. Short contact with air is fine. Gradually the pomace will settle at the bottom.
8
Confirm fermentation is complete. Depending on yeast, sugar content, and temperature, cherry wash ferments for 5–45 days. Signs: no gas from airlock, foam disappears, wash clarifies with sediment at bottom, smells like alcohol, tastes dry (not sweet).
9
Filter the wash. Siphon the wash off sediment through a tube. Strain through cheesecloth or a fine sieve to remove small pulp and skin particles that could burn during heating.
10
First distillation. Assemble equipment in simple pot-still mode (minimal column height, no packing, one direct-flow cooler, optional thump keg). Fill the still 30–75% full. Distill at maximum speed. Stop when the ABV in the stream drops below 25%.
11
Measure and calculate. Measure the ABV at 20 °C and calculate the total pure alcohol (ABV% × volume in litres ÷ 100).
12
Reassemble for second distillation. Assemble equipment again in simple pot-still mode (column height up to 60 cm, one direct-flow cooler, no dephlegmator, optional thump keg disabled). Add one section of copper packing to the column for better removal of sulfur impurities. Stainless steel packing is useless and only slows the process. Important: Distilling cherry moonshine on fully assembled columns leaves almost no berry tones in aroma and flavor — not recommended.
13
Dilute and second distillation. Dilute the moonshine to 18–20% ABV. After boiling, reduce heat so the distillate drips slowly and stays cool.
14
Collect the heads. Collect the first 15% of pure alcohol volume as "heads" — discard this fraction, never consume it. This fraction contains many harmful impurities.
15
Collect the hearts. Increase heat slightly so the distillate drips quickly or flows in a thin stream, but stays cool. Collect the drinking fraction ("hearts") until the ABV in the stream drops below 45%. Then stop or collect "tails" separately.
16
Dilute and rest. Dilute the cherry moonshine with soft water to 40–45% ABV. Bottle in glass, seal tightly, and rest for at least 3 days in a cool place to stabilize the flavor. Shelf life in glass is unlimited.
💡 Editor's tip: Dark cherries give a rich aroma, while light cherries give a soft fruit flavor more similar to grape distillate. Different varieties can be mixed in one wash, but separating by color preserves the organoleptic profile better.
