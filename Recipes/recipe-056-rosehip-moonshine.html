🌹 Rosehip Moonshine

A berry-herbal aroma with a light "tea" shade and a soft taste with slightly astringent dried-fruit notes. Fresh, frozen and dried berries all work; the wash requires a short boil to release sugar and reduce acidity and tannins.

Category
Fruit
ABV
40–45%
Difficulty
Medium
Fermentation
5–45 days
Yield
12–96 ml/kg (40%)
⚠️ Educational purposes only. Home distillation is illegal in many countries without a licence. Check your local laws first.

Choosing the Ingredients

Rosehip berries

Any rosehip works: fresh, frozen or dried — as long as the fruit is ripe, since unripe berries contain more pectin, which yeast converts into methanol (the spirit stays safe but the hangover is heavier). Garden varieties (Jubileiny, Rubinovy, Titan, Krupnoplodny Michurina, Vorontsovsky-1) hold the most sugar (6–8%); wild rosehip 3–5%; ornamental berries only 1–3%.

Sugar, water, yeast

If fruit is scarce or low in sugar, add up to 300 g of sugar per 1 kg of berries (150–200 g is enough for dried berries and sweet varieties); beyond that the aroma and taste degrade noticeably. Fructose or dextrose are preferable (no kvass or molasses tones). Water: 3 l per 1 kg of berries plus 4 l per 1 kg of sugar, non-chlorinated.

Wild yeast on rosehip is weak and tolerates freezing poorly, and the recipe includes a thermal treatment that kills wild strains anyway. Cultured wine yeast (universal or for red grape juice) is optimal; a raisin or berry starter set 4–5 days ahead is a free alternative. Spirit yeast finishes in 5–8 days but the aroma is poorer; baker's yeast is not recommended.

Ingredients

  • Rosehip (fresh, frozen or dried) — 3 kg
  • Sugar — up to 300 g per 1 kg of berries (optimum 150–200 g)
  • Water — 3 l per 1 kg of berries, plus 4 l per 1 kg of sugar
  • Yeast or starter — per wash volume; baker's: 50 g pressed or 10 g dry per 1 kg of total sugar

Wash Preparation

1
Crush fresh rosehip with a wooden rolling pin, or cut dried berries in half. Do not mince them: destroyed seeds make the moonshine bitter.
2
Put the berries into a cooking pot, add the sugar and half of the recipe water. Stir until the sugar dissolves; if the wort is too thick, dilute with more water.
3
Bring to a boil and simmer for 15 minutes on low heat, stirring occasionally. Then switch off the heat, cover and leave for 3 hours. The thermal treatment releases all the sugar from the berries and reduces excess acidity and tannins; skipping it makes fermentation slow.
4
Activate the yeast per the package, or use the universal starter method.
5
Pour the cooled syrup into a fermentation vessel, add the second half of the water, stir, then pitch the yeast or starter. Fill to max 80% to leave room for foam and CO₂, and fit an airlock.
6
Move the vessel to a dark place at 18–28 °C. Fermentation must start within 48 hours: foam on the surface and gas from the airlock (the glove inflates). If not, find and fix the cause immediately.
7
For the first 3–4 days watch the vessel so the floating berry residue does not block the gas outlet; open and stir, submerging the cap. Once most berries sink, leave the wash alone.
8
Fermentation lasts 5–45 days depending on yeast, sugar and temperature. It is finished when: the airlock releases fewer than 5 bubbles per minute or the glove deflates; almost no foam remains; the wort lightens with a loose sediment layer; the wash smells of alcohol and is not sweet.
9
Decant the finished wash from the sediment (a thin tube works best) and strain through gauze or a fine sieve, removing all solid residue that could scorch during distillation.

Distillation

10
Assemble the equipment as a simple pot still: column up to 60 cm with no packing, dephlegmator and auxiliary units off, one direct-flow condenser (shell-and-tube or coil), one bubble trap if the design has one. Fill the still to 30–70%.
11
First run at maximum speed: the distillate may run warm and cloudy but never steamy. Stop when the stream strength falls below 25%; running almost "to water" is acceptable if the extra energy and time seem worth the small gain.
12
Measure the strength at 20 °C (or correct for temperature in a calculator) and compute the absolute alcohol: strength (%) × volume (l) ÷ 100. Dilute to 18–20%; do not clean with activated carbon or other methods, or the rosehip character disappears.
13
Second run: after boiling, adjust power so the spirit drips in small drops and stays cold as the cooling water. Collect the first 15% of the absolute-alcohol amount separately as "heads" — a harmful initial fraction not fit for drinking.
14
Raise the power to fast drops or a thin stream while keeping the spirit cold. Collect the "body" until the stream strength falls below 43%, then stop or collect "tails" separately for later rectification.
15
Dilute the finished rosehip moonshine with softened water to 40–45%, bottle in glass, seal tightly and rest for at least 5 days in a cool place. Shelf life is unlimited.
💡 To keep the berry-herbal profile, run the second distillation in pot-still mode and put a few plugs of copper packing (SPN/RPN) in the column; stainless packing gives no effect and only slows the run.
