🍈 Melon Moonshine (with and without sugar)

Melon moonshine has a barely perceptible fruit aroma and a soft taste with tones of ripe pulp and herbal honey in the aftertaste. However, the melon wash must be made and distilled correctly: a coarse mistake gives the moonshine a pronounced vegetable profile.

Category
Fruit
ABV
40–45%
Difficulty
Medium
Fermentation
4–50 days
Yield
~30 kg pulp
⚠️ Legal & Safety Disclaimer: This recipe is provided for educational purposes only. Distilling alcohol at home is illegal in many countries without a proper licence. Always check your local laws. Read our Distillation Safety Guide before attempting any distillation.

Choosing the Ingredients

Melons

Ripe and overripe melons of any condition are suitable: ordinary, cracked, irregular shape, too small, etc. — as long as there is no mold, rot or unripe flesh. You can arrange with market sellers to take unwanted leftovers at a good price. The best moonshine comes from muskat and honey varieties: "Amal", "Cantaloupe", "Kolkhoznitsa", "Malaga", "Iroquez", "Torpeda", "Efiopka", "Zolotistaya", etc. The more aromatic the fruit, the better. A musty smell indicates wrong or too long storage; unripe melons smell of freshly cut hay.

Cut melon pulp sours quickly and is prone to pathogenic infection, so fermentation must be started as fast as possible.

Yield: depending on ripeness and variety, melons contain 6–9% sugar (usually 8%). Therefore 1 kg of pulp gives 72–110 ml of 40° moonshine, but up to 20% of ethanol is lost during the two distillations. Making melon moonshine without sugar is worthwhile only if you have plenty of raw material — from 30 kg.

Sugar, Acid, Yeast

  • Sugar (optional): 1 kg of beet or cane sugar increases the yield by 1.2 L (40%) but worsens organoleptics, so balance quantity and quality. Empirically, up to 100 g of sugar per 1 kg of pulp is acceptable; beyond that kvass and molasses tones prevail over melon ones. Fructose or dextrose in the same proportion affects organoleptics less.
  • Citric acid (optional): melon contains few acids; low-acid wash ferments slower and is prone to souring and infection. Correcting wort acidity with citric acid is the simplest home solution and does not affect the finished drink.
  • Yeast: wild yeast cannot be used — the pulp contains none. Wine yeast gives no clear advantage here because about 90% of melon weight is water; if a 35–50 day fermentation is acceptable, cultured wine yeast for white juices or a raisin/berry starter made 4–5 days ahead can be used. The best fit is special fruit-wash yeast, which releases less harmful methanol thanks to the pectinase enzyme; ordinary spirit yeast is also fine. Dry or pressed baker's yeast worsens the fruit distillate with kvass and bread tones and is undesirable — usually added only when nothing else is at hand.

Ingredients

  • Melon — 30 kg of cleaned pulp
  • Sugar (optional) — up to 3 kg (1 kg per 10 kg of pulp)
  • Citric acid (optional) — 120 g (4 g per 1 kg of pulp)
  • Yeast or starter — for 30 L of wort

Baker's yeast dosage: 20 g dry or 100 g pressed per 1 kg of total sugar (in melons + added). Exceeding this proportion will not harm the wash or speed up fermentation.

Step-by-Step Process

1
Prepare the pulp. Cut thoroughly washed melons in half. Remove the seeds and the seed chamber — the loose central part of the fruit. Scrape the ripe pulp into a bucket or pot without splashing the juice. Green parts contain no sugar and add an unpleasant grassy taste to the moonshine.
2
Puree. Mince the melon pulp to a puree — with a blender, meat grinder, or simply by hand. Pour the whole mass into a wide-mouth fermentation vessel.
3
Activate yeast. Dilute the yeast per the package instructions, or use the universal activation method.
4
Make the wash. Add sugar and citric acid to the wort. Stir until fully dissolved. Fill the vessel to a maximum of 75%, leaving free space for foam and carbon dioxide.
5
Seal and ferment. Pitch the yeast or starter. Close the wash with an airlock and leave it in a dark place (or cover with dense fabric) at 18–28 °C. Fermentation must start within 36 hours: the airlock begins releasing gas and foam appears on the surface. If these signs are absent, find out why the wash is not fermenting and fix the cause urgently.
6
Monitor the cap. Control fermentation at least twice a day so that pulp floating to the surface does not accumulate in a dense layer blocking gas release from the airlock. Open and stir the melon wash if necessary. After a few days almost all pulp will settle at the bottom and stop floating.
7
Confirm completion. Depending on sugar content and temperature, melon wash on spirit or baker's yeast ferments 4–12 days; on wine yeast or starter — up to 50 days. Signs of a finished process: the airlock stops bubbling (fewer than 5 bubbles per 60 seconds); no foam on the surface (sometimes "islands" up to 3 cm remain); the wort has noticeably clarified with a layer of loose sediment at the bottom; the wash smells of alcohol and has no sweetness (yeast converted all sugar).

Distillation

8
Filter. Drain the fermented wash off the sediment through a tube, then strain through cheesecloth or a fine sieve, removing solid particles that could scorch.
9
Fill the still. Pour the wash into the boiler, filling 30–75% of volume.
10
Assemble equipment. Set up the equipment in simple distiller mode: column height up to 65 cm with no packing inside, dephlegmator and other units off. Only a shell-and-tube or coil condenser must remain in the system, plus one bubble trap if provided by design. In factory columns the required mode is called pot still.
11
First distillation. Distill the melon wash at maximum cooler power: the distillate may flow warm and cloudy, but necessarily without steam. Switch off the heat when the strength in the stream drops below 25°. Running "to water" is acceptable if the small gain in quantity justifies the extra energy, water and time costs.
12
Calculate. Determine the strength of the obtained distillate at 20 °C, or correct for another temperature in a moonshine calculator. Calculate the amount of absolute (pure) alcohol: volume in liters multiplied by strength in percent, divided by 100. Dilute the melon moonshine with water to 18–20%. Do not clean with activated charcoal or other methods, preserving the organoleptic properties.
13
Second distillation — heads. Make the second fractional distillation: after boiling, reduce the heating power so the moonshine drips slowly and is cold — approximately like the water in the apparatus. Collect separately 14–15% of liquid from the amount of absolute alcohol (calculated at step 12). This is the "head" — the initial fraction with a high concentration of harmful impurities.
14
Collect the hearts. Smoothly raise the power: the moonshine must drip fast or flow in a thin stream, but remain cold. Collect the "body" (middle drinking part) until the strength in the stream drops below 44°. After that switch off the apparatus, or accumulate "tails" separately for future rectification on a column.
15
Dilute and rest. Dilute the melon moonshine ("body") to drinking strength (40–45%) with softened water: bottled or filter-purified, but not hard tap water. More than half of the taste depends on water quality. Pour into glass containers, close tightly and leave for 3–4 days in a cool place to stabilize. Shelf life is unlimited.
💡 Editor's tip: To keep the melon organoleptics in the drink as much as possible, reassemble the column in pot still mode for the second run and follow this recipe. For the repeat distillation you can add one section of any copper packing to the column to increase the efficiency of removing sulfur compounds; stainless packing gives no result and only slows the distillation.
