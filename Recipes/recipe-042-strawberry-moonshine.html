🍓 Strawberry Moonshine (with and without sugar)

Proper strawberry moonshine has a soft berry taste without alcohol tones and a refined strawberry aroma. However, the wash preparation recipe has a number of features, without considering which the organoleptic properties of the drink will not be fully revealed or will be lost altogether.

Category
Fruit
ABV
40–45%
Difficulty
Medium
Fermentation
4–35 days
Yield
~5 kg strawberries
⚠️ Legal & Safety Disclaimer: This recipe is provided for educational purposes only. Distilling alcohol at home is illegal in many countries without a proper licence. Always check your local laws. Read our Distillation Safety Guide before attempting any distillation.

Choosing the Ingredients

Strawberries

Often substandard strawberries are processed into moonshine: bruised, too small, misshapen, slightly spoiled, unripe or overripe. It is important not to allow moldy and rotten raw material into the wort, especially when fermenting on wine yeast, because this increases the risk of souring. Dirty strawberries must be washed, otherwise the moonshine will have an earthy taste. From quality berries it is better to make strawberry wine — one of the most original homemade alcoholic drinks.

Ripe strawberries contain 3.5–4.9% sugar, so theoretically from 1 kg of berries you can get 40–58 ml of 40° strawberry moonshine. In practice 14–20% of alcohol is lost during fermentation and double fractional distillation. Considering this, only some amateur distillers can afford to make strawberry moonshine without sugar: it's good if from 10 kg of peeled berries about 0.5 L of 40° distillate "drips out" in the end.

Sugar

The low-yield problem can be solved by adding beet or cane sugar: 1 kg gives an additional 1.2 L (40%) of drink. However, it is important not to overdo it, since beet sugar worsens strawberry moonshine with kvass tones, and cane sugar with molasses notes. Fructose or dextrose is preferable to sugar because it has no aroma or taste. Experimentally established: 200–300 g of sugar (dextrose, fructose) per 1 kg of berries minimally affects organoleptics; 300–600 g acceptably worsens it; after 650 g/kg alcohol tones almost completely drown out the strawberry.

Yeast & Water

  • Yeast: desirable to set strawberry wash on "wild" (living on berry surface) or wine yeast: due to ester accumulation you get the most aromatic and soft moonshine. Drawback — long fermentation (up to 45 days). Wild yeast can only be counted on with home (country) strawberries not treated with chemicals. Store wine yeast (better for red juice) ferments stably and releases less foam. Spirit yeast finishes in 4–7 days but organoleptics will be poorer. Fruit-wash yeast is optimal for unripe raw material and when fermentation must end quickly (7–10 days max). The worst option — pressed or dry baker's yeast: kvass tones appear.
  • Water: strawberries are not juicy enough, and thick wort ferments poorly and is difficult to filter, so water must be added to strawberry wash. Any drinking water is suitable, but chlorinated water should be settled for 2–3 days. For diluting moonshine after the second distillation, soft water is needed — bottled or after reverse osmosis filter is optimal. The taste of moonshine depends on water quality by about 40%.

Ingredients

  • Strawberries — 5 kg
  • Sugar (fructose, dextrose) — 200–600 g per 1 kg of berries (optional)
  • Water — 2 L per 1 kg of berries, plus 3 L per 1 kg of sugar
  • Yeast — per package instructions depending on wash volume

Baker's yeast dosage: 10 g dry or 50 g pressed per 1 kg of sugar (sum of natural in berries + added). Exceeding the recommended amount will not worsen the moonshine but will not speed up fermentation either.

Step-by-Step Process

1
Sort. Sort the strawberries, not allowing rotten and moldy berries into the wort. Problem parts can be cut off.
2
Remove stems. Remove the stems ("tails") from the berries so that grassy tones do not appear in the moonshine.
3
Wash. Wash the strawberries, removing the smallest particles of sand and soil. If "wild" yeast is used, leave most berries unwashed — process only dirty ones and those picked near the ground.
4
Activate yeast. Dilute yeast according to manufacturer's instructions or by the universal method.
5
Crush and mix syrup. Mash the strawberries to a homogeneous mass. In a separate vessel mix water and sugar (fructose, dextrose).
6
Combine. Pour the strawberry mush into the fermentation vessel, add the sugar syrup. Mix until homogeneous. Fill the fermentation vessel to a maximum of 75% of volume, leaving room for foam and carbon dioxide.
7
Seal and ferment. Add yeast to the wort. Close the vessel with a classic airlock or a medical glove with a hole in one finger. Transfer to a dark place (or cover with fabric) at a stable temperature of 18–28 °C. Signs of fermentation should appear within 20 hours: foam and hissing.
🛑 Watch the Cap: For the first 3–5 days, carefully monitor the wort, not allowing the pomace ("cap" of pulp) to accumulate on the surface in a dense layer, impeding carbon dioxide release. If necessary, open and stir the wash once every 8–12 hours. Short air access will not harm.
8
Confirm completion. Depending on yeast, sugar content, and temperature, strawberry wash ferments for 4–35 days. Signs of process completion: no gas from the water lock (up to 3 bubbles per minute) or deflated glove; the wort became lighter than initial, and a layer of loose sediment accumulated at the bottom; no foam on the surface (small islands possible); the wash smells of alcohol and is not sweet (the most important sign — yeast converted all the sugar).

Distillation

9
Filter. Drain the fermented wash from the sediment (preferably through a tube like gasoline from a tank), then strain through cheesecloth or a kitchen sieve to get rid of the smallest pulp particles that could scorch, spoiling the moonshine with a bitter taste.
10
Assemble equipment. Assemble the apparatus in simple distiller mode: column height up to 50 cm without packing, direct-flow condenser (coil or shell-and-tube), dephlegmator and other units off, only one bubble trap or barboter can be left. In columns the suitable mode is called pot still.
11
First distillation. Pour strawberry wash into the distillation pot, filling 30–70% of volume. Make the first distillation at maximum speed: distillate may flow warm but without steam. End when the strength in the stream drops below 25%. If it seems advisable, you can run to almost zero strength.
12
Calculate. Determine the overall strength of the resulting moonshine at 20 °C. Calculate the amount of pure alcohol: volume in liters multiplied by strength in percent, divided by 100. Do not clean with activated charcoal or other methods, otherwise the aroma will disappear.
13
Reassemble. Reassemble the apparatus in simple distiller mode (pot still), but this time you can add one section of copper packing to the column for effective removal of sulfur compounds; stainless packing will not help and will only slow the distillation speed.
14
Dilute. Dilute the raw spirit with water to 18–20%.
15
Second distillation — heads. Make the second distillation: after boiling, adjust the heating power so that moonshine flows in small drops and is cold. Collect the first 12–15% of output from the amount of pure alcohol (determined at step 12) separately. This harmful fraction is called the "head"; it contains a high concentration of harmful impurities, so it is not suitable for drinking.
16
Collect the hearts. Smoothly raise the apparatus power so that moonshine flows fast in drops or a thin stream but remains cold. Collect the "body" (drinkable fraction) until the strength in the stream drops below 45%, then end the distillation or collect "tails" separately to process them in the future.
17
Dilute and rest. Dilute the finished strawberry moonshine ("body") with water to drinking strength — 40–45% vol., pour into glass bottles or jars, close tightly, and leave for at least 3 days (better 5–7 days) in a cool place to stabilize the taste. Shelf life is unlimited.
💡 Editor's tip: Standard distillation on a brewing or rectification column impoverishes the organoleptic properties of strawberry moonshine, so classic fractional distillation by the method specified in this recipe is desirable.
