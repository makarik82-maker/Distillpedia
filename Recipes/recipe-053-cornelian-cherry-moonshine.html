🍒 Cornelian Cherry Moonshine (with and without sugar)

Cornelian cherry moonshine has a recognizable organoleptic profile — with shades of cherry, red plum and pomegranate. Individual notes depend on the condition of the fruit: fresh ones give a barely perceptible astringency, frozen ones emphasize the berry profile but give almost no aroma, and dried ones add dried-fruit tones. Preparing cornelian cherry wash has several features, discussed below.

40–45%

Medium

5–50 days

~5 kg berries
⚠️ Legal & Safety Disclaimer: This recipe is provided for educational purposes only. Distilling alcohol at home is illegal in many countries without a proper licence. Always check your local laws. Read our Distillation Safety Guide before attempting any distillation.

Choosing the Ingredients

Cornelian Cherry

Any ripe cornelian cherry is required. Unripe berries have a high pectin concentration, from which yeast produces methanol. Although poisoning with methanol from cornelian cherry moonshine is impossible even theoretically, the hangover will be stronger. Large and juicy berries can be used for homemade cornelian cherry wine, and if the drink does not appeal, distill it by the method specified here.

Cornelian cherry seeds add a pronounced roasted-almond taste that overwhelms the berry profile; although this is a very labor-intensive process, it is desirable to remove the seeds.

Yield: depending on ripeness and variety, cornelian cherry berries contain 7–9% sugar. This means that from 1 kg of raw material you can theoretically get 84–100 ml of 40° moonshine, but in practice ethyl alcohol losses during distillation will be up to 20%. Making cornelian cherry moonshine without sugar is advisable when there are many fruits (from 10 kg) and they are sweet.

Sugar, Water, Yeast

  • Sugar: 1 kg of sugar additionally yields 1.2 L of 40° moonshine, but sugar worsens berry distillates with molasses and kvass tones, so it is important to find a balance between quality and quantity of the drink. Empirically, up to 200 g of sugar per 1 kg of cornelian cherry is acceptable for fresh or frozen berries, or up to 150 g for dried ones. To reduce the negative impact on organoleptic properties, sugar can be replaced in the same amount with fructose or dextrose (powdered glucose).
  • Water: any drinking water is suitable for the wash, preferably non-chlorinated, since chlorine slows fermentation. Tap water is first stood for three days in an open bucket or pot, partially evaporating the chlorine. The finished cornelian cherry moonshine (after the second fractional distillation) must be diluted with softened water — with a minimum concentration of heavy metal salts. For example, bottled, purified by reverse-osmosis filters, or from street machines adjusted to maximum softening. Boiled water retains no oxygen or microelements — the worst option for both wash and dilution.
  • Yeast (optional): if the fruits were collected at least a day after rain, not washed and not frozen for a long time, you can try to make cornelian cherry wash without yeast — starting fermentation with "wild" strains. However, "wild" yeast on cornelian cherry is weak — it starts slowly, ferments slowly and releases much foam, so "cultured" wine yeast for red juice or universal strains is optimal. A free alternative is a berry or raisin starter set 4–5 days before processing the raw material. Cornelian cherry wash on spirit yeast will be ready for distillation in 5–8 days, whereas "cultured" or "wild" wine yeast may need up to 50 days, but aroma and taste worsen, since only wine yeast releases complex esters that noticeably round the organoleptics. Special fruit-wash yeast is advisable if there is much unripe cornelian cherry, since such yeast partially destroys pectin thanks to enzymes, but otherwise it is ordinary spirit yeast. Dry or pressed baker's yeast suits cornelian cherry wash least of all, as it adds inappropriate bread tones and pronounced alcohol sharpness. These types are used when nothing else is at hand and the berries must be processed urgently.

Ingredients

  • Cornelian cherry berries — 5 kg
  • Sugar — up to 200 g per 1 kg of berries
  • Water — 3 L per 1 kg of cornelian cherry, plus 4 L per 1 kg of sugar
  • Yeast or starter — per instructions for 20 L of wort

For 1 kg of dried or cured berries, add 4 L of water and up to 150 g of sugar; otherwise the recipe does not change. Baker's yeast: 20 g dry or 100 g pressed per 1 kg of total sugar (in fruits + added). Calculations can be approximate, since exceeding the dosage will not harm the wash but will not speed up fermentation.

Step-by-Step Process

1
Sort the berries. Sort the cornelian cherry: remove unripe, very small, rotten and moldy berries. For fermentation on "wild" yeast do not wash the fruits; in other cases rinse with running water, especially thoroughly washing fallen berries (collected from the ground).
2
Remove seeds. Cut each berry in half, then remove the seeds so that almond tones do not appear in the finished moonshine. If it is decided to leave the seeds, crush each fruit with a wooden rolling pin without damaging the kernels.
3
Make the wash. Place the cornelian cherry in the fermentation vessel. Separately dissolve sugar in water, pour the resulting syrup (or only water) onto the berries. Stir to a homogeneous consistency. Fill the vessel to a maximum of 75% of volume, leaving space for foam and carbon dioxide.
4
Activate yeast. Activate yeast per the package instructions (if used), or by the universal starting method. Add yeast or starter to the wort (optional).
5
Seal and ferment. Install any airlock on the wash. Leave the vessel in a dark place (or covered) at a stable 18–28 °C. Fermentation must start within 48 hours: foam appears and the airlock releases gas (glove inflates). If this did not happen, figure out why the sweet wash is not fermenting and urgently eliminate the cause.
6
Monitor the cap. Watch the wort for the first 2–3 days so that floated berry residue does not accumulate at the surface in a dense layer, preventing free release of carbon dioxide from the vessel. Open and stir the wash as needed. When most fruits settle at the bottom, leave it alone.
7
Confirm completion. Depending on the amount of sugar, yeast used and temperature, cornelian cherry wash ferments 5–50 days. The process is finished when: fewer than five bubbles per minute come out of the airlock or the glove deflates; almost no foam remains at the surface (separate areas up to 3 cm in diameter may remain); a loose sediment has accumulated at the bottom and the wort has at least partially lightened; the wash is not sweet (yeast converted all the sugar) and smells of alcohol.

Distillation

8
Filter. Drain the fermented cornelian cherry wash off the sediment through a tube, then strain through cheesecloth or a fine sieve, guaranteed removing berry residue that could scorch, spoiling the moonshine with bitterness.
9
Assemble equipment. Assemble the equipment in simple distiller mode: column height up to 60 cm with no packing, one direct-flow cooler (coil or shell-and-tube), dephlegmator and other units off, leave only one bubble trap if provided by the design. In factory products the required mode is usually called pot still.
10
Fill the still. Pour the filtered wash into the distillation pot. Fill 30–70% of volume.
11
First distillation. Distill the cornelian cherry wash at maximum speed: the distillate may flow warm and cloudy, but necessarily without steam. End the process when the strength in the stream drops below 20 degrees. If desired, you can run "to water" if a slight increase in quantity seems justified by the additional energy, water and time costs.
12
Calculate. Measure the strength of the obtained raw spirit at 20 °C, or correct the reading in a moonshine calculator. Calculate the amount of absolute (anhydrous) alcohol: volume in liters multiplied by strength in percent, then divided by 100.
13
Dilute. Dilute the raw spirit with water to 18–20%. Do not clean with wood activated charcoal or other methods, preserving the organoleptics of cornelian cherry berries.
14
Second distillation — heads. As soon as the liquid boils, reduce the heating power so the distillate flows in small drops and is cold, almost like the water at the apparatus inlet. Collect separately 14% of "heads" from the amount of absolute alcohol (result of the calculation at step 12). This top fraction contains many harmful impurities, so it is not suitable for drinking.
15
Collect the hearts. Raise the heating power. The moonshine must flow in a thin stream or drip fast, but remain cold. Collect the "body" (drinking part) until the strength in the stream drops to 43%. Then switch off the apparatus or accumulate "tails" separately for future rectification on a column.
16
Dilute and rest. Dilute the cornelian cherry moonshine with softened water to 40–45% strength. Pour into glass vessels, close tightly and leave for at least 3 days in a cool place to stabilize. Shelf life is unlimited.
💡 Editor's tip: To preserve the cornelian cherry organoleptics in the moonshine, it is better to reassemble the column in pot still mode and follow the recipe. Several plugs of any copper packing can be placed in the column this time for better removal of sulfur compounds; stainless packing is useless for these purposes.
