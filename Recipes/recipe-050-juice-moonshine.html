🧃 Store-Bought Juice Moonshine (Packaged or Concentrated)

It is worth distilling store-bought juice if the raw material was obtained free or at a significant discount. Predicting the quality of the drink in advance is difficult, since everything depends on the juice composition. Packaged juices are concentrate diluted with water, with stabilizers and preservatives added for long storage; preservatives inhibit fermentation, so their effect must be neutralized with high temperature.

Category
Fruit
ABV
40–45%
Difficulty
Easy
Fermentation
4–50 days
Yield
~20 L juice
⚠️ Legal & Safety Disclaimer: This recipe is provided for educational purposes only. Distilling alcohol at home is illegal in many countries without a proper licence. Always check your local laws. Read our Distillation Safety Guide before attempting any distillation.

Theory

It is the presence of additives that influences the aroma and taste of the moonshine. Every manufacturer has its own composition, so the organoleptic properties of store-bought juice moonshine cannot be predicted in advance — sometimes unpleasant sharp shades appear. From the juice you have on hand, first make a small test batch. However, even from the most natural juice the aroma will not be the same as from fresh fruit: part of the aromatic substances sits in the skin, and modern juice-production methods almost completely exclude contact between juice and skin.

Store-bought and concentrated juices are interesting to distillers because they contain comparatively much sugar. Approximate sugar content of packaged juices:

  • grape — 14–15%;
  • apple, pear, peach — 12%;
  • orange — 8–10%;
  • cherry, pomegranate — 7–8%;
  • carrot — 4–5%;
  • tomato — 3–4%.

A more or less accurate way to learn the sugar content of a particular brand is to read the label: the "carbohydrates" value indicates the juice sugar content. Important: a juice may taste very sweet but contain little sugar, because sweeteners are used instead — yeast cannot convert them into alcohol.

From 1 kg of sugar you can get up to 1.2 L of 40° moonshine. If you have grape, apple, peach or pear juice, additional sugar need not be added. For cherry, pomegranate, carrot and tomato juice I recommend raising the sugar content with beet sugar — this increases yield and supports fermentation.

In any case, the sugar content of the wort before fermentation must not exceed 19–20%. This means that if the initial sugar content of the juice is 11% (carbohydrates — 11 g), a maximum of 90 g of sugar can be added per 1 liter of such juice. Concentrates are handled the opposite way — diluted with water to reduce sugar content. For example, if a concentrate contains 60% sugar, dilute it 1:3 to bring the sugar content down to 20%.

Juice wash can be fermented with any yeast: baker's, spirit or wine. Spirit and baker's yeast give a fast result in 4–7 days, but the moonshine has characteristic strong alcohol notes in the aroma. Wine yeast lacks this drawback but ferments for a long time — 20–50 days.

Recipe A: Store-Bought Juice Wash

  • Juice — 20 L
  • Sugar — up to 120 g per 1 L of juice
  • Yeast — 40 g dry, 200 g pressed baker's, or spirit/wine for 25 L of wort
  1. Pour the juice into a pot, bring to a boil, add sugar, stir and simmer on low heat for 3–5 minutes, skimming foam. Then turn off the heat, cover the pot with a lid and cool the wort to a temperature comfortable for pitching yeast — 26–28 °C. Boiling breaks down part of the preservatives in the juice that inhibit fermentation.
  2. Transfer the wort to a fermentation vessel, pitch yeast per the package instructions, and install any airlock design on the vessel neck.
  3. Leave the juice wash to ferment in a dark room (or covered with dense fabric) at a stable 20–28 °C. A finished wash becomes bitter to taste, stops releasing gas from the airlock, and a sediment layer appears at the bottom. Fermentation duration depends on the chosen yeast and temperature.

Recipe B: Concentrated Juice Wash

  • Juice concentrate — 5 L
  • Water — 15–20 L
  • Yeast — 40 g dry or 200 g pressed; wine and spirit for 25–30 L of wort
  1. Dilute the juice concentrate with water to a sugar content below 20%. Bring to a boil, turn off the heat and cover with a lid. Leave to cool to 26–28 °C. The high temperature destroys part of the preservatives that inhibit fermentation.
  2. Transfer the wort to a fermentation vessel. Add yeast. Install an airlock or a medical glove with a hole in one finger.
  3. Move the concentrate-juice wash to a dark place (cover with dense fabric) at 20–28 °C and leave until fermentation ends: the wash becomes bitter without sweetness, the airlock stops releasing gas (glove deflates), and sediment appears at the bottom.

Distillation

1
Fill the still. Transfer the fermented wash into the distillation pot without touching the sediment at the bottom.
2
First distillation. Distill at maximum speed; collect the first 50–80 ml of distillate separately and discard it. Collect the product until the strength in the stream drops below 18% ABV.
3
Calculate. Determine the strength of the first-distillation moonshine. Then calculate the amount of pure alcohol: strength in percent multiplied by volume in liters, divided by 100.
4
Second distillation — heads. Dilute the drink with water to 18–20% and run the second distillation. Collect the first 15% of output from the amount of pure alcohol separately and discard it (or use for technical needs). This harmful fraction is called the "head" and is dangerous to drink.
5
Collect the hearts. End collection of the main fraction ("body") when the strength in the stream drops below 45%. Further, "tails" can be collected in a separate vessel for adding to future wash batches before distillation, or the distillation can be ended immediately.
6
Dilute and rest. Dilute the drinking part of the juice moonshine ("body") to 40–45% ABV, pour into glass bottles or jars, close tightly and leave for 2–3 days in a dark cool place to stabilize the taste.
💡 Editor's tip: Always make a small test batch first — the additives in each manufacturer's juice make the final organoleptics unpredictable. Juices with sweeteners (aspartame, sucralose, etc.) will not ferment at all.
