🍎 Apple Moonshine (with and without sugar, juice or pulp)

Apple moonshine can be made using different methods: fermenting with pulp or pure juice only, on wild, wine, or spirit yeast, with or without sugar. This universal recipe covers all variations and their impact on the final result, with a distillation technique that preserves the apple organoleptic profile. The base for homemade Calvados.

Category
Fruit
ABV
40–45%
Difficulty
Medium
Fermentation
4–50 days
Yield
~30 kg apples
⚠️ Legal & Safety Disclaimer: This recipe is provided for educational purposes only. Distilling alcohol at home is illegal in many countries without a licence. Always check your local laws. Read our Distillation Safety Guide before attempting any distillation.

Ingredients

  • 30 kg apples — will yield approximately 18–20 L of juice
  • Sugar (optional): up to 3 kg (1 kg per 10 kg apples or 6 L juice)
  • Water: 0.35 L per 1 kg apples, plus 3 L per 1 kg sugar (do not dilute pure juice)
  • Yeast (optional): per manufacturer's instructions for total wash volume

Apples contain 6–20% sugar (average 12%). Theoretical yield: 72–240 ml of 40% moonshine per 1 kg apples, minus 15–20% lost during double distillation.

🛑 Safety reminder: Unripe apples contain high pectin levels, which yeast converts to methanol. Use ripe fruit and always discard the "heads" fraction during distillation. See Distillation Safety Guide.

Red vs. White Fermentation Scheme

Red scheme (with pulp and skin): Simpler to implement, yields 5–10% more moonshine with richer organoleptic profile. However, souring risk is higher, fermentation takes 1–2 days longer, and methanol concentration increases. Harmful substances are removed by double fractional distillation.

White scheme (pure juice only): Cleaner fermentation, lower souring risk, but less intense apple flavor and lower yield.

The red scheme is generally preferred for apple moonshine, but the choice is yours.

Step-by-Step Process

1
Sort the apples. Remove rotten and mouldy fruit. Cut away darkened flesh from otherwise intact apples. If fermenting on wild yeast, do not wash the apples (wipe dirty ones with a dry cloth). If using starter or wine yeast, wash thoroughly under running water.
2
Remove cores and seeds. This is critical — otherwise the moonshine will be noticeably bitter.
3
Crush or press. Grind the apples into a homogeneous puree or press for juice. Transfer the puree (or pure juice) to a plastic fermentation vessel. A construction mixer or drill with a paddle attachment works well for crushing apple slices if you don't have a crusher or screw press.
4
Prepare the wash. Mix water and sugar separately. Add the syrup (or water only) to the fermentation vessel. Do not dilute pure juice wash without sugar. If the pulp wash remains thick, add more water than specified — this won't affect quality. Fill the vessel no more than 75% of volume.
5
Add yeast (if using). Activate yeast according to manufacturer's instructions and add to the wash.
6
Seal and ferment. Fit an airlock and move to a dark place with temperature 18–28 °C. Signs of fermentation should appear within 8–36 hours: foam, hissing, and gas from the airlock.
7
Monitor early fermentation (pulp wash only). For the first 2–3 days, ensure floating pomace doesn't block the airlock. Open and stir if needed, pushing pulp back into the liquid. After a few days, all pomace will settle at the bottom.
8
Confirm fermentation is complete. Depending on yeast and temperature, apple wash ferments for 4–50 days (longest on wild/wine yeast). Signs: no gas from airlock, foam disappears, wash clarifies with sediment at bottom, tastes dry (not sweet) with slight bitterness and alcohol aroma.
9
Filter the wash. Siphon the wash off sediment through a tube. Filter through a fine sieve or several layers of cheesecloth to remove pulp and skin residue that could burn during heating. Filtering pure juice wash is optional.
10
First distillation. Assemble equipment in simple pot-still mode (minimal column height, no packing, no dephlegmator, one direct-flow cooler, optional thump keg). Fill the still 30–75% full. Distill at maximum power. Stop when the ABV in the stream drops below 25%.
11
Measure and dilute. Measure the ABV at 20 °C and calculate the total pure alcohol. Dilute with water to 18–20% ABV. Do not clean with activated charcoal or other methods — this strips the apple aroma and flavor.
12
Second distillation with cuts. After boiling, reduce heat so the distillate drips slowly and stays cool. Collect the first 14% of pure alcohol volume as "heads" — discard this fraction, never consume it. Increase heat slightly and collect the "hearts" until the ABV in the stream drops below 45%.
13
Dilute and rest. Dilute the apple moonshine with softened water to 40–45% ABV. Bottle in glass, seal tightly, and rest for at least 5 days in a cool, dark place to stabilize the flavor. Shelf life is unlimited.
💡 Editor's tip: For homemade Calvados, use firm green apple varieties and age the distillate in oak barrels or infuse on oak chips. The result rivals commercial Calvados.
