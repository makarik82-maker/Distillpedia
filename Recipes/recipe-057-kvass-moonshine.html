🍺 Kvass Moonshine

Expired kvass or kvass wort concentrate yields a spirit with light bread notes in taste and aroma. Much depends on raw-material quality: the cheapest flavouring-only kvass without malt is a bad idea, while kvass wort concentrate almost always works. In essence kvass here is not a standalone raw material but a simple way to improve a sugar wash.

Category
Beer / Grain
ABV
40–45%
Difficulty
Easy
Fermentation
4–10 days
Yield
~1.2 l per 1 kg sugar (40%)
⚠️ Educational purposes only. Home distillation is illegal in many countries without a licence. Check your local laws first.

Yeast Choice

For both recipes below, whiskey yeast or spirit yeast is best; if unavailable, ordinary baker's yeast (dry or pressed) works. Wine yeast is not recommended: it combines poorly with malt, and preservatives in the kvass may stop its fermentation completely.

Recipe A: Wash from Expired Kvass

Kvass always contains less than 1.2% ABV (Russian GOST requirement), usually up to 0.5%, so distilling kvass alone is pointless — the yield would be minimal. But expired kvass can replace water in a sugar wash to improve the aroma and taste of the spirit. Sugar must be added even if the kvass tastes very sweet, because producers use synthetic sweeteners that yeast cannot ferment; the exception is kvass with natural sugar (a rarity) — then add sugar so the wash sugar stays at or below 22%. Orient on 1 kg of added sugar → up to 1.2 L of 40% spirit.

  • Kvass — 12 l
  • Sugar — 3 kg
  • Yeast — 300 g pressed, 60 g dry, or whiskey yeast per 15 l of must
1
Pour the kvass into a cooking pot or bucket. Let it stand for 1 hour, stirring every 15 minutes, so the gas escapes.
2
Bring to a boil over medium heat, add all the sugar, stir, bring to a boil again, then remove from heat and cool below 30 °C. Boiling destroys the preservatives that inhibit fermentation.
3
Pour the kvass into the fermentation vessel (leave at least 25% headspace for foam and CO₂), add the yeast, fit any airlock and move to a dark place at 20–28 °C. Never pitch yeast above 30 °C or they may die.
4
Leave until fermentation ends (usually 4–10 days): the airlock stops releasing gas (glove deflates). The finished kvass wash tastes bitter.

Recipe B: Wash from Kvass Wort Concentrate

Moonshine from kvass concentrate is more aromatic than from expired kvass, but the sugar amount is harder to calculate because the wort itself may contain sugar. In any case do not add more than 3 kg (per the proportions below) so the density does not get too high. Optimal wash sugar before fermentation: 20–22%.

  • Kvass wort concentrate — amount for 25 l of kvass
  • Water — 20 l
  • Sugar — 1–3 kg
  • Yeast — 500 g pressed, 100 g dry, or whiskey yeast per 25 l of must
1
Pour the water into the fermentation vessel. Add the kvass wort concentrate and stir until the liquid is homogeneous.
2
Add the sugar and stir again until fully dissolved. Fill the vessel to max 75% of volume, leaving room for foam and CO₂.
3
Add the yeast, fit a water lock (or glove) and leave in a dark place at 20–28 °C until fermentation ends (usually 4–10 days). The finished wort wash is not sweet, with a light bitterness. Check after the airlock stops bubbling or the glove deflates.

Distillation (both recipes)

4
Rack the finished wash off the sediment, which could scorch during heating and make the spirit bitter.
5
First run at maximum speed without fraction separation. Stop when the stream strength falls below 15–20%. The spirit may be cloudy — this is normal.
6
Measure the raw spirit strength and compute the pure alcohol: strength % × volume in litres ÷ 100. Dilute with water to 18–20%.
7
Second run: collect the first 10% of the pure-alcohol amount separately as "heads" — a harmful fraction that must not be drunk.
8
Collect the main fraction ("body") until the stream strength falls below 40–42%. Then stop, or collect "tails" separately to add to the next wash batch before distillation.
9
Dilute the kvass moonshine ("body") with water to 40–45% ABV, bottle in glass, seal tightly and rest for 2–3 days in a cool place for taste improvement.
💡 Kvass is essentially a flavour improver for a sugar wash here. For a richer bread profile use kvass wort concentrate; for a quick, simple run use expired kvass as the water base.
