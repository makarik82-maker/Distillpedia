🍬 Candy Moonshine (Expired Chocolate, Caramel, with Filling)

The organoleptic properties of candy moonshine depend on the chemical additives in the composition (the fewer, the better), so the result is difficult to predict. Often you get a distillate at the level of simple sugar moonshine — a good base for tinctures and liqueurs. The technology for preparing candy wash has several important nuances.

Category
Sugar
ABV
40–45%
Difficulty
Medium
Fermentation
4–12 days
Yield
~3 kg candies
⚠️ Legal & Safety Disclaimer: This recipe is provided for educational purposes only. Distilling alcohol at home is illegal in many countries without a licence. Always check your local laws. Read our Distillation Safety Guide before attempting any distillation.

Candy Sugar Content

  • Hard candy and lollipops: 90–95%
  • Toffee and soft caramel: 70–80%
  • Chewing marmalade: 50–70%
  • Chocolate (including milk chocolate): 45–60%
  • Dark chocolate: 15–25%

Also check for "hidden" sugars: molasses, invert and glucose syrups, fructose, dextrose, maltodextrin, fruit juice concentrate. All are processed by yeast.

Ingredients

  • 3 kg candies — one type or assorted (chocolate, caramel, with filling)
  • Water: 15–18 L (5–6 L per 1 kg)
  • Citric acid: 5 g per 1 L wash
  • Yeast: depending on volume

Spirit yeast per package instructions. Baker's yeast: 20 g dry or 100 g pressed per 1 kg total sugar in candies.

🛑 Important: Synthetic sweeteners are NOT fermentable: aspartame (E951), acesulfame K (E950), sucralose (E955), saccharin (E954), steviol glycosides, erythritol. Very sweet candies do not necessarily yield more moonshine. Always discard the "heads" fraction during distillation. See Distillation Safety Guide.

Step-by-Step Process

1
Prepare the candies. Remove wrappers. Sort by type if needed (caramel, chocolate, with filling separately). Grind soft candies through a meat grinder or crush by hand. Leave hard ones whole.
2
Boil water. Bring water to a boil at a rate of 4 L per 1 kg candies. Each type needs a separate pot, or sequential boiling in one pot.
3
Add candies to boiling water. Reduce heat to minimum. Add candies in several stages, stirring constantly to avoid burning.
4
Cook the wash. Simmer on medium heat, stirring periodically (especially near the bottom), until homogeneous. Chocolate candies usually need 10–25 minutes, caramel about an hour. If the consistency becomes thick and/or sticky, add water. Warning: Do not mix candies of different density in one pot — soft ones will burn before hard ones dissolve.
5
Cool and transfer. Turn off heat, cover, and wait until cooled to 35–40 °C. Transfer to fermentation vessel (different types can be combined now). Add unboiled cold water (1 L per 4 L candy wash) and citric acid. Stir until homogeneous. Fill the vessel no more than 70% of volume — candy wash can foam heavily.
6
Activate the yeast. Spirit yeast per package instructions. Pressed baker's yeast can be used directly from the fridge, crumbled with clean hands. Dry baker's yeast: sprinkle on the surface of sugar syrup (1 g sugar and 10 ml water per 1 g yeast) at 28–30 °C, wait 5 minutes, stir, and leave for 10–15 minutes until foamy.
7
Pitch the yeast. Add activated yeast to the wash. Temperature must be below 30 °C. Seal with an airlock.
8
Ferment. Move to a dark place (or cover with dense fabric) with stable temperature 18–27 °C. Check every 8–10 hours, extinguishing foam with crackers or cookies if needed. Important: An oily film may form on the surface from palm or other vegetable oil added to candies. Remove this film periodically, especially before distillation.
9
Confirm fermentation is complete. This takes 4–12 days depending on yeast, temperature, and sugar content. Signs: airlock bubbles fewer than 5 times per minute, foam almost gone, wash clarified with sediment at bottom, smells like alcohol, tastes bitter or minimally sweet.
10
Filter the wash. Siphon off sediment and simultaneously remove oil from the surface: if the spot is solid, make a hole with your finger and insert a thin tube closer to the bottom. After filtering, it's desirable to clarify with bentonite to settle all solid particles.
11
First distillation. Assemble equipment in pot still mode: column height up to 65 cm, no packing, only direct-flow cooler. Fill the still 30–75% full. Distill at maximum speed. Stop when ABV in the stream drops below 25%.
12
Measure and dilute. Measure ABV at 20 °C and calculate total pure alcohol (ABV% × volume in litres ÷ 100). Dilute to 17–20% ABV. If there are foreign odors, it's desirable to clean with wood activated charcoal.
13
Second distillation with cuts. After boiling, reduce heat so the distillate drips slowly and stays cool. Collect the first 15% of pure alcohol volume as "heads" — discard this fraction, never consume it. Increase heat slightly and collect the "hearts" until ABV drops below 45%.
14
Dilute and rest. Dilute the candy moonshine with soft water to drinking strength — usually 40–45%. Transfer to glass bottles or jars, seal tightly, and leave for at least 5 days in a cool place to stabilize. Shelf life is unlimited.
💡 Editor's tip: Natural hard candies without filling can be used to make a candy tincture — an interesting dessert drink. For pure sugar moonshine, use caramel or lollipops with minimal additives.
