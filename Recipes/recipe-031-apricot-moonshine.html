🍑 Apricot Moonshine (Proper Recipe with Methanol Control)

Apricot moonshine is one of the most aromatic and flavorful fruit distillates available to home distillers. However, during wash preparation it is important to choose the right yeast and not overdo it with sugar (better not to add it at all), then do proper double distillation, getting rid of excessive methanol and fusel oils.

Category
Fruit
ABV
40–45%
Difficulty
Advanced
Fermentation
4–50 days
Yield
~20 kg apricots
⚠️ Legal & Safety Disclaimer: This recipe is provided for educational purposes only. Distilling alcohol at home is illegal in many countries without a licence. Always check your local laws. Read our Distillation Safety Guide before attempting any distillation.

Choosing the Right Apricots

Ripe apricots of any variety and condition are suitable: even crushed and deformed ones, but necessarily without rot or mould. Distillate from wild forest varieties is the most aromatic with a slight bitterness, while garden varieties give a well-rounded soft fruit taste.

Unripe apricots contain a lot of pectin, which during fermentation converts to methanol. Proper distillation solves the methanol problem, but it is more correct to minimize its appearance at the wash preparation stage, so avoid green-skinned and hard fruits in the wash. Apricot pits are safe but bring pronounced almond bitterness to the moonshine — remove them during preparation.

Yield: Depending on variety, growing region, and ripeness, garden apricots contain 7-15% sugar (average 10%), while wild ones only 5-6%. This means from 1 kg of raw material you can get 70-180 ml of 40-degree apricot moonshine, with 14-20% of alcohol lost during double distillation.

Ingredients

  • Apricots (weight with pits) — 20 kg
  • Sugar (not recommended) — up to 4 kg (2 kg per 5 kg apricots)
  • Water — 0.5 L per 1 kg apricots, plus 3 L per 1 kg sugar
  • Spirit or wine yeast (optional) — per package instructions

Baker's yeast requires 20 g dry or 100 g pressed per 1 kg of total sugar (natural + added). Exceeding the recommended amount won't harm the wash, but won't speed up fermentation either.

🛑 Important: Unripe apricots contain high pectin levels, which yeast converts to methanol during fermentation. Although you cannot be poisoned by methanol from apricot moonshine even with a gross technology error, it is more correct to minimize harmful impurities at the wash preparation stage. Always discard the "heads" fraction during distillation — 15% in this recipe. See Distillation Safety Guide.

Step-by-Step Process

1
Prepare the apricots. Sort apricots: remove mouldy and rotten ones. Remove pits. If fermenting on "wild" yeast, do not wash the fruit.
2
Crush the pulp. Squeeze the pulp by hand. Place the resulting mass in the fermentation vessel. It is desirable not to grind apricots mechanically (e.g., with a blender), otherwise the fermented wash will remain thick and cloudy.
3
Activate yeast (if using). Dilute yeast according to package instructions.
4
Add sugar and water. Mix sugar and water. Pour the resulting syrup (or just water for apricot moonshine without sugar) into the fermentation vessel. Fill maximum 75% of volume, leaving room for carbon dioxide and foam.
5
Mix until homogeneous. Stir until consistency is homogeneous. The wash should be liquid (easy to stir); if necessary, add more water. Optionally add yeast.
6
Seal and ferment. Install airlock. Move apricot wash to a dark place (or cover with dense fabric) at 18-28 °C. After 6-20 hours, fermentation signs should appear: hissing, foam, and gas release from the airlock. This means fermentation is proceeding normally.
7
Monitor early fermentation. Watch the wash for the first 3-7 days: every 12 hours, open the vessel and stir, sinking the floating pulp and skin into the liquid, so these particles don't block carbon dioxide release. When almost all pulp settles at the bottom (no dense layer forms on the surface), leave the wash alone — don't open.
8
Confirm fermentation is complete. Depending on chosen yeast, temperature, and sugar content, apricot wash ferments for 4-50 days. Process is complete when: gas stopped releasing from airlock (less than 5 bubbles per minute) or glove deflated; almost no foam on surface; wash is lighter than initial, with a layer of loose sediment at bottom; wash smells like alcohol and is not sweet (with slight bitterness) — yeast converted all sugar to ethanol.
9
Filter the wash. Siphon fermented wash off sediment, preferably through a tube like gasoline from a tank. Squeeze pomace dry. Strain wash through cheesecloth or a fine kitchen sieve, removing the smallest particles that would burn and spoil the taste with persistent bitterness. Cloudy wash can be clarified with bentonite. If apricots were not ripe enough (high pectin), sometimes the wash remains thick like slime — in this case dilute with water to liquid state.
10
Assemble the equipment. Assemble moonshine apparatus or column in simple distiller mode: minimal height and no packing in the thump keg, remove dephlegmator, leave only direct-flow cooler and one thump keg (if provided in design). In factory apparatuses, the appropriate mode is called pot still.
11
First distillation. Pour apricot wash into the distillation pot, filling 30-75% of volume. Make the first distillation at maximum speed (cooling capacity): moonshine may come out warm and cloudy, but necessarily without steam. Finish the process when ABV in stream drops below 25%, or distill to almost zero ABV if energy, water, and time costs seem justified.
12
Measure and calculate. Determine ABV of resulting moonshine at 20 °C. Calculate absolute (anhydrous) alcohol content: volume in litres multiplied by ABV percentage, then divided by 100. All calculations and temperature corrections can be done in the moonshine calculator.
13
Dilute. Dilute with water to 18-20%. Do not clean with charcoal or other methods, otherwise unique tones will disappear.
14
Second distillation. Distill again. After boiling, adjust supplied power so distillate comes out in small drops and is cold — approximately like water in the system. Collect separately the first 15% of liquid from the amount of anhydrous alcohol (result of calculation at step 12). This technical fraction is called "heads" and contains high concentration of harmful substances, so it is not suitable for drinking.
15
Collect the hearts. Increase power so moonshine starts dripping quickly or flowing in a thin stream, but remains cold. Collect "body" (drinking part) until ABV in stream drops below 45%. Then turn off equipment or collect "tails" separately for future processing on a column.
16
Dilute and rest. Dilute finished apricot moonshine ("body") with soft water to drinking strength — 40-45%. Pour into glass containers, seal tightly, and leave for at least 3 days in a cool place for taste stabilization. Shelf life is unlimited.
💡 Editor's tip: To preserve aroma and taste, apricot moonshine should not be distilled on a fully assembled mash or rectification column. Equipment should again be set to simple distiller mode: thump keg height up to 60 cm, dephlegmator and thump keg disabled (if possible), leaving only direct-flow cooler. This time it is desirable to place one section of copper SPN or RPN packing in the thump keg to remove sulfur compounds. Stainless steel packing is useless.
