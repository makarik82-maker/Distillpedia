🍑 Plum Moonshine (with and without sugar)

Plum moonshine has a pronounced fruit aroma and soft taste with notes of dried fruit, caramel, and jam. This recipe covers both approaches: with sugar for higher yield, and without sugar for maximum fruit character. A simpler alternative to classic Balkan Slivovitz.

Category
Fruit
ABV
38–45%
Difficulty
Medium
Fermentation
4–45 days
Yield
~15 kg plums
⚠️ Legal & Safety Disclaimer: This recipe is provided for educational purposes only. Distilling alcohol at home is illegal in many countries without a licence. Always check your local laws. Read our Distillation Safety Guide before attempting any distillation.

Ingredients

  • 15 kg plums — any variety, pitted weight
  • Sugar (optional): up to 100 g per 1 kg plums (50–70 g recommended)
  • Water: 1 L per 1 kg plums, plus 3 L per 1 kg sugar
  • Yeast: per manufacturer's instructions

Sugar increases yield by 1.2 L per kg but adds kvas-like notes. For better flavor, replace sugar with dextrose or fructose in the same quantities.

🛑 Safety reminder: Unripe plums contain high pectin levels, which yeast converts to methanol. Use only ripe fruit, and always discard the "heads" fraction during distillation. See Distillation Safety Guide.

Step-by-Step Process

1
Sort the plums. Remove any blackened, rotten, or mouldy fruit. If fermenting on wild yeast, do not wash the plums (wipe dirty ones with a dry cloth). If using fallen fruit from the ground, rinse briefly under running water to avoid earthy tones.
2
Remove the pits. This is critical to avoid bitter almond flavours. Plum pits contain amygdalin, which gives an unpleasant bitter taste to the final distillate.
3
Mash the plums. Place the pitted flesh and skin in a wide-mouth fermentation vessel. Crush into a smooth paste.
4
Prepare the wash. Activate yeast according to package instructions. If using sugar, dissolve it in warm water (28–30 °C) to make a syrup. Pour the syrup or water into the plum paste and stir until the mixture is liquid enough to stir freely. Fill the vessel no more than 75% full.
5
Add yeast and seal. Pitch the activated yeast. Fit an airlock and move to a dark place with stable temperature 18–28 °C.
6
Monitor early fermentation. For the first 3–4 days, check every 8–12 hours. If floating pulp blocks the airlock, open and stir, pushing solids back into the liquid. After a few days most pulp will settle at the bottom.
7
Confirm fermentation is complete. This takes 4–45 days depending on yeast, temperature, and sugar content. Signs: airlock stops bubbling (fewer than 5 bubbles/min), foam disappears, sediment forms at bottom, wash clarifies, smells like alcohol, and tastes dry (not sweet).
8
First distillation. Siphon the wash off sediment. Squeeze the pulp gently and filter through several layers of cheesecloth or a fine sieve to remove solid particles that could burn. Fill the still 30–75% full. Assemble in simple pot-still mode. Distill at maximum speed. Stop when the ABV in the stream drops below 25%.
9
Measure and dilute. Measure the ABV at 20 °C and calculate the total pure alcohol. Dilute with water to 18–20% ABV. Do not clean with charcoal — this strips the fruit aroma.
10
Second distillation with cuts. Heat slowly so the distillate drips and stays cool. Collect the first 14% of pure alcohol volume as "heads" — discard this fraction, never consume it. Increase heat slightly and collect the "hearts" until the ABV in the stream drops below 43%. Optionally collect "tails" separately for future redistillation.
11
Dilute and rest. Dilute the plum moonshine with softened water to 38–45% ABV. Bottle in glass, seal tightly, and rest for at least 3 days in a cool place to stabilize the flavor. Shelf life is unlimited.
💡 Editor's tip: The variety of plum significantly affects the flavor. "Hungarian" (Венгерка) gives a pronounced prune profile, while "Renklod" gives soft fruit tones with a subtle caramel finish. Darker-skinned plums produce richer dried-fruit and jam notes.

Choosing the Right Plums

  • Color: Blue and red plums give dried-fruit, jam, and spice notes. Yellow plums give soft fruit and caramel tones.
  • Variety: Do not mix different varieties in one batch — the flavor profile becomes "blurred." Blend finished distillates instead.
  • Ripeness: Use only ripe or overripe plums. Underripe fruit is high in pectin, which increases methanol production.
  • Sugar content: Average sugar content is 10–13% (up to 20% in southern regions). Theoretical yield: 120–156 ml of 40% moonshine per 1 kg plums.
