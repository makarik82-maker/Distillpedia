🥔 Potato Moonshine (Classic Wash Recipe)

Due to high protein and pectin content, potato moonshine has a specific character — with notes of fresh tubers in the aroma and taste. Moreover, if prepared incorrectly, it causes a severe hangover. It is advisable to make this drink if you have a lot of unwanted potatoes that cannot be processed in other ways. For distillation, it is better to use a rectification or mash column to obtain the purest spirit. If you only have a regular moonshine apparatus, potato moonshine can become the alcohol base for spicy male tinctures, e.g., Pertsovka (pepper vodka) or Khrenovukha (horseradish vodka).

Category
Grain
ABV
40–45%
Difficulty
Advanced
Fermentation
5–12 days
Yield
~10 kg potatoes
⚠️ Legal & Safety Disclaimer: This recipe is provided for educational purposes only. Distilling alcohol at home is illegal in many countries without a licence. Always check your local laws. Read our Distillation Safety Guide before attempting any distillation.

Ingredients

  • 10 kg potatoes — preferably high-starch varieties
  • Non-fermented malt: 1.5 kg (15% of potato weight)
  • Water: 25 L (2.5 L per 1 kg potatoes)
  • Baker's yeast: 100 g dry or 500 g pressed

Spirit yeast should be added per manufacturer's instructions depending on wash volume.

🛑 Important: The selected malt must be non-fermented. Fermented rye malt sold in culinary departments is a food additive and cannot convert starch to sugar. A thermometer is mandatory for saccharification. The temperature must stay within 61–72 °C — outside this range, enzymes are destroyed or saccharification efficiency drops dramatically. Always discard the "heads" fraction — 15% in this recipe (higher than usual due to high pectin). See Distillation Safety Guide.

Step-by-Step Process

1
Prepare the potatoes. Sort potatoes: cut away rotten, mouldy, and greenish parts (contain harmful solanine). The skin can be left — it adds pronounced potato tones to the moonshine.
2
Cut the tubers. Cut into 5–6 cm cubes or grate.
3
Cook the potatoes. Place potatoes in a cooking pot, add water. Bring to a boil and cook on medium heat for 45–60 minutes until you get a porridge-like mass. Stir periodically to prevent potatoes from burning near the bottom and walls.
4
Add malt. Cool the pot contents to 65 °C. Slowly add the crushed malt, stirring constantly for even distribution throughout the potato wash volume.
5
Maintain saccharification. Maintain temperature 62–70 °C for 90 minutes. The easiest way: cover with a lid, then stir and check temperature every 15 minutes; when the value approaches the lower limit of the range, turn on low heat. Toward the end of saccharification, the wash will clarify, and the solid part will settle at the bottom. The main sign of successful starch breakdown to simple sugars — sweetness.
6
Cool the wash. Cool the wash to 25–28 °C. To reduce the risk of contamination by pathogenic microflora, do this as quickly as possible. For example, place the pot in cold water or surround with ice.
7
Activate the yeast. Activate spirit yeast according to package instructions. Dry baker's yeast: sprinkle evenly over the surface of water at 28–30 °C (10 ml per 1 g), stir after 15 minutes and leave for another 5 minutes. Foam indicates successful activation. Pressed baker's yeast can be added directly from the refrigerator, crumbled with clean hands.
8
Transfer to fermentation vessel. Pour the cooled wash into the fermentation vessel, add yeast. Fill max 70% of volume, as potato wash can foam heavily. If the process gets out of control, add any foam suppressant.
9
Ferment. Close with an airlock, leave the container in a dark room (cover with dense fabric) at 18–28 °C. Depending on the yeast, temperature, and success of saccharification, potato wash ferments for 5–12 days. When the airlock stops releasing gas (fewer than 5 bubbles per minute) or the glove deflates, and there are no sweet tones in the wash taste (yeast converted all sugars), you can proceed to the next stage.
10
Filter the wash. Siphon the wash off sediment through a tube, then filter through cheesecloth or a fine sieve to remove solid particles that could burn. Thick washes can only be distilled with a steam generator; in all other cases, filtration is mandatory, otherwise the moonshine will be bitter.
11
Assemble the equipment. Assemble distillation equipment in pot still mode: column height up to 60 cm, disable dephlegmator and all additional units, leave only a direct-flow cooler and one thump keg (if included in the design).
12
First distillation. Pour the potato wash into the still. Fill 30–70% of volume. Distill at maximum speed (maximum cooler utilization): the moonshine may come out cloudy and warm, but there should be no steam. When the ABV in the stream drops below 25%, turn off the heat. It is permissible to distill almost "to water" if additional energy and time costs seem justified by a slight increase in quantity.
13
Measure and dilute. Measure the ABV of the resulting low wines at 20 °C, or adjust the value for actual temperature using an online calculator. Determine the amount of pure (absolute) alcohol: ABV% × volume in litres ÷ 100. Dilute the distillate with water to 18–20%. To reduce harmful impurities and slightly improve taste, it is desirable to clean with wood activated charcoal.
14
Second distillation with cuts. Distill the potato moonshine a second time. After the liquid in the still boils, reduce the power; the distillate should drip slowly and be cold, almost like water in the system. Collect the first 15% of the pure alcohol volume separately. This is the "heads" — the initial fraction with high content of harmful impurities.
15
Collect the hearts. Increase the apparatus power, achieving fast drops or a stream, but the drink must remain cold. Collect the drinking portion ("body") until the ABV in the stream drops below 45%, then turn off the equipment or collect "tails" separately to rectify them later on a column.
16
Dilute and rest. Dilute the finished potato moonshine ("body") with softened water to drinking strength — 40–45%. You can clean once more with wood or coconut activated charcoal to bring the organoleptics closer to potato vodka. Pour into glass bottles or jars, seal tightly, and leave for at least 5 days in a cool place to stabilize. Shelf life is unlimited.
💡 Editor's tip: For potato moonshine, base barley malt is best. If using homemade malt, increase the proportion to 20% of total potato weight to ensure enough enzymes. The malt must be non-fermented — fermented "red" malt sold in culinary departments is only a flavor additive and cannot saccharify starch.
