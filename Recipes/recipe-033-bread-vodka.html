🍞 Homemade Bread Vodka (Sugar-Grain Moonshine)

Despite the sugar base, homemade bread vodka has a pleasant grain aftertaste without harsh tones. The technology does not require "hot" or "cold" saccharification: only the added sugar will be converted to alcohol, while the wheat and rye will simply improve the organoleptic properties of the drink and serve as a source of "wild" yeast. This is a popular Soviet recipe with modern adjustments.

Category
Grain
ABV
40–45%
Difficulty
Advanced
Fermentation
7–40 days
Yield
~4 kg grain
⚠️ Legal & Safety Disclaimer: This recipe is provided for educational purposes only. Distilling alcohol at home is illegal in many countries without a licence. Always check your local laws. Read our Distillation Safety Guide before attempting any distillation.

Choosing the Right Grain

Wheat softens the taste, while rye adds pronounced bread notes and the characteristic vodka sharpness. All grain must be whole, well-dried, untreated with chemicals (otherwise "wild" yeast will die), without foreign odors (especially mustiness), and stored in a ventilated room for no more than a year.

One batch of grain can be used for three portions of wash: simply add sugar and water again in the same proportions, then start from the third point of the recipe — skip the starter preparation (yeast cells will remain on the surface). Often the second bread vodka turns out to be the best.

Ingredients

  • Wheat — 1 kg
  • Rye — 3 kg
  • Sugar — 5 kg
  • Water — 36 L (4 L per 1 kg sugar and grain)

Grain proportions can be adjusted to create a different blend, or you can use only one type — just wheat or rye. Barley is not recommended, as it gives weak organoleptics in this recipe.

🛑 Important: The sugar in this recipe provides the ethyl alcohol. The grain cannot be fermented directly by yeast (starch is not converted). However, the grain contributes "wild" yeast and flavor. Always discard the "heads" fraction during distillation — 12% in this recipe. See Distillation Safety Guide.

Step-by-Step Process

1
Sift the grain. Winnow all grain, removing husks, debris, and dust.
2
Make the starter. Put 1 kg unwashed wheat (or rye if using only this grain) in a three-liter jar. Cover with water so grains are submerged by 4-5 cm. Close tightly with a lid and leave for 2-3 days in a dark place (or cover) at 8-16 °C. When small sprouts appear, dissolve 500 g sugar in 200-250 ml water, pour the syrup into the jar, and mix until homogeneous. Tie the jar neck with gauze (air must access), leave for 5-7 days in a dark room at 20-26 °C. Stir once a day, preventing souring. The starter is ready when foam is visible near the surface, hissing is heard, and a light cucumber smell appears. Warning: In rare cases, "wild" yeast activates up to 10 days. If fermentation doesn't start or mold appears, the chosen grain is not suitable for starter preparation. Save the wash with regular spirit or baker's yeast.
3
Mix the wash. Mix remaining sugar and water in the fermentation vessel. Add grain, then add the starter and stir. Fill the vessel maximum 75% of volume, leaving enough free space for foam.
4
Seal and ferment. Install an airlock, otherwise the wash will sour. If the neck diameter allows, you can tie or tape a new medical glove, piercing any finger.
5
Ferment. Leave the wash in a dark room (cover with dense fabric) with stable temperature 20-28 °C. Depending on the activated "wild" yeast strain and temperature, fermentation lasts 7-40 days. Process is finished when: the airlock emits less than 5 bubbles per minute or the glove deflated; almost no foam remains on the surface; the wash at least partially clarified; the wash smells like alcohol and is not sweet (yeast converted all sugar). Warning: If mold appears during fermentation or other signs of contamination: unpleasant smell and/or wash became thick, urgently filter the grain (unsuitable for new batches), boil the liquid part, simmer on medium heat for 2-3 minutes, then cool below 30 °C, add spirit (optimal) or baker's yeast, and finish fermenting under an airlock in a thoroughly washed vessel.
6
Filter the wash. Filter the fermented grain wash through a fine sieve or gauze, then pour into the distillation pot, filling 30-70% of volume. The released grain can be poured again with sugar syrup in the same proportion and left to ferment under an airlock again.
7
Assemble the equipment. Assemble distillation equipment in simple distiller mode: thump keg height up to 60 cm and no packing, disable all auxiliary units, leaving only a shell-and-tube or coil cooler in the system, as well as one thump keg if provided by design. In factory apparatuses, the suitable mode is usually called pot still.
8
First distillation. Make the first distillation at maximum speed (cooler capacity): moonshine may come out cloudy and warm, as long as there is no steam. End the process when strength in the stream drops below 25 degrees. If time and energy costs seem justified, you can distill the wash almost "to water".
9
Measure and calculate. Determine the strength of the obtained crude spirit at 20 °C or correct the value for another temperature in the moonshine calculator. Calculate the volume of absolute (pure) alcohol: strength percentage multiplied by volume in liters, then divided by one hundred.
10
Dilute. Dilute the distillate with regular water to 18-20% strength. Do not clean with any methods, preserving bread-grain tones. Final finishing of the drink will be done later.
11
Second distillation. Distill a second time. When the pot liquid boils, reduce power so moonshine drips in small drops and is cold like water in the apparatus. Pour separately 12% of initial output from the amount of absolute alcohol (calculated in step 9). This is the "head" of moonshine — a fraction with high concentration of harmful substances.
12
Collect the hearts. Collect "body" (main part) until strength in the stream drops to 42%. Then end distillation (turn off heating) or accumulate "tails" in another vessel to rectify this part on a column in the future, extracting remaining ethyl alcohol.
13
Dilute and rest. Dilute the obtained moonshine ("body") with softened water to 43-45%. Pour into glass bottles or jars, seal tightly, and leave in a cool place for 5 days to stabilize.
14
Evaluate the taste. To approach vodka organoleptics, you can charcoal filter by any of the suggested methods (requires activated coconut or wood charcoal). However, it's better to experiment on a small amount first, as homemade bread vodka after charring becomes sharp — an acquired taste. Second path — infuse on oak pegs, getting a decent whiskey imitation. In hermetically sealed glass containers, shelf life is unlimited.
💡 Editor's tip: This recipe is unique because it requires no saccharification — the sugar provides the alcohol, while the grain contributes flavor and wild yeast. It's an excellent introduction to grain spirits for beginners who aren't ready for malt-based recipes yet.
