🥭 Mango Moonshine

A delicate fruit-citrus aroma with lime tones; the taste shows notes of ripe mango pulp, gooseberry, green apple, melon and tropical fruit. One of the richest tropical distillates you cannot buy in a shop.

Category
Fruit
ABV
40–45%
Difficulty
Medium
Fermentation
5–45 days
Yield
96–144 ml/kg (40%)
⚠️ Educational purposes only. Home distillation is illegal in many countries without a licence. Check your local laws first.

Choosing the Ingredients

Mango

Any mango works: crushed, mis-stored, undersized, etc. — as long as there is no rot or mould (problem spots can be cut off). Home distillers usually arrange with market sellers to take unsold leftovers at a reasonable price. Green mangoes are more aromatic, but yellow pulp keeps more fruit tones. Very firm fruit should rest for 4–5 days at room temperature, otherwise a sour note appears in the aftertaste.

Yield

Mango contains 8–12% sugar (usually about 9%). Theoretically 1 kg gives 96–144 ml of 40° spirit; the practical yield is 15–20% lower after two runs.

Sugar

1 kg of sugar adds 1.2 L of 40% distillate but dulls the profile with molasses and kvass tones, so do not overdo it. Experimentally, up to 150 g of sugar per 1 kg of mango is acceptable (optimum 80–100 g). Fructose and dextrose in the same proportion are preferable: they have no aroma or taste of their own and only "smear" the mango notes with pure ethanol.

Water

Clean juice cannot be pressed from mango without losing 50–60% of the sugar, and a thick must ferments poorly, sours easily and is hard to distil — so water is required. It must be non-chlorinated (chlorine suppresses yeast): well, street dispenser, column or filtered; tap water stood 3 days in an open vessel also works. Dilute the finished spirit only with softened water (bottled or reverse-osmosis); boiled water lacks oxygen and microelements and is the worst choice.

Citric acid

After dilution with water the total acidity of mango pulp becomes insufficient for normal fermentation — a low-acidity must ferments slower and is prone to infection. The simplest fix is adding citric acid to the must (or fresh lemon juice).

Yeast or starter

There are no wild yeast on mango skin because tropical fruit is treated with preservatives. Best choice: cultured wine yeast (universal strains or for white grape juice), or a berry/raisin starter set 4–5 days ahead. Spirit yeast finishes in 4–8 days but the aroma and taste are poorer. Fruit-wash yeast (with pectinase) is justified for unripe pulp with much pectin. Dry or pressed baker's yeast leaves almost no mango tones and adds a bread-kvass background — use only when nothing else is available.

Ingredients

  • Mango — 5 kg (whole fruit weight)
  • Water — 10 l, plus 4 l per 1 kg of sugar
  • Sugar (optional) — up to 150 g per 1 kg of mango
  • Citric acid (optional) — to balance must acidity
  • Yeast or starter — per wash volume (baker's: 100 g pressed or 20 g dry per 1 kg of total sugar)

Wash Preparation

1
Rinse the mango with warm running water and wipe dry with paper napkins to remove the preservative film. Peel the skin (it contains preservatives that slow fermentation and adds a grassy-bitter aftertaste). Cut out blackened, mouldy and rotten parts.
2
Cut the pulp with a blender or meat grinder to a puree and place it into the fermentation vessel.
3
Activate the yeast per the package instructions, or by the universal starter method.
4
Dissolve the sugar (optional) and citric acid in water, add the solution to the mango pulp and stir to a homogeneous consistency. If the must stays thick (hard to stir by hand), add more water. Fill the vessel to max 75%, leaving room for foam and CO₂.
5
Pitch the yeast or starter, fit any airlock and keep the wash in a dark place (or covered) at 18–28 °C. Fermentation must start within 36 hours: hissing, foam and gas from the airlock (glove inflates). If not, find and fix the cause urgently.
6
For the first 3–4 days check the wash every 8–10 hours so the floating pulp cap does not block the airlock tube. Open and stir as needed, submerging the cap in the liquid. After a few days almost all pulp settles and stops floating.
7
Fermentation lasts 5–45 days depending on yeast, sugar and temperature. It is finished when: gas release stops (glove deflates); a loose sediment layer forms at the bottom; foam has disappeared from the surface (patches up to 3 cm may remain); the wort has partly lightened; the wash smells of alcohol and has no sweetness.
8
Rack the finished wash off the sediment (preferably through a thin tube); the pomace may be lightly pressed. Strain the liquid through a fine sieve or cheesecloth to remove solids that could scorch and make the spirit bitter.

Distillation

9
Fill the still to 30–70% and assemble the equipment as a simple pot still: column up to 60 cm with no packing, dephlegmator and auxiliary units off, leaving only a coil or shell-and-tube condenser and one thump keg if the design has one. Factory columns: pot still mode.
10
First run at maximum cooler capacity: the distillate may run warm and cloudy but never steamy. Stop when the stream strength falls below 20%. Running almost "to zero" is acceptable if the small gain justifies the extra energy, water and time.
11
Measure the raw spirit strength at 20 °C (or correct in a calculator) and compute the pure alcohol: volume in litres × strength % ÷ 100. Dilute to 18–20%. Do not clean with carbon or other methods — keep the delicate fruit organoleptics.
12
Second run: after boiling, reduce power so the distillate drips slowly and stays cold, almost like the inlet water. Collect the first 14% of the pure-alcohol amount separately as "heads" — a harmful technical fraction.
13
Raise power smoothly so the spirit drips fast or runs in a thin stream but stays cold. Collect the "body" until the stream strength falls below 42%, then stop or collect "tails" separately for future rectification.
14
Dilute the mango moonshine ("body") with softened water to 40–45%, bottle in glass, seal tightly and rest for at least 5 days in a cool place. Shelf life is unlimited.
💡 To keep the tropical profile, run the second distillation in pot-still mode and add one or two sections of copper packing (SPN/RPN) to the column for sulfur removal; stainless packing only slows the run.
