🍊 Mandarin Moonshine (with or without sugar)

Mandarin moonshine has a floral aroma with tones of orange and lemon zest, a soft fruit taste with a barely noticeable bitterness, and a long aftertaste dominated by candied fruit notes. The main thing is to set the wash correctly, otherwise the bitterness will become excessive.

Category
Fruit
ABV
40–45%
Difficulty
Medium
Fermentation
5–45 days
Yield
~10 kg mandarins
⚠️ Legal & Safety Disclaimer: This recipe is provided for educational purposes only. Distilling alcohol at home is illegal in many countries without a proper licence. Always check your local laws. Read our Distillation Safety Guide before attempting any distillation.

Choosing the Ingredients

Mandarins

Mandarins of any variety and condition are suitable for processing into moonshine: expired, crushed, too small, etc. Usually distillers arrange with market sellers and take unwanted leftovers at a discount. It is important not to allow rotten and moldy pulp into the wash, carefully sorting the raw material. The sweeter and juicier the fruit, the better. In not fully ripe fruit (with greenish spots on the skin) there is an increased pectin content, which yeast converts into methanol, intensifying the hangover.

When there is enough quality raw material, I advise making mandarin wine, and if the drink does not appeal, distilling it according to the method proposed here.

Yield: depending on variety and ripeness, mandarins contain 7–14% sugar. This means that from 1 kg of fruit you can theoretically get 84–200 ml of 40° moonshine, but after two distillations the practical yield will be 15–20% lower due to losses of ethyl alcohol.

Sugar, Water, Yeast

  • Sugar (optional): 1 kg of beet or cane sugar increases the distillate by 1.2 L (40%), simultaneously worsening organoleptics with molasses or kvass tones. Empirically, up to 100 g of sugar per 1 kg of mandarins can be added (optimally 40–70 g). Fructose and dextrose affect mandarin moonshine less.
  • Water: reduces excessive acidity of mandarin juice, promoting fermentation, and allows controlling maximum wash sugar content (should be up to 20%). Any chlorine-free water is acceptable.
  • Yeast: there are no "wild" yeast on mandarin pulp, and adding even untreated peel is impossible, otherwise the drink will be very bitter. Best to set the wash on "cultured" wine yeast: universal strains or those for white grape juice. A free alternative is raisin or berry starter prepared 4–5 days before processing. Spirit yeast finishes in 4–8 days. Baker's yeast is the worst fit.

Ingredients

  • Mandarins — 10 kg (weight of unpeeled fruit)
  • Sugar (optional) — up to 100 g per 1 kg of mandarins
  • Water — 250 ml per 1 kg of fruit, plus 4 L per 1 kg of sugar
  • Yeast — per label instructions depending on wort volume

Starter requires 2–3% of wash volume; baker's yeast — 20 g dry or 100 g pressed per 1 kg of total sugar (natural + added). Approximate calculations suffice.

Step-by-Step Process

1
Clean the fruit. Wash mandarins with running water, then remove the peel and maximize removal of the albedo — the white pulp near the segments. Optionally dry the peel from 2 medium fruits per 5 kg of total weight to enhance the citrus aroma during distillation; the remaining peel is not needed.
2
Sort segments. Inspect the segments, discard rotten, moldy, and vinegar-smelling ones. Remove most of the seeds.
🛑 Cleaning Matters: The organoleptic properties of mandarin moonshine depend on the thoroughness of cleaning. An excess of essential oils from the peel adds a persistent eau-de-cologne smell, and the white pulp and seeds add bitterness with grassy tones.
3
Crush. Mash the mandarin segments by hand or mechanically: meat grinder, blender, etc. Place in the fermentation vessel.
4
Activate yeast. Dilute yeast per package instructions, or start with the universal method. The starter can be left unfiltered from raisin or berry residues, making the wash more nutritious.
5
Make the wash. Dissolve sugar in water. Pour the resulting syrup (or only water) into the mandarin pulp, mix until homogeneous. The wort should become liquid — freely mixable by hand; if necessary, dilute with more water.
6
Pitch and fill. Add active yeast or starter. Fill fermentation vessels to a maximum of 75% of volume, leaving enough space for foam and carbon dioxide.
7
Seal and start. Close with an airlock of any design. Leave in a dark room (cover with dense fabric) at a stable temperature of 18–28 °C. Fermentation should start within 36 hours: foam will appear, and gas will begin to release from the airlock (glove inflates). If this did not happen, find the cause and fix the situation as quickly as possible.
8
Monitor the cap. For the first 3–4 days, check the wash every 8–10 hours so that the pomace (mandarin pulp) floating to the surface does not impede carbon dioxide release. If necessary, open the vessel and stir the wort, submerging pulp residues in the liquid. Over time all pomace will settle at the bottom and stop floating; then leave the wash alone.
9
Confirm completion. Depending on yeast, temperature, and sugar content, mandarin wash ferments for 5–45 days. Signs of readiness: gas release from the airlock has stopped (fewer than 5 bubbles per minute) or the glove deflated; almost no foam remains near the surface; the wort has at least partially clarified, a layer of loose sediment is visible at the bottom; the wash smells of alcohol and is not sweet (yeast fermented all the sugar).

Distillation

10
Filter and fill. Drain the fermented wash from the sediment (preferably through a tube). Squeeze the pulp, then filter through a fine sieve or cheesecloth, guaranteed removing solid particles that could scorch from high temperature during distillation. Pour the wash into the distillation pot, filling 30–70% of volume.
11
Assemble equipment. Assemble the equipment in simple distiller mode: column height up to 60 cm with no packing inside, all auxiliary units off, leaving only a shell-and-tube or coil condenser, plus one bubble trap if provided by design. In factory columns the required mode is usually called pot still.
12
First distillation. Make the first distillation at maximum speed: moonshine may flow cloudy and warm, as long as there is no steam. End the process when the strength in the stream drops below 20%. Running "to water" is also acceptable if a slight increase in drink quantity seems justified by additional energy and time costs.
13
Calculate. Measure the strength of the resulting moonshine at 20 °C, or correct the value in a moonshine calculator for another temperature. Determine the amount of pure (absolute) alcohol: liters of volume multiplied by strength percentage, divided by 100.
14
Dilute. Dilute the raw spirit with water to 18–20%. Do not clean with activated charcoal or other methods, preserving the organoleptics.
15
Second distillation — heads. Distill mandarin moonshine a second time. When the liquid boils, reduce the power so the drink flows in small drops and is cold almost like water in the system. Collect separately 13–14% of output from the amount of absolute alcohol (calculated at step 13) — this is the "head", the initial fraction that is not suitable for drinking due to high concentration of harmful impurities.
16
Collect the hearts. Smoothly raise the power: moonshine should drip fast or flow in a thin stream. Collect the drinkable fraction ("body") until the strength in the stream drops below 43%. Then end the distillation or accumulate "tails" separately to rectify them on a column in the future.
17
Dilute and rest. Dilute the finished mandarin moonshine ("body") with softened water to drinking strength — 40–45 degrees. Pour into glass jars or bottles, seal tightly. Leave for at least 3 days in a cool place to stabilize. Shelf life is unlimited.
💡 Editor's tip: To enhance mandarin aroma and citrus taste, you can place dry zest (prepared at step 1) in the bubble trap or hang it in the distillation pot in a cheesecloth bag. The bag is more effective but must not touch the liquid or pot walls. In turn, it is safe to fill the bubble trap no more than a third of the volume, otherwise zest swollen from steaming may accidentally block the steam pipe.
