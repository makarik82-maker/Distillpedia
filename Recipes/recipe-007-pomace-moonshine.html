🍇 Pomace Moonshine (Apple or Grape Pressings)

After pressing juice or making wine, the leftover pomace (skins, pulp, seeds) still contains residual sugars. This recipe shows how to turn those pressings into an aromatic distillate with subtle fruit character. Works with grape, apple, cherry, apricot, or plum pressings.

Category
Fruit
ABV
38–45%
Difficulty
Medium
Fermentation
5–40 days
Yield
~15 kg pomace
⚠️ Legal & Safety Disclaimer: This recipe is provided for educational purposes only. Distilling alcohol at home is illegal in many countries without a licence. Always check your local laws. Read our Distillation Safety Guide before attempting any distillation.

Ingredients

  • 15 kg pomace — apple or grape pressings (skins, pulp, seeds)
  • Sugar (optional): up to 1 kg per 3 kg pomace
  • Water: 1–1.5 L per 1 kg pomace, plus 3 L per 1 kg sugar
  • Yeast (optional): wine yeast recommended, per package instructions

Start fermentation within 10–14 hours of pressing to prevent souring. If not possible, store pomace submerged in water, sealed, in the fridge for up to 2 days.

🛑 Safety reminder: Strong pressing can damage seeds, releasing tannins that give a bitter almond taste. Always discard the "heads" fraction during distillation. See Distillation Safety Guide.

Step-by-Step Process

1
Prepare the pomace. Place the pressings in a wide-mouth fermentation vessel. For grape pomace, remove any remaining stems (rachises) if you don't want astringent tones. Activate yeast according to package instructions.
2
Prepare the wash. If using sugar, dissolve it in warm water (28–30 °C, no higher) to make a syrup. Pour the syrup or clean water into the pomace and stir until the mixture is liquid enough to stir freely. Fill the vessel no more than 80% full.
3
Add yeast and seal. Pitch the activated yeast. Fit an airlock and move to a dark place with stable temperature 18–28 °C.
4
Monitor early fermentation. For the first 2–3 days, check that floating pomace does not block the airlock. Open and stir if needed, pushing solids back into the liquid. After a few days most pomace will settle at the bottom.
5
Confirm fermentation is complete. This takes 5–40 days depending on yeast, sugar content, and temperature. Signs: airlock stops bubbling, foam disappears, sediment forms at bottom, wash clarifies, smells like alcohol, and tastes dry (not sweet).
6
First distillation. Filter the wash through several layers of cheesecloth or a fine sieve. Squeeze the pomace gently. Fill the still 30–75% full. Assemble in simple pot-still mode (no packing, no dephlegmator). Distill at maximum speed. Stop when the ABV in the stream drops below 20%.
7
Enhance aroma (optional). Hang a small amount of pomace in a cheesecloth bag inside the still, ensuring it does not touch the boiling wash, walls, or lid. Alternatively, add pomace to the thump keg (fill up to 25% volume). This enriches the distillate with additional fruit aroma.
8
Measure and dilute. Measure the ABV at 20 °C and calculate the total pure alcohol. Dilute with water to 18–20% ABV. Do not clean with charcoal — this strips the fruit aroma.
9
Second distillation with cuts. Heat slowly so the distillate drips and stays cool. Collect the first 12–15% of pure alcohol volume as "heads" — discard this fraction, never consume it. Increase heat slightly and collect the "hearts" until the ABV in the stream drops below 43–44%. Optionally collect "tails" separately for future redistillation.
10
Dilute and rest. Dilute the pomace moonshine with softened water to 38–45% ABV. Bottle in glass, seal tightly, and rest for at least 3 days in a cool place to stabilize the flavor. Shelf life is unlimited.
💡 Editor's tip: The sugar content of pomace varies: grape pomace typically contains 2–10% sugar (usually 3–4%), while apple pomace contains 3–8% (usually 4–5%). Theoretical yield: 24–120 ml of 40% moonshine per 1 kg pomace, but up to 20% alcohol is lost during double distillation.

Pomace vs. Pressings: What's the Difference?

  • Pomace (мезга): In winemaking, this refers to the residue after pressing grapes for juice — pulp, skins, and sometimes stems. In a broader sense, it includes any fruit or berry pressings.
  • Pressings (жмых): The solid residue after extracting juice from fruits or vegetables. Essentially the same as pomace, but the term is more commonly used for apple, carrot, or other non-grape pressings.
  • Both work identically for this recipe. The techniques described apply to any fruit pressings.
