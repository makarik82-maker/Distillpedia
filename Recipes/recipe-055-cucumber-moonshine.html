🥒 Cucumber Moonshine

A herbal aroma with a watermelon-rind note and quince (duchess) tones in the aftertaste. Because cucumber pulp is very low in sugar, the result is effectively a refined sugar spirit — an excellent drink for light meat and fish snacks.

Category
Fruit / Veg
ABV
40–45%
Difficulty
Medium
Fermentation
4–12 days
Yield
~24 ml/kg + sugar
⚠️ Educational purposes only. Home distillation is illegal in many countries without a licence. Check your local laws first.

Choosing the Ingredients

Cucumbers

Use green (no yellow spots), firm and aromatic cucumbers; overripe ones give bitterness from the seeds. The optimal size is 5–10 cm. Wash off all soil residues.

Yield & sugar

Natural sugar content is only 1.5–2%, so the theoretical yield without sugar is up to 24 ml of 40° spirit per 1 kg — and such a low-sugar wash ferments poorly because yeast lacks nutrition. Therefore sugar is mandatory here: 400 g per 1 kg of fruit is the empirically best balance. Fructose or dextrose in the same amount are preferable (no kvass or molasses tones).

Water & acid

Although cucumbers are 95% water, it sits in the intercellular space and is hard to press out; a thick wash ferments slowly and is prone to infection, so water is added — but less than for a classic sugar wash. Cucumber acidity is at most 3 g/l versus the recommended 5–12 g/l, so concentrated citric acid stabilises the wort and speeds up fermentation without affecting the finished drink.

Yeast

After washing, no wild yeast remain on the skin, so another source is required. Spirit yeast (regular or "turbo") is best; the cucumber wort is poor in nutrients, so a homemade nutrient (raisins, malt, bread, tomato paste) is recommended for baker's or standard spirit yeast. Baker's yeast ferments a couple of days longer and adds kvass tones, but competent fractional distillation removes them.

Ingredients

  • Cucumbers — 5 kg
  • Sugar — 2 kg (400 g per 1 kg of fruit)
  • Water — 3 L (1.5 L per 1 kg of sugar)
  • Citric acid — 45 g (15 g per 1 L of water)
  • Spirit or baker's yeast — for 10 L of wort (baker's: 20 g dry or 100 g pressed per 1 kg of sugar)

Wash Preparation

1
Wash the cucumbers, cut off the ends (they are very bitter) and any blackened, mouldy or rotten parts. Grate, blend or mince the fruit to a homogeneous liquid mass and place it into the fermentation vessel.
2
Activate the yeast: spirit yeast per the package; dry baker's scattered over a sugar syrup surface (10 ml water and 2 g sugar per 1 g yeast) at 28–30 °C, wait 5 minutes, stir and leave 10–15 minutes in a dark place until stable foam; pressed baker's straight from the fridge, kneaded with clean hands.
3
Dissolve the sugar and citric acid in the water. Pour the syrup into the cucumber mass and stir until homogeneous. Add the activated yeast and the nutrient (optional). Fill the vessel to max 75%, leaving space for foam and CO₂.
4
Fit an airlock and move the wash to a dark place at 18–28 °C. Fermentation signs (foam and hissing) must appear within 24 hours; if not, pitch fresh yeast.
5
For the first 3 days check at least once every 8–10 hours so the floating pulp and skins do not block the airlock tube; open and stir as needed, breaking the dense top layer. After a few days all pulp settles and stops floating.
6
Fermentation lasts 4–12 days depending on yeast and temperature. It is finished when: the airlock releases fewer than 5 bubbles per minute (or the glove deflates); the wort has lightened with a loose sediment layer; almost no foam remains; the wash has a slightly bitter taste without sweetness.
7
Rack the finished wash off the sediment (preferably through a thin tube), then strain through cheesecloth or a fine sieve to remove solids that could scorch during heating.

Distillation

8
Assemble the equipment as a simple pot still: column up to 65 cm with no packing, all auxiliary units off, leaving only a direct-flow condenser (coil or shell-and-tube) and one bubble trap if the design has one. Factory columns: pot still mode.
9
Fill the boiler to 30–75% and run the first distillation at maximum speed: the distillate may run warm and cloudy but never steamy. Stop when the stream strength falls below 25%; running almost to zero is acceptable if the small gain justifies the extra energy and time.
10
Measure the raw spirit strength at 20 °C (or correct in a calculator) and calculate the pure alcohol: volume in litres × strength % ÷ 100. Dilute to 18–20%. Do not clean with carbon or other methods — keep the cucumber organoleptics.
11
Second run: after boiling, reduce power so the spirit drips slowly and stays cold. Collect the first 12% of the pure-alcohol amount separately as "heads" — a harmful fraction unfit for drinking.
12
Raise power smoothly so the spirit drips fast or runs in a thin stream but stays cold. Collect the "body" until the stream strength falls below 43%.
13
Stop, or collect "tails" separately for future rectification on a column.
14
Dilute the cucumber moonshine ("body") with softened water to 40–45%, bottle in glass, seal tightly and rest for at least 5 days in a cool place. Shelf life is unlimited.
💡 Cucumber moonshine is essentially a flavoured sugar spirit: do not expect a strong cucumber profile. It shines as a base for light tinctures and as a clean, soft drink for cold snacks.
