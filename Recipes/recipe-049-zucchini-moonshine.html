🥒 Zucchini Moonshine (Vegetable Marrow Distillate)

Zucchini moonshine has a barely perceptible grassy aroma with watermelon-rind tones, and a taste remembered for its vegetable softness — without a harsh alcohol profile. Since the sugar content of the fruit is very low, the result is effectively an unusually refined sugar moonshine.

Category
Fruit
ABV
40–45%
Difficulty
Medium
Fermentation
3–12 days
Yield
~4 kg zucchini
⚠️ Legal & Safety Disclaimer: This recipe is provided for educational purposes only. Distilling alcohol at home is illegal in many countries without a proper licence. Always check your local laws. Read our Distillation Safety Guide before attempting any distillation.

Choosing the Ingredients

Zucchini

Ripe or overripe zucchini of any variety are required. Unripe fruit传递 a weak organoleptic profile and contain more pectin, from which yeast produces methanol — this intensifies the hangover.

Yield: sugar content of zucchini is 1.5–3%. Some varieties grown in warm climates (Kavili, Sangrum, Aral, Aeronaut and similar) accumulate up to 5% sugar. Theoretically 1 kg of raw material gives 10–36 ml of 40° moonshine, but up to 20% of alcohol is lost during the two distillations.

Sugar, Water, Acid, Yeast

  • Sugar: 1 kg adds 1.2 L of 40° distillate, but white beet sugar worsens zucchini moonshine with kvass tones, and brown cane sugar with molasses and caramel notes, so it is desirable to replace sugar in the same proportion with fructose or dextrose (powdered glucose). Empirically, up to 250 g of sugar per 1 kg of zucchini can be added — beyond that the organoleptics are lost against the ethyl alcohol background.
  • Water: zucchini are 93–95% liquid, but it sits in the intercellular space, so the pulp releases juice poorly and thick wort ferments slowly and is prone to pathogenic infection. The non-boiled part of the water must be non-chlorinated (chlorine suppresses yeast cells): after reverse-osmosis filters, from columns, wells, etc. Tap water may be stood for 3 days in an open bucket or pot to evaporate the chlorine.
  • Citric acid: the natural acidity of zucchini is insufficient for normal yeast development. Concentrated citric acid stabilizes acidity, accelerating fermentation and reducing the risk of wash infection.
  • Yeast: because zucchini are thoroughly washed before processing (removing wild yeast from the skin), another source is required. Theoretically wine yeast (universal or for white grape wines) or a homemade raisin/berry starter can be used, but a 50-day fermentation is not justified: aroma and taste improve little. Spirit yeast ("Turbo" or regular) suits zucchini wash best. Dry or pressed baker's yeast is also acceptable: it adds kvass tones, but this problem is solved by competent fractional distillation.

Ingredients

  • Zucchini — 4 kg (whole weight)
  • Sugar — 1 kg (250 g per 1 kg of fruit)
  • Water — 5 L (1.25 L per 1 kg of zucchini)
  • Citric acid — 50 g (10 g per 1 L of water)
  • Yeast — for 10 L of wort

Baker's yeast dosage: 20 g dry or 100 g pressed per 1 kg of sugar. Spirit and wine yeast are added per the package instructions depending on wash volume. Exceeding these dosages will not harm the moonshine but will not speed up fermentation either.

Step-by-Step Process

1
Prepare the fruit. Wash the zucchini clean of soil residue, cut off the stems ("tails") and the blossom end (opposite tips). Halve the fruit, remove the fibers and seeds, and cut out blackened, rotten and moldy areas.
2
Grind the pulp. Grind the pulp together with the skin to a paste-like mass using a grater, meat grinder or blender, then place it in an enamel or stainless vessel with a wide mouth — a bucket or pot.
3
Boil the syrup. Boil 75% of the water, add all the sugar and citric acid, stir, and cook for 5 minutes on low heat, skimming the foam.
4
Combine and steep. Pour the boiling sugar syrup into the zucchini mass, stir to a homogeneous consistency, cover with a lid and leave for 8 hours.
5
Activate the yeast. Activate the yeast 20 minutes before pitching. Spirit or wine yeast per the package instructions. Pressed baker's yeast can be added straight from the refrigerator, kneaded with clean hands. Dry baker's yeast: scatter over the surface of sugar syrup (10 ml water and 1 g sugar per 1 g yeast) at 28–30 °C, stir after 5 minutes to remove lumps, and leave open for 10–15 minutes in a dark place at room temperature until stable foam appears.
6
Fill the fermenter. Transfer the wort to the fermentation vessel. Add the remaining 25% of non-boiled water and stir. "Raw" water contains the oxygen yeast need for reproduction at the initial stage. Total fill — up to 80%, with free space reserved for foam and carbon dioxide.
7
Pitch and ferment. Add the activated yeast to the wort. Close the vessel with an airlock and leave the wash in a dark place (or cover with dense fabric) at a stable 20–28 °C.
8
Monitor the cap. Check the vessel at least every 8–10 hours for the first 3 days so that pulp and skin residue does not clog the airlock outlet tube. Open and stir the wash as needed, breaking up the dense top layer of pomace.
9
Confirm completion. Depending on yeast and temperature, zucchini wash ferments 3–12 days on baker's or spirit yeast, and up to 50 days on wine yeast. Signs of a finished process: the airlock bubbles fewer than 5 times per minute or the glove deflates; foam has disappeared from the surface (sometimes areas up to 3 cm in diameter float); the wort has lightened and a layer of loose sediment has accumulated at the bottom; the wash is not sweet to taste (yeast converted all the sugar).

Distillation

10
Filter. Drain the fermented zucchini wash off the sediment, then strain through several layers of cheesecloth or a fine sieve, guaranteed removing solid particles that could scorch from high temperature.
11
Assemble equipment. Assemble the apparatus as a simple distiller: column height up to 65 cm with no packing inside, all auxiliary units off, leaving only a coil or shell-and-tube condenser plus one bubble trap (if provided by the design). In factory units the suitable mode is called pot still.
12
First distillation. Pour the wash into the boiler, filling 30–75% of volume. Run the first distillation at maximum speed (cooler utilization power): the distillate may flow warm and cloudy, but necessarily without steam. End the process when the strength in the stream drops below 25%. Running almost to zero strength is acceptable if the extra time, cooling water and energy costs seem justified by a small increase in quantity.
13
Calculate. Measure the strength of the obtained raw spirit at 20 °C, or correct the reading for another temperature in a moonshine calculator. Calculate the amount of absolute (pure) alcohol: volume in liters multiplied by strength in percent, divided by 100. Dilute the distillate with water to 18–20%. Do not clean with charcoal or other methods, preserving the organoleptics.
14
Second distillation — heads. Make the second distillation: after boiling, reduce the heating power so the zucchini moonshine flows in small drops and is cold, almost like the feed water. Collect the first 12% of output from the amount of absolute alcohol in a separate vessel. This is the "head" — the initial fraction with a high content of harmful impurities, unsuitable for drinking.
15
Collect the hearts. Smoothly raise the power, achieving fast drops or a thin stream, but the drink must remain cold. Collect the drinking part ("body") until the strength in the stream drops below 43%. Then switch off the apparatus or accumulate "tails" separately for future rectification on a column.
16
Dilute and rest. Dilute the zucchini moonshine ("body") with softened water to 40–45%, pour into glass bottles or jars, close tightly and leave for at least 5 days in a cool place to stabilize. Shelf life is unlimited.
💡 Editor's tip: To preserve the grassy zucchini organoleptics, reassemble the column in pot still mode for the second run and follow this recipe. The column can be filled with any copper packing for efficient removal of sulfur compounds; stainless packing only slows the distillation without giving any result.
