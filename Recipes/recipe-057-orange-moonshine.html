🍊 Orange Moonshine

A persistent citrus aroma, a soft taste and a light candied-fruit aftertaste; with bottle aging the aroma gains light vanilla notes. A good way to process non-standard fruit that would otherwise spoil. The intensity of the citrus note can be adjusted during wash preparation.

Category
Fruit
ABV
40–45%
Difficulty
Medium
Fermentation
4–50 days
Yield
up to 60 ml/kg (40%)
⚠️ Educational purposes only. Home distillation is illegal in many countries without a licence. Check your local laws first.

Theory & Ingredients

Orange juice contains 8–10% sugar and about 2 kg of fruit are needed per litre of juice, so the maximum theoretical yield is 60 ml of 40° spirit per 1 kg of fruit — 10–15% less in practice. Sugar raises the yield (1 kg adds ~1.1 L) but must not overpower the citrus tones: 1–2 kg of sugar per 10 kg of raw material is optimal.

Wine yeast (preferably for white wines) or a homemade raisin starter gives the softest spirit without pronounced alcohol tones, but fermentation takes up to 50 days. Spirit or baker's yeast finishes in 4–10 days but the spirit is less aromatic and soft.

  • Oranges — 10 kg
  • Sugar — 1–2 kg (optional)
  • Water — 4 L per 1 kg of sugar
  • Yeast — for 10 L of wort: wine, spirit, starter, or 100 g pressed / 20 g dry baker's (choose one type only)

Wash Preparation

1
Wash the oranges in warm water and wipe dry to remove surface microbes. Peel the fruit; the more white pith you remove, the less bitterness in the spirit. Mince the peeled fruit or, better, press the juice with a juicer (juice guarantees no bitterness).
2
Place the minced pulp (or the juice) into the fermentation vessel.
3
Dissolve the sugar in water (optionally warmed to 35–45 °C) and mix the syrup with the orange wort. Fill the vessel to max 75%, leaving space for foam and CO₂.
4
Activate the yeast per the package and pitch it into the wort. Close with any airlock (or a medical glove with a pinhole in one finger).
5
Move the wash to a dark place at 20–28 °C and leave until fermentation ends. Depending on yeast, fermentation lasts 4–50 days. Readiness signs: no gas from the airlock (deflated glove), a slightly bitter taste without sweetness, and a sediment layer at the bottom.

Distillation

6
A juice wash can simply be drained off the sediment into the boiler; a pulp wash must first be strained through 1–2 layers of cheesecloth and pressed dry.
7
Run the first distillation at maximum speed, collecting until the stream strength falls below 30%.
8
Measure the strength and calculate the pure alcohol: volume in litres × strength % ÷ 100. Dilute to 18–20%.
9
Second run: collect the first 18–20% of the pure-alcohol amount separately as "heads" — a harmful fraction. To intensify the orange aroma, hang orange peel or zest (peel without white pith) in the boiler in a gauze bag or place it in the bubble trap; zest is preferable because it gives no bitterness. Measure: peel from half a medium orange per 5 L of raw spirit, or less.
10
Collect the "body" until the stream strength falls below 45%, then stop or collect "tails" separately.
11
Dilute the orange moonshine to the desired drinking strength (usually 40–45%), bottle in glass, seal tightly and rest for at least 3–4 days in a cool place before tasting.
💡 Do not overdo the zest on the second run: an excess makes the aroma too strong and intrusive. Start with less — you can always aromatize a portion later.
