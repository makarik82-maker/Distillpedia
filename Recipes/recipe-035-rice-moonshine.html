🍚 Classic Rice Moonshine (with Malt Saccharification)

Properly made rice moonshine has a neutral aroma without fusel oil shades, as well as a soft taste with nutty and creamy aftertones that are absent in distillates from other grain crops. This recipe covers the classic rice wash preparation method (hot saccharification with malt) and distillation technology that preserves the organoleptic properties of the drink.

Category
Grain
ABV
40–45%
Difficulty
Advanced
Fermentation
3–10 days
Yield
~4 kg rice + 1 kg malt
⚠️ Legal & Safety Disclaimer: This recipe is provided for educational purposes only. Distilling alcohol at home is illegal in many countries without a licence. Always check your local laws. Read our Distillation Safety Guide before attempting any distillation.

Important Note: Rice Moonshine ≠ Sake

Rice moonshine is NOT sake — the Japanese alcoholic beverage, which in production technology is close to wash, but set on specific Koji mould fungi. True Koji (strain Aspergillus oryzae) have no relation to Chinese "Kodzi Angel", containing mould Rhizopus oryzae.

Alternative methods of preparing rice wash exist: on "Kodzi Angel" yeast and using enzymes "A" and "G", however traditional is considered hot saccharification. Advantages and disadvantages of each approach are described by links.

Choosing the Right Ingredients

Rice

Any types and varieties of rice are suitable for moonshine, regardless of color and degree of processing (polishing). You can use substandard grain: expired, pest-damaged, mouldy, etc. Thermal treatment eliminates all disadvantages.

Yield: Rice moonshine yield depends on starch content in grain, malt enzyme activity, and saccharification process efficiency. Maximum possible theoretical amount is 1.3 liters of 40-degree moonshine from 1 kg rice (excluding malt). In practice, part of the alcohol will be lost during double fractional distillation, so realistic forecast is about 1.1 liters at 40% strength.

Malt

Malt is sprouted grain in which enzymes are activated, capable of breaking down starch in rice into simple sugars. In moonshine production, barley malt is usually used, as its enzymes are the most effective. Malt can be sprouted at home or purchased ready-made in stores for amateur distillers.

Optimal proportion is 4:1 (for four parts rice add 1 kg malt). With this ratio, enzymes will definitely be enough for saccharification, and rice tones of moonshine won't be "smeared" by barley ones. If malt is high quality (has high diastatic power), for example intended for brewing, you can use proportion 6:1 or 5:1, but there's a risk that some starch still won't be converted to sugar. Malt itself increases moonshine amount — about 1 liter at 40% strength additionally comes from 1 kg.

Warning: For saccharification, non-fermented malt is required (also called "white" or "base"). Rye fermented malt ("red"), sold in culinary departments, is a flavor additive and cannot break down starch into simple sugars, therefore is not suitable.

Ingredients

  • Rice — 4 kg
  • Malt (barley non-fermented) — 1 kg (250 g per 1 kg rice)
  • Water — 20 L (4 L per 1 kg rice and malt)
  • Baker's yeast — 50 g dry or 250 g pressed (10 g or 50 g per 1 kg raw material)

Spirit yeast should be added according to manufacturer's instructions depending on wash volume after saccharification.

🛑 Important: During wash preparation, it is very important to accurately control saccharification temperature, otherwise nothing will work, so a thermometer is mandatory. Always discard the "heads" fraction during distillation — 10% in this recipe. See Distillation Safety Guide.

Step-by-Step Process

1
Heat water. Heat 15 L water (75% of recipe amount) in enameled or stainless steel pot to 80 °C. It's desirable to grind whole rice into coarse groats so cooking takes less time.
2
Add rice. Add rice to hot water. Fill pot maximum 60%, leaving room for malt and water.
3
Cook the rice. Bring to boil, stirring periodically. Reduce heating power to medium, cook 60-90 minutes until homogeneous "porridge". It's important to regularly stir pot contents near bottom and walls so rice doesn't burn, otherwise strong bitterness will appear in moonshine.
4
Cool and add remaining water. Cool must to 75 °C, pour in remaining 5 L water (25% of recipe volume). Stir.
5
Add malt. Add ground barley malt to rice. Stir again so malt distributes evenly throughout must volume.
6
Adjust temperature. By heating or cooling, adjust temperature to 65 °C (with 2-3 degree difference), cover pot with lid and turn off stove.
7
Maintain saccharification. For the next 90-120 minutes, maintain temperature in range 62-70 °C: every 15-20 minutes stir and take temperature measurements closer to bottom, slowly heating if necessary. At the end of saccharification, must should become sweet and liquid (rice remnants will collect at bottom). After 90 minutes you can do iodine test: pour half teaspoon of liquid must on plate with white bottom, add drop of iodine with pipette, stir with cotton swab or spoon, then wait 30 seconds. If liquid turns black or purple, continue saccharification for another 30 minutes, since not all starch is converted (darker color means more remains). If liquid doesn't change color, you can proceed to next stage. Warning: During saccharification, temperature must not go beyond 61-72 °C range, otherwise efficiency will drop dramatically, and above 73 °C enzymes will completely break down.
8
Mash-out. Slowly heat must to 75 °C, destroying remaining enzymes that can worsen moonshine aroma and taste.
9
Cool quickly. Turn off heat, cover pot with lid and cool must as quickly as possible to recommended yeast addition temperature — usually 25-30 °C. For this, you can place pot in bath with ice or cold water.
10
Activate yeast. Dilute yeast according to manufacturer's instructions. Dry baker's: sprinkle in even layer in jar with water (10 ml per 1 g yeast) at 28-30 °C, leave for 15 minutes, then stir and wait another 5 minutes — foam should appear. Pressed baker's yeast can be added directly from refrigerator, just knead with hands beforehand.
11
Transfer to fermentation vessel. Pour must from pot to fermentation vessel. Fill maximum 75% of volume, leaving room for carbon dioxide and foam. In this recipe, rice wash is set by red scheme (without filtering grain remnants). Alternative option is white scheme (fermentation of only liquid part). Pros and cons of each approach are described by link.
12
Add yeast and ferment. Add yeast, then install water lock. Leave wash in dark place (or cover) with temperature 20-28 °C. Depending on yeast and temperature, rice wash ferments for 3-10 days (usually 4-6). Process completion is determined by absence of gas from water lock and absence of foam on surface. Finished wash should smell like alcohol and be unsweet (yeast fermented all sugar).
13
Filter the wash. Drain wash from sediment, then filter through cheesecloth or fine kitchen sieve to guaranteed remove all grain remnants that can burn at high temperature.
14
Assemble equipment. Assemble moonshine apparatus or column in simple distiller mode: with minimal thump keg height and no packing, disable dephlegmator. Only direct-flow cooler and one thump keg (optional) should remain. In factory columns, this operating mode is called pot still.
15
First distillation. Pour rice wash into distillation pot, filling 30-75% of volume. Make first distillation at maximum power: moonshine can come out in stream, be warm and cloudy, but necessarily without steam. Collect drink until strength in stream drops below 25 degrees, or distill almost to zero alcohol content, if energy, cooling water, and time costs seem justified.
16
Measure and calculate. Measure distillate strength after first distillation at 20 °C. Calculate concentration of anhydrous (absolute) alcohol: strength in percentages multiplied by volume in liters and divided by one hundred. Temperature corrections and all calculations can be done in online moonshine calculator.
17
Dilute and clean (optional). Dilute moonshine with water to 18-20%. If desired, clean with wood activated charcoal, removing some harmful substances, but final drink taste will become closer to vodka — characteristic rice tones will disappear. Warning: Further distillation technology is designed for apparatuses with direct-flow cooler, while column operation instructions are by link. However, to preserve rice organoleptics, it's desirable to use column in pot still mode and follow methodology described here.
18
Second distillation. Reassemble equipment in simple distiller mode and make second distillation. After first distillation spirit boils in pot, adjust apparatus power and cooling water pressure so moonshine comes out in drops and is cold. First 10% of output from amount of pure alcohol (result of calculation at step 16) pour separately. This is the "head" — upper fraction with high content of harmful substances.
19
Collect the hearts. Collect drinking part ("body") until strength in stream drops to 40% (distillate doesn't burn in spoon). Finish distillation or collect "tails" in separate vessel to process them on mash or rectification column later.
20
Dilute and rest. Obtained rice moonshine ("body") dilute with softened water to drinking strength — 40-45%, transfer to glass bottles or jars, tightly close and leave in cool place for at least 3 days to stabilize taste. You can make one more cleaning with activated wood charcoal, removing almost all rice tones.
💡 Editor's tip: Rice moonshine is unique among grain distillates for its neutral aroma with nutty and creamy aftertones. The 4:1 rice-to-malt ratio ensures complete saccharification while preserving rice character. Using high-quality brewing malt allows ratios up to 6:1, but risks incomplete starch conversion.
