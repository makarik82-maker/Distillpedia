🌾 Barley Spirit with Green Malt

A grain distillate with a subtle fresh-grass aroma and soft bread-crumb flavour. Made using "green malt" (sprouted but undried barley), it is smoother than spirits made from traditional dried malt and is typically enjoyed without oak aging.

Category
Grain
ABV
38–45%
Difficulty
Advanced
Fermentation
3–10 days
Yield
~24 L mash
⚠️ Legal & Safety Disclaimer: This recipe is provided for educational purposes only. Distilling alcohol at home is illegal in many countries without a licence. Always check your local laws. Read our Distillation Safety Guide before attempting any distillation.

Ingredients

  • 6 kg barley grain — brewing, food-grade, or feed-grade
  • 24 L water — 4 L per 1 kg of grain
  • Yeast: spirit yeast or baker's yeast (10 g dry or 50 g fresh per 1 kg of grain)

At least 1 kg of the barley is sprouted to make green malt; the remaining 5 kg is used as raw grain. Minimum ratio: 250 g green malt per 1 kg raw grain.

🛑 Safety reminder: Always discard the "heads" fraction during the second distillation — it contains harmful volatile compounds. A thermometer is mandatory for saccharification. See Distillation Safety Guide.

Step-by-Step Process

Part 1: Making Green Malt

1
Soak the barley. Place 1 kg of barley (for malting) in a container. Cover with 35–40 °C water by 5–7 cm, stir, wait 10 minutes, then drain. Refill with fresh 10–16 °C water and leave for 12 hours.
2
Optional disinfection. Prepare a solution of 30 drops of iodine or 3 g of potassium permanganate per 10 L of cold water. Drain the soaking water, add the disinfecting solution, leave for 3 hours, then drain.
3
Sprout the grain. Spread the soaked barley in a 5–10 cm layer. Cover with damp cheesecloth. Place in a dark spot at 12–16 °C with good ventilation. Stir every 8–12 hours and mist with water if dry. Allow at least 15 cm of headroom — grain expands.
4
Check readiness. Sprouting takes 5–8 days. The malt is ready when the sprout reaches about 30% of the grain length (if all grain is being malted) or roughly the full grain length (if only part is malted).

Part 2: Saccharification (Starch to Sugar)

5
Make malt milk. Grind the green malt (sprouts included) through a meat grinder. Mix with water at 26–29 °C (4 L per 1 kg of malt) to create a liquid rich in active enzymes.
6
Cook the grain. Boil 4 L of water per 1 kg of raw grain. Crush the remaining 5 kg of barley into coarse grits (just crack the husks, don't grind fine). Stir the grits into the hot water in batches until smooth. Fill the vessel no more than 70% full.
7
Boil the porridge. Bring to a boil, reduce to medium heat, and cook for 60–120 minutes until you have a soft porridge. Stir every 10–15 minutes to prevent burning.
8
Add malt milk. Cool the porridge to 68 °C. Slowly pour in the malt milk while stirring vigorously. Turn off the heat and cover.
9
Maintain saccharification. Keep the temperature between 62–70 °C for 2 hours. Stir and check every 15 minutes. Critical: the temperature must never go outside 61–72 °C, or the enzymes will be destroyed. The wash is ready when it turns liquid and tastes sweet.
10
Mash-out and cool. Heat to 75 °C to stop enzymatic activity. Then cool as quickly as possible (under a closed lid) to 25–30 °C — the ideal temperature for yeast. Use an ice bath or cold water.

Part 3: Fermentation

11
Pitch the yeast. Prepare spirit yeast per package instructions, or activate baker's yeast in a sugar syrup at 30 °C until foamy. Transfer the cooled wash to a fermentation vessel (max 75% full), add yeast, and seal with an airlock.
12
Ferment. Keep in a dark place at 18–27 °C. Fermentation typically takes 3–10 days (usually 5–7). Signs of completion: fewer than 5 bubbles per minute from the airlock, a strong alcohol aroma, clarified wash with sediment, and no residual sweetness.

Part 4: Distillation

13
First distillation. Siphon the wash off the sediment and filter through a fine sieve or cheesecloth. Fill the still 30–70% full. Run a fast pot-still distillation. Stop when the ABV in the stream drops below 25%.
14
Measure and dilute. Measure the ABV at 20 °C and calculate the total pure alcohol. Dilute to 18–20% ABV. Optional: charcoal filtering is possible but will remove the soft grassy character.
15
Second distillation with cuts. Heat slowly so the distillate drips and stays cool. Collect the first 10% of pure alcohol volume as "heads" — discard this fraction, never consume it. Then increase heat and collect the "hearts" until the ABV in the stream drops below 41%.
16
Dilute and rest. Dilute the barley spirit with softened water to 38–45% ABV. Bottle in glass, seal tightly, and rest in a cool place for at least 5 days before tasting. Shelf life is unlimited.
💡 Editor's tip: A thermometer is non-negotiable for this recipe. Saccharification enzymes work in a narrow window (62–70 °C). Outside this range, you will lose fermentable sugars or destroy the enzymes entirely.
