Hawthorn Moonshine (Combined Fermentation Scheme)

Hawthorn moonshine reveals tones of dried apple, rosehip and pear in its aroma, while its slightly oily berry taste is remembered for apple notes that transition into unobtrusive astringency with a delicate bitterness. The wash preparation technology has several features, so it is important to follow the recipe exactly.

Category
Fruit
ABV
38–45%
Difficulty
Medium
Fermentation
4–50 days
Yield
~5 kg berries
⚠️ Legal & Safety Disclaimer: This recipe is provided for educational purposes only. Distilling alcohol at home is illegal in many countries without a proper licence. Always check your local laws. Read our Distillation Safety Guide before attempting any distillation.

Choosing the Ingredients

Hawthorn

Berries must be ripe: uniform bordeaux, red or orange color, easily removable from clusters. Unripe hawthorn contains very much pectin, from which yeast releases methanol. Poisoning with hawthorn moonshine is impossible even theoretically, but the hangover from improper distillation will be stronger.

The harvested crop should be washed and then left for 24 hours in the freezer so that negative temperature partially destroys cell walls and tannins, promoting juice separation from the pulp and reducing the bitter taste of the distillate. Dried or candied berries can be processed by the dried fruit recipe.

Hawthorn seeds contain amygdalin, which adds a pronounced roasted almond taste to the drink, so seeds must not be crushed — their hard shell must not be damaged. The enzymes that convert amygdalin into harmful hydrocyanic acid decompose from high temperature during distillation, so the moonshine is guaranteed safe, but the nutty bitterness remains.

Yield: hawthorn sugar content is 4–15% (average 7–9%). This means 1 kg of berries theoretically gives 48–180 ml of 40° moonshine, but in practice up to 20% of ethanol is lost during double fractional distillation. Garden large-fruited varieties are usually sweeter than small wild ones.

Sugar, Water, Yeast

  • Sugar (optional): 1 kg of beet or cane sugar gives an additional 1.2 L of distillate (40%), but any sugar worsens hawthorn moonshine with molasses and kvass tones — a compromise between quantity and quality must be found. Empirically, up to 200 g of sugar per 1 kg of hawthorn can be added (the sweeter the berries, the less). Replacing sugar with fructose or dextrose (powdered glucose) in the same amount is also desirable: these substances practically do not affect the aroma and taste, only "blurring" the organoleptics with a pure ethanol background.
  • Water: hawthorn gives almost no juice, and the berries are too acidic; thick wash of wrong acidity ferments slowly and is prone to pathogenic infection, so wort dilution is mandatory. Water for the wash must be non-chlorinated (chlorine suppresses yeast cells): after reverse-osmosis filters, from street machines, wells or columns. Tap water can also be stood for 3 days in an open pot or bucket to evaporate chlorine. Dilute the finished hawthorn moonshine (after the second fractional distillation) with softened water containing a minimum of heavy metal salts — bottled is best. About 40% of the distillate taste depends on water quality; boiled water lacks oxygen and microelements and is the least suitable for both wash and dilution.
  • Yeast or starter (optional): if the harvest was collected at least a day after rain in calm weather, fermentation can be started with "wild" yeast living on the fruit. However "wild" yeast on hawthorn is "weak" — it does not always activate quickly and ferment stably, and the berries cannot be washed, which is not recommended for fruit grown near roads and other dusty places. It is best to set hawthorn wash on wine yeast: for red juice or universal strains. A free alternative is a raisin or berry starter prepared 4–5 days before processing the raw material. Hawthorn wash on spirit yeast finishes in 4–8 days versus 30+ for any wine, but organoleptic properties will be worse since only wine strains produce complex esters during long fermentation. Special fruit-wash yeast is justified for unripe raw material as it contains enzymes that partially break down pectin; otherwise it equals ordinary spirit yeast. Dry or pressed baker's yeast spoils hawthorn moonshine with kvass and bread tones and is added only as a last resort.

Ingredients

  • Hawthorn — 5 kg of fresh or frozen berries
  • Sugar (optional) — up to 1 kg (max 200 g per 1 kg of hawthorn)
  • Water — 5 L (1 L per 1 kg of raw material), plus 4 L per 1 kg of sugar
  • Yeast (optional) — depending on wort volume

Wine and spirit yeast are added per the package instructions; baker's yeast requires 20 g dry or 100 g pressed per 1 kg of sugar (in berries + added). Natural sugar content can be assumed slightly above average — 10%. Exceeding the yeast amount will not affect the moonshine or speed up fermentation.

Step-by-Step Process

1
Sort the berries. Reject unripe, too small, rotten and moldy hawthorn. Remove stems and remnants of the flower bed (on the opposite end of the berries). If fermentation will run on wild yeast, do not wash the fruit; in other cases rinse with running water.
2
Crush gently. Mash the hawthorn by hand or with a wooden pestle without damaging the seeds, otherwise the moonshine will be bitter with a pronounced almond taste. There is no point in turning the berries into a paste — it is enough that the skin bursts. Place the hawthorn mass into an enamel, stainless or plastic vessel with a wide mouth — a bucket or pot.
3
Activate yeast. Activate yeast (if used) per the package instructions. Pressed baker's yeast can be added straight from the fridge, kneaded with clean hands.
4
Make the wash. Dissolve sugar in water. Pour the resulting syrup (or only water) into the hawthorn and stir to a homogeneous consistency. The wort must become liquid — easily stirrable by hand; add more water if necessary.
5
Start fermentation. Pitch yeast or starter (optional). Tie the vessel mouth with cheesecloth and leave it for 3 days in a dark room at a stable 18–26 °C. Stir every 10–12 hours, breaking and submerging the pomace — the dense layer of pulp and skin that accumulates on the surface. Signs of fermentation must appear within the first 36 hours: hissing, foam and a characteristic sour smell; if absent, find out why the wash is not fermenting and eliminate the cause urgently.
6
Strain (combined scheme). Strain the wort through cheesecloth or a fine sieve, squeezing the berries dry (no longer needed). The combined fermentation scheme extracts almost all useful substances from hawthorn while not oversaturating the wash with tannins and amygdalin, so the moonshine will not have strong bitterness or a harsh almond aftertaste.
7
Seal and ferment. Pour the filtered wort into the fermentation vessel, filling up to 75% of volume — free space is needed for foam and carbon dioxide. Install any airlock design and move to a dark place or cover with dense fabric.
8
Confirm completion. Depending on temperature and sugar amount, hawthorn wash on spirit or baker's yeast ferments 4–12 days; on wild or wine yeast — 30–50 days. Signs of a finished process: fewer than 5 bubbles per minute from the airlock; no foam left near the surface (sometimes areas up to 3 cm in diameter float); the wort has clarified with a layer of loose sediment at the bottom; the open vessel smells strongly of alcohol and the wash itself is not sweet (yeast converted all sugars).

Distillation

9
Filter. Drain the fermented wash off the sediment (preferably through a tube), then strain through cheesecloth or a fine sieve, guaranteed removing skin and pulp residues that could scorch.
10
Assemble equipment. Assemble the apparatus in simple distillation mode (pot still): column height up to 60 cm with no packing inside, all auxiliary units off, leaving only a direct-flow condenser (coil or shell-and-tube) plus one bubble trap if provided by the apparatus design.
11
First distillation. Pour the wash into the boiler, filling 30–70% of volume. Run the first distillation at maximum speed (cooler utilization power): the distillate may flow warm and cloudy, but necessarily without steam. Finish the process when the strength in the stream drops below 25%. Running almost to zero is acceptable if the extra boiling energy costs seem justified by a small increase in drink quantity.
12
Calculate. Determine the strength of the obtained raw spirit at 20 °C, or correct the value for another temperature in a moonshine calculator. Calculate the concentration of absolute (pure) alcohol: liters of volume multiplied by strength in percent, then divided by 100. Dilute the raw spirit with water to 18–20%. Do not clean with activated charcoal or other methods, preserving hawthorn tones.
13
Second distillation — heads. Distill a second time. When the liquid boils, reduce the heating power so the distillate flows in small drops and is cold, almost like the water fed into the system. Collect separately the first 15% from the amount of pure alcohol (determined at step 12). This is the "head" — the initial fraction with a high content of harmful substances, unsuitable for drinking.
14
Collect the hearts. Smoothly increase the heating power. The moonshine may drip fast or flow in a thin stream, but must remain cold. Collect the "body" (drinking part) until the strength in the stream drops below 45%, then finish the distillation or accumulate "tails" separately for future rectification on a column.
15
Dilute and rest. Dilute the finished hawthorn moonshine ("body") with softened water to the desired drinking strength — 38–45%. Pour into glass containers, close tightly and leave for at least 5 days in a cool place to stabilize. Shelf life is unlimited.
💡 Editor's tip: To preserve hawthorn organoleptics, reassemble the equipment in simple distiller mode for the second run and follow this recipe. You can add several sections of any copper packing (SPN, RPN) to the column for more efficient removal of sulfur compounds; stainless packing gives no result and only slows the distillation.
