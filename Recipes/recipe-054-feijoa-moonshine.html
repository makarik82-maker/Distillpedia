🍈 Feijoa Moonshine

A delicate aroma with tones of pear, wild strawberry, fresh hay and citrus zest; a soft fruit taste with green apple, kiwi and a barely perceptible astringency on the finish.

Category
Fruit
ABV
37–45%
Difficulty
Medium
Fermentation
4–45 days
Yield
120–192 ml/kg (40%)
⚠️ Educational purposes only. Home distillation is illegal in many countries without a licence. Check your local laws first.

Choosing the Ingredients

Feijoa

Any ripe feijoa works: bruised, undersized, long-stored, etc. Unripe berries contain more pectin, which yeast converts into methanol — the spirit stays safe but the hangover gets heavier, so keep hard berries at room temperature for 3–5 days to ripen fully.

Yield. Sugar content of feijoa is 10–16%, so 1 kg theoretically gives 120–192 ml of 40° spirit; the practical yield is 15–20% lower due to losses in two fractional distillations.

Sugar, water, acid, yeast

  • Sugar (optional): 1 kg adds 1.2 L of 40° spirit but dulls the fruit profile. Empirically, up to 200 g per 1 kg of feijoa is acceptable; fructose or dextrose in the same amount affect the distillate less.
  • Water: non-chlorinated (chlorine suppresses yeast): reverse-osmosis, well, spring, or tap water stood for 3 days in an open vessel. Soften the water used for final dilution.
  • Citric acid: feijoa is low in natural acids; 7 g per 1 L of water stabilises fermentation and reduces infection risk without affecting the finished spirit.
  • Yeast: cultured wine yeast (universal or for white grape juice) is optimal; a berry or raisin starter set 4–5 days ahead is a free alternative. Spirit yeast finishes in 4–7 days but the aroma is poorer; baker's yeast adds bread and kvass tones and is a last resort.

Ingredients

  • Feijoa — 5 kg
  • Sugar (optional) — up to 1 kg (200 g per 1 kg of fruit)
  • Water — 10 L (2 L per 1 kg of feijoa), plus 4 L per 1 kg of sugar
  • Citric acid — 7 g per 1 L of water
  • Yeast or starter — for 15 L of wort (baker's: 20 g dry or 100 g pressed per 1 kg of total sugar; assume 14% natural sugar if unknown)

Wash Preparation

1
Sort the feijoa, discarding rotten and mouldy berries (small problem spots can be cut off). Wash the berries in running water and mince them together with the skin (it gives the characteristic aroma and a pleasant bitterness) until homogeneous; place the mass into the fermentation vessel.
2
Dissolve the citric acid and the sugar (optional) in the water. Pour the solution into the feijoa mass and stir until homogeneous. The wort must be liquid — freely stirrable by hand; add more water if needed. Fill the vessel to max 75%, leaving space for foam and CO₂.
3
Activate the yeast per the package instructions (or use the universal method) and pitch it into the wort.
4
Fit an airlock of any design. Leave the wash in a dark place (or covered) at 18–28 °C. Fermentation must start within 36 hours: foam on the surface and hissing. If not, find and fix the cause urgently — usually the yeast is at fault.
5
For the first 3–4 days check the wash every 6–10 hours so the floating pulp does not block the airlock tube. Open and stir as needed, submerging the dense cap. Over time all pulp settles and stops floating.
6
Fermentation lasts 4–45 days depending on yeast, sugar and temperature. It is finished when: the airlock bubbles fewer than 5 times per minute (or the glove deflates); foam has disappeared (small patches up to 3 cm may float); the wort has lightened with a loose sediment layer; the wash smells of alcohol and is not sweet.
7
Rack the finished wash off the sediment (a thin tube works best), then strain through a fine sieve or cheesecloth to remove solids that could scorch during distillation.

Distillation

8
Assemble the equipment as a simple pot still: column up to 60 cm with no packing, dephlegmator and auxiliary units off, only a direct-flow condenser (coil or shell-and-tube) and one bubble trap if the design has one. Factory columns: pot still mode.
9
Fill the boiler to 30–75% and run the first distillation at maximum speed: the distillate may run warm and cloudy but never steamy. Stop when the stream strength falls below 20%. Running almost "to water" is acceptable if the extra energy and time seem worth the small gain.
10
Measure the strength at 20 °C (or correct for temperature in a calculator) and calculate the pure alcohol: volume (L) × strength (%) ÷ 100. Dilute to 18–20%. Do not clean with charcoal or other methods, or the tropical notes will be lost.
11
Second run: after boiling, reduce power so the distillate drips slowly and stays cold, almost like the cooling water. Collect the first 12–15% of the pure-alcohol amount separately as "heads" — a harmful fraction fit only for technical use.
12
Raise power smoothly to fast drops or a thin stream, keeping the spirit cold. Collect the "body" until the stream strength falls below 43%, then stop or collect "tails" separately for future rectification.
13
Dilute the feijoa moonshine ("body") with softened water to the desired drinking strength — usually 37–45%. Bottle in glass, seal tightly and rest for at least 5 days in a cool place. Shelf life is unlimited.
💡 To preserve the unique feijoa profile, keep both runs in pot-still mode and add one section of copper packing (SPN/RPN) on the second run to remove sulfur compounds; stainless packing only slows the run.
