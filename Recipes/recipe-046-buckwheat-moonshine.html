🌾 Buckwheat Moonshine (Buckwheat Whiskey Base)

Buckwheat moonshine has a slightly oily texture and a pronounced aroma of toasted crust, while its rough bread taste reveals a light bitterness of buckwheat honey with a nutty note. Barrel aging or infusion on oak wood (chips) smooths the roughness and ennobles this distillate with vanilla, chocolate and caramel tones — the result is homemade buckwheat whiskey.

Category
Grain
ABV
38–45%
Difficulty
Advanced
Fermentation
3–10 days
Yield
~4 kg buckwheat + 1 kg malt
⚠️ Legal & Safety Disclaimer: This recipe is provided for educational purposes only. Distilling alcohol at home is illegal in many countries without a proper licence. Always check your local laws. Read our Distillation Safety Guide before attempting any distillation.

Choosing the Ingredients

Buckwheat

Any condition is acceptable: whole (yadritsa), cracked (prodel), expired, pest-damaged, etc. — but necessarily with a pleasant aroma, without rancidity, mold or rot smell. Buckwheat grains contain a high concentration of lipids (plant fats), so any foreign aromas are guaranteed to enter the drink.

The organoleptic properties depend on the roast degree of the grains:

  • Brown buckwheat: aromatic but rougher distillate with a pronounced bread aftertaste; recommended as the base for buckwheat whiskey.
  • Green buckwheat: soft moonshine with herbal and fruit notes; barrel or wood aging is not required.

Yield: buckwheat contains 60–64% starch, so theoretically 1 kg of raw material gives 1.1–1.15 L of 40° moonshine, but in practice full saccharification of all starch is unlikely, and up to 20% of alcohol is lost during the two distillations. The real amount is about 0.8 L of 40° distillate; malt also increases the yield — 1 kg adds 0.6–0.9 L.

Malt, Water, Yeast

  • Malt: only non-fermented malt ("base" or "white") is suitable for saccharification. Barley malt is desirable for brown buckwheat; wheat malt pairs well with green buckwheat. Recommended ratio 4:1 (1 kg malt per 4 kg buckwheat) — enzymes will definitely be enough, and harsh buckwheat tones will be smoothed by barley or wheat ones. Homemade malt can be taken 30–40% more since its enzyme activity is lower than store-bought. Fermented rye "red" malt from grocery stores is only a flavor additive and cannot convert starch.
  • Water: any drinking water is fine, but the non-boiled part must be chlorine-free (chlorine suppresses yeast); let tap water stand 3 days in an open bucket. Dilute the finished buckwheat moonshine (after the second distillation) with softened water — bottled, reverse-osmosis filtered, from street machines or wells. Boiled water spoils the taste due to missing microelements.
  • Yeast: ordinary spirit yeast (without the "Turbo" prefix) is optimal — buckwheat wort is nutritious enough and needs no feeding, and too intensive fermentation prevents yeast from releasing the complex esters that improve organoleptics. Dry or pressed baker's yeast is also acceptable: competent double distillation removes the kvass tones these strains bring. Wine and beer yeast are not suitable — they are meant for other drinks.

Ingredients

  • Buckwheat (yadritsa or prodel) — 4 kg
  • Malt — 1 kg (250 g per 1 kg of groats)
  • Water — 25 L (5 L per 1 kg of buckwheat and malt)
  • Yeast — spirit or baker's

Spirit yeast is added per the package instructions depending on wort volume. Baker's dosage: 10 g dry or 50 g pressed per 1 kg of grain raw material (buckwheat + malt).

🛑 Saccharification Temperature Rule: the temperature must not exceed 72 °C even for a short time, otherwise enzymes are irreversibly destroyed. A drop below 60 °C slows saccharification several times. When measuring, account for thermometer error and keep away from the range borders. A thermometer is mandatory.

Step-by-Step Process

1
Prepare the grain. Grind the malt to coarse grits (if required). Buckwheat can be left uncrushed — this slightly increases cooking time but keeps the wash from becoming too thick, simplifying work.
2
Cook the porridge. Heat 80% of the water (20 L per recipe) to 70 °C. Add all the buckwheat and stir to a homogeneous consistency. Bring to a boil, then cook on medium heat for 25–45 minutes to a homogeneous porridge, stirring periodically near the walls and bottom to avoid scorching. Break up appearing lumps (they reduce saccharification efficiency).
3
Add the malt. Cool the wort to 65 °C. Add the malt in several portions, constantly stirring so enzymes distribute evenly through the whole wort volume. Turn off the stove and cover the pot with a lid.
4
Maintain saccharification. Keep the 61–68 °C range for 2 hours: stir and check the temperature near the bottom every 15 minutes, switching on weak heat if necessary. Toward the end the wort must become liquid and necessarily sweetish (starch successfully converted to sugar). Remaining starch can be checked with an iodine test.
5
Stop enzymes and cool. Heat the wort to 80 °C, destroying pathogenic microflora and deactivating remaining enzymes (they can worsen buckwheat moonshine). Turn off the stove, pour in the remaining 20% of cold non-boiled water (needed as an oxygen source for yeast reproduction at the initial stage) and stir. Then cool the wort as fast as possible to below 30 °C — before vinegar bacteria and other microorganisms activate — for example by surrounding the pot with ice or immersing it in a cold water bath.
6
Activate yeast. Dilute spirit yeast per the manufacturer's instructions. Pressed baker's yeast can be added straight from the fridge, kneaded with clean hands. Dry baker's yeast: scatter over the surface of sugar syrup (10 ml water and 1 g sugar per 1 g yeast) at 28–30 °C, wait 5 minutes, stir, and keep in a dark place for 10–15 minutes until stable foam appears, without covering.
7
Fill and pitch. Pour the wort into the fermentation vessel, filling a maximum of 75% of volume so enough free space remains for foam (buckwheat foams heavily due to proteins) and carbon dioxide. Add the diluted yeast and close the vessel with an airlock. Leave to ferment in a dark room (or cover with dense fabric) at 18–27 °C.
8
Confirm completion. Depending on yeast and temperature, buckwheat wash ferments 3–10 days. Signs of a finished process: fewer than 5 bubbles per minute from the airlock (glove deflated); almost no foam left on the surface; a layer of loose sediment visible at the bottom; the wash smells of alcohol and is not sweet (all sugar converted by yeast).

Distillation

9
Filter. Drain the fermented wash off the sediment, then strain through cheesecloth or a fine sieve, guaranteed removing solid particles that could scorch from high temperature and spoil the moonshine with pronounced bitterness. Thick washes can be distilled only by steam or in a water bath; in all other cases filtration is mandatory.
10
Assemble equipment. Set up the equipment in simple distiller mode: column height up to 65 cm with no packing, auxiliary units off, leaving only a coil or shell-and-tube condenser and one bubble trap (if provided by design). In factory apparatus the suitable mode is called pot still.
11
First distillation. Pour the buckwheat wash into the boiler, filling 30–70% of volume. Distill at maximum speed: moonshine may flow warm and cloudy, but necessarily without steam. Finish the process when the strength in the stream drops below 25°. Running "to water" is acceptable if the extra energy and time costs seem justified by a small increase in quantity.
12
Calculate. Measure the distillate strength at 20 °C, or correct the value for another temperature in a moonshine calculator. Determine the amount of pure alcohol: strength in percent multiplied by volume in liters, divided by 100. Dilute the raw spirit with water to 18–20%. Charcoal cleaning is optional: it removes some fusel oils, but activated carbon molecules reduce the intensity of buckwheat organoleptics, making the drink vodka-like.
13
Second distillation — heads. Distill a second time: when the liquid boils, reduce the stove power so the moonshine flows in small drops and is cold, almost like the water fed into the apparatus. Collect separately the first 10% of distillate from the amount of absolute alcohol (calculated at step 12). This is the "head" — a fraction with a high content of harmful impurities, unsuitable for drinking.
14
Collect the hearts. Smoothly raise the heating power, achieving "fast" drops or a thin stream, but the moonshine must remain cold. Collect the "body" (drinking part) up to a stream strength of 44%. Then switch off the equipment or accumulate "tails" separately for future rectification on a column.
15
Dilute, rest or age. If wood ennobling is not planned, dilute the buckwheat moonshine ("body") with softened water to drinking strength — 38–45%. Pour into glass containers, close tightly and leave in a cool place for at least 5 days to stabilize. Shelf life is unlimited. For buckwheat whiskey, pour the distillate into barrels or infuse on oak chips for several months.
💡 Editor's tip: To preserve buckwheat tones, reassemble the equipment in pot still mode for the second run and follow this recipe. You can also place several sections of copper SPN or RPN packing in the column for better removal of sulfur compounds; stainless packing only slows the distillation without giving any effect.
