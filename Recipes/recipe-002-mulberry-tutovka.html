🫐 Mulberry Brandy (Caucasian Tutovka)

Tutovka is a traditional Caucasian spirit from Armenia, Georgia, and Azerbaijan, distilled from fermented mulberries. With its soft fruit character, grassy undertones, and subtle astringency, it rivals good brandy or whisky. Rarely sold commercially, it is traditionally made at home.

Category
Fruit
ABV
38–65%
Difficulty
Advanced
Fermentation
4–45 days
Yield
~20 L mash
⚠️ Legal & Safety Disclaimer: This recipe is provided for educational purposes only. Distilling alcohol at home is illegal in many countries without a licence. Always check your local laws. Read our Distillation Safety Guide before attempting any distillation.

Ingredients

  • 10 kg mulberries — ripe, fresh, not sour or fermented
  • 10 L water — chlorine-free, non-boiled (1 L per 1 kg of fruit)
  • Sugar (optional, not recommended): up to 1.5 kg (150 g per 1 kg of fruit)
  • Yeast (optional): wine yeast preferred; wild fermentation is traditional

Traditional Caucasian tutovka is made without added sugar — only the natural fruit sugars are fermented.

🛑 Safety reminder: Unripe mulberries contain more pectin, which yeast converts into methanol. Use only ripe fruit, and always discard the "heads" fraction during distillation. See Distillation Safety Guide.

Step-by-Step Process

1
Sort the berries. Remove leaves, stems, and discard any blackened, mouldy, rotten, or unripe berries. If fermenting on wild yeast, do not wash the berries. Process within 24 hours of harvest for best results.
2
Mash by hand. Crush the mulberries with your hands. Do not use a blender or meat grinder — crushed seeds release an almond-like flavour that can spoil the distillate.
3
Prepare the wash. Transfer the mashed berries to a wide-mouth fermentation vessel. Heat water to 28–30 °C (no higher), dissolve sugar if using, and pour into the fruit. Stir to a smooth consistency. The wash should be liquid enough to stir freely. Fill the vessel no more than 80% full.
4
Add yeast or rely on wild fermentation. If using wine yeast, prepare according to package instructions and add. For wild fermentation, skip this step. Seal with an airlock and place in a dark spot at 18–28 °C.
5
Stir during early fermentation. For the first 3–4 days, remove the airlock and stir every 8–10 hours, pushing floating fruit pulp back into the liquid. Once the pulp stops rising to the surface, leave the wash undisturbed.
6
Confirm fermentation is complete. Signs: airlock bubbling stops (fewer than 5 bubbles per minute), foam disappears, the wash clarifies with sediment at the bottom, and it tastes dry with a noticeable alcohol aroma. This takes 4 days on spirit yeast, or up to 45 days on wine/wild yeast.
7
First distillation. Siphon the wash off the sediment and filter through cheesecloth to remove fruit solids (which can burn and add bitterness). Fill the still 30–75% of capacity. Run a fast pot-still distillation. Stop when the ABV in the stream drops below 27%.
8
Measure and dilute. Measure the ABV at 20 °C and calculate the total pure alcohol. Dilute the distillate to 18–20% ABV with water. Do not filter with charcoal — this strips the fruit aroma.
9
Second distillation with cuts. Heat slowly so the distillate drips and stays cool. Collect the first 12–14% of pure alcohol volume as "heads" — discard this fraction, never consume it. Then increase heat slightly and collect the "hearts" until the ABV in the stream drops below 43%.
10
Dilute and rest. If not aging in oak, dilute with softened water to 38–45% ABV. Bottle in glass, seal, and rest in a cool place for at least 3 days to stabilise the flavour.
💡 Editor's tip: For a barrel-aged version, rest the distillate in an oak barrel for 3–18 months, or infuse on toasted mulberry or oak chips for up to 100 days, tasting weekly to avoid over-extraction.

Choosing the Right Mulberries

  • Colour: Black mulberries give the richest body; white are the sweetest. Avoid mixing colours in one batch — blend distillates instead.
  • Ripeness: Use only fully ripe berries. Underripe fruit is high in pectin, which increases methanol production.
  • Freshness: Mulberries spoil fast. Start fermentation within 24 hours of harvest for the best quality.
