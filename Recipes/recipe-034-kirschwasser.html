🍒 Homemade Kirschwasser (German Cherry Moonshine)

In Germany, cherry moonshine is called Kirschwasser (literally "cherry water") and is valued for its soft berry taste with pronounced almond tones, which appear due to fermentation with pits. With sufficient raw material (at least 5 kg), you can prepare Kirschwasser at home. This recipe covers the universal cherry wash and proper distillation technology that preserves the organoleptic properties of the berries.

Category
Fruit
ABV
40–45%
Difficulty
Advanced
Fermentation
5–45 days
Yield
~12 kg cherries
⚠️ Legal & Safety Disclaimer: This recipe is provided for educational purposes only. Distilling alcohol at home is illegal in many countries without a licence. Always check your local laws. Read our Distillation Safety Guide before attempting any distillation.

Choosing the Right Cherries

Any ripe cherries of any variety and condition are suitable for processing into moonshine. The juicier and sweeter, the easier it is to work with the wash and the higher the distillate yield. You can use overripe and crushed berries, but necessarily without rot, mould, and blackened skin, especially if fermentation will be on wine or "wild" yeast. Moonshine from frozen cherries is strongly shifted towards almond tones, but in general this recipe is acceptable for frozen berries.

Cherry pits contain amygdalin — an organic substance that adds a bitter almond taste to Kirschwasser. Amygdalin is converted into harmful hydrocyanic acid in the body, but during distillation of cherry wash, all enzymes responsible for amygdalin breakdown are neutralized by high temperature, so the moonshine is safe for health. Removing pits is advisable only if you need to remove almond tones and get a "clean" berry taste.

Yield: Depending on variety, growing region, and weather in a particular year, cherries contain 5-18% sugar. Therefore, from 1 kg of raw material you can make 60-216 ml of 40-degree moonshine. It's also important to consider that 14-20% of ethyl alcohol will be lost during double fractional distillation.

Ingredients

  • Cherries (fresh weight with pits) — 12 kg
  • Water — 1 L per 3 kg berries, plus 4 L per 1 kg sugar
  • Sugar (optional) — up to 200 g per 1 kg berries
  • Wine or spirit yeast (optional) — per instructions for wash volume

Baker's yeast requires 20 g dry or 100 g pressed per 1 kg sugar (sum in berries and added). Calculation can be approximate: increased yeast dosage won't harm, but won't speed up fermentation either.

🛑 Important: It is very important not to damage the pits (the hard shell must remain intact), otherwise the moonshine will be too bitter. Therefore, it is desirable to crush cherries manually. Always discard the "heads" fraction during distillation — 15% in this recipe. See Distillation Safety Guide.

Step-by-Step Process

1
Sort the berries. Remove stems and leaves, as well as blackened, rotten, and mouldy fruits. If the wash will be set on "wild" yeast, do not wash the cherries; in all other cases, it is desirable to rinse with running water.
2
Crush the cherries. Remove pits if almond tones like real Kirschwasser are not required, or simply crush each berry with your hands. Warning: It is very important not to damage the pits (the hard shell must remain intact), otherwise the moonshine will be too bitter. Therefore, it is desirable to crush cherries manually.
3
Place in fermentation vessel. Put the berry mass (with or without pits) in the fermentation vessel.
4
Activate yeast. Dilute yeast (if used) according to manufacturer's instructions.
5
Add water and sugar. Mix water and sugar. Pour syrup into cherry mass. Stir until consistency is homogeneous. The must should be liquid and freely stirrable (depends on berry juiciness), add more water if necessary, this will not affect the quality of the finished moonshine. Leave at least 25% of fermentation vessel volume free for foam and carbon dioxide.
6
Add yeast and seal. Add diluted yeast (optional). Install water lock of any design, then transfer wash to a dark room (can be covered) with temperature 18-28 °C. After 8-20 hours, fermentation signs should appear: foam, hissing, and gas release from water lock (or glove inflates).
7
Monitor early fermentation. For the first 3-5 days, carefully monitor the cherry wash: once a day, open the vessel and stir the pulp — floating particles of flesh and skin, so this "cap" does not interfere with carbon dioxide release. Over time, all pulp will sink to the bottom and will no longer float. Short contact of must with oxygen won't harm.
8
Confirm fermentation is complete. Depending on chosen yeast, sugar concentration, and temperature, cherry wash ferments for 5-45 days. You can proceed to the next stage when: gas stopped releasing from water lock (less than 5 bubbles per minute) or glove deflated; almost all foam disappeared from surface, sometimes small "islands" remain; a layer of loose sediment appeared at the bottom; no sweet taste (yeast converted all sugar) and alcohol smell is felt.
9
Filter the wash. Drain fermented wash from sediment through a tube, then strain through cheesecloth or kitchen sieve to guaranteed remove solid particles that can burn at high temperature.
10
Assemble equipment. Assemble moonshine apparatus or column in simple distiller mode: with minimal thump keg height and no packing inside, disable dephlegmator, leaving only direct-flow cooler (coil or shell-and-tube), as well as one thump keg (bubble separator), if this element is provided by design. In factory columns, the suitable operating mode is called pot still.
11
First distillation. Pour wash into distillation pot, filling 30-75% of volume, so enough liquid remains at the bottom after boiling, and steam zone forms. Distill wash first time at maximum speed: distillate can come out warm and cloudy, but necessarily without steam. Finish process after strength in stream drops below 25%, or distill almost to water (about zero alcohol content), if energy and time costs seem justified.
12
Measure and calculate. Measure strength of obtained distillate at 20 °C. Determine amount of pure (anhydrous) alcohol: volume in liters multiplied by strength in percentages and divided by one hundred. Do not clean with wood activated charcoal or other methods, otherwise delicate berry organoleptics will disappear. All calculations and temperature corrections can be done in online moonshine calculator.
13
Reassemble equipment. Reassemble equipment in simple distiller mode: maximum thump keg height 50-60 cm and only with direct-flow cooler (without dephlegmator), it's also desirable to disable thump keg (bubble separator). You can add one section of copper SPN or RPN packing to the thump keg for better removal of sulfur compounds. Stainless steel packing will be useless and will only reduce distillation speed.
14
Dilute and second distillation. Dilute first distillation moonshine with water to 18-20% and pour into distillation pot. After liquid boils, adjust supplied power and/or water pressure so moonshine comes out in small drops and is necessarily cold.
15
Collect the heads. First 15% of distillate from amount of pure alcohol (calculated at step 12) collect separately. This is the "head" — harmful fraction with sharp smell, which cannot be drunk.
16
Collect the hearts. Gradually increase power so moonshine starts dripping quickly or flowing in thin stream, but remains cold — like water temperature. Collect "body" (drinking fraction) until strength in stream drops below 45 degrees, then finish distillation or collect "tails" separately even "to water" for subsequent processing on column. Alternative methods of "heads" selection are described in a separate article.
17
Dilute and rest. Ready homemade Kirschwasser (cherry moonshine) dilute with softened drinking water to strength 40-45% ABV, transfer to glass bottles (jars), tightly close and leave in refrigerator or cellar for at least 3 days to stabilize taste.
💡 Editor's tip: The almond tones in Kirschwasser come from amygdalin in cherry pits. During distillation, high temperature neutralizes the enzymes that would convert amygdalin to harmful hydrocyanic acid, making the moonshine safe. However, if you prefer a pure cherry taste without almond notes, remove the pits before fermentation.
