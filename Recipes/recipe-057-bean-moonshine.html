🫘 Bean Moonshine

Barely perceptible pea notes in aroma and taste. Suitable neat or as a base for strong "male" tinctures (not fruit-based). A good way to process surplus beans, even substandard ones.

Category
Grain / Starch
ABV
40–45%
Difficulty
Advanced
Fermentation
4–28 days
Yield
up to 210 ml/kg (40%)
⚠️ Educational purposes only. Home distillation is illegal in many countries without a licence. Check your local laws first. Never taste a Koji-based wash.

Theory

Any bean variety works: white, red, etc. Slightly spoiled or even mouldy raw material is acceptable. Beans contain about 15% starch, so 1 kg theoretically yields up to 210 ml of 40° spirit; the practical yield is 10–15% lower.

Yeast cannot ferment pure starch, so the starch must first be split into simple sugars. Two methods exist: hot saccharification with malt (classic) and cold saccharification with Koji yeast. For beans the choice is not critical — this raw material has no unique notes worth preserving. Cold saccharification is simpler but needs Koji and a longer fermentation; hot saccharification needs malt and strict temperature control but is considered more natural, ferments faster, and the malt adds up to 800 ml of 40% spirit per 1 kg.

The protein factor. Beans contain about 8% protein. If not broken down, the wash foams heavily and can scorch during distillation. In cold saccharification the enzymes digest almost all protein themselves; in the hot method an extra step — the protein pause — destroys the protein compounds.

Adding sugar to increase yield is not recommended given the relative complexity of the wash. Any spirit or baker's yeast works for fermentation.

Ingredients

  • Beans — 5 kg
  • Water — 20–30 L
  • Koji yeast — 45 g (cold saccharification only)
  • Baker's or spirit yeast — for 25 L of wort (hot saccharification only)
  • Barley malt — 1.5 kg (hot saccharification only)

Method A: Koji Wash (Cold Saccharification)

1
Disinfect the fermentation vessel with steam, iodine (10 ml per 25 L of cold water, fill to the edges, leave 60 minutes) or any other method. Slow fermentation risks infection, so disinfection is mandatory; some distillers also add antibiotics to Koji washes.
2
Grind the beans into grits, or better into flour. Whole beans cannot be fermented — the starch will not split.
3
Boil 20 L of water and pour it over the bean grits/flour. This disinfects the raw material.
4
Wait until the wort cools to 30–32 °C, then pitch the Koji per the package instructions. At higher temperatures the yeast dies.
5
Transfer the wort to the fermentation vessel and fit an airlock (mandatory, otherwise the wash sours).
6
Move the wash to a dark place at a stable 20–28 °C (25–26 °C recommended). Stir once every 4–5 days so enzymes distribute evenly. Fermentation usually lasts 20–28 days; first signs appear 6–20 hours after pitching. The finished Koji wash lightens, shows a sediment layer and the airlock stops. Never taste this wash.

Method B: Malt Wash (Hot Saccharification)

7
Grind the beans into grits or flour for faster cooking. Whole beans work but need 3–4 hours of boiling (versus 1–1.5 h for grits/flour).
8
Heat 25 L of water to 50–55 °C. Pour the beans into the cooking vessel, adding the water slowly while stirring to avoid lumps. Fill the vessel to max 75%.
9
Raise the temperature to 60 °C and hold for 15 minutes, stirring occasionally so the wort does not scorch at the bottom.
10
Bring to a boil and cook for 1–4 hours on medium heat until a homogeneous mash forms. Stir near the bottom.
11
Protein pause: cool the mash to 44–59 °C and hold in that range for 15 minutes. This destroys proteins so the wash foams less, is less viscous and does not scorch during distillation.
12
Raise the temperature to 63–68 °C (never above 71 °C) and add the ground malt gradually while stirring.
13
Even out the temperature to 61–65 °C and cover the vessel. Hold for 60–90 minutes with slow heating (wrap in warm clothes or use a hot-water bath). If the wort tastes sweet, saccharification succeeded.
14
To avoid infection, cool the wort to 25–29 °C as fast as possible, adding 5 L of cold water. An ice bath works too.
15
Transfer the wort to the fermentation vessel and pitch spirit yeast (per package) or baker's yeast (50 g dry or 250 g pressed). Stir and fit an airlock.
16
Move the bean wash to a dark place at a stable 20–28 °C. Fermentation usually lasts 4–8 days. The finished wash is not sweet, lightens, shows a sediment layer and the airlock stops (glove deflates).

Distillation (both methods)

17
Strain the finished wash through 2–3 layers of cheesecloth, removing solid parts that could scorch during distillation.
18
First run without fraction separation. Collect until the stream strength falls below 20%.
19
Measure the strength of the first-run bean spirit and calculate the pure alcohol (strength % × volume L ÷ 100). Charcoal cleaning is optional.
20
Second run: collect the first 10% of the pure-alcohol amount separately as "heads" — a harmful fraction unfit for drinking.
21
Collect the "body" until the stream strength falls below 45%, then stop or collect "tails" separately to add to a new wash batch.
22
Dilute the bean moonshine ("body") with water to 40–45%, bottle in glass and rest in a dark cool place for 3–4 days for taste stabilisation.
💡 Beans have no unique aroma worth preserving, so the choice between Koji and malt is purely practical: Koji is simpler but slower; malt is faster and adds volume.
