🍍 Pineapple Moonshine

A light fruit aroma and a soft taste; with bottle aging the aroma shifts toward light vanilla tones. A good way to process non-standard fruit that would otherwise spoil. The technology below extracts maximum aroma while keeping the taste soft.

Category
Fruit
ABV
40–45%
Difficulty
Medium
Fermentation
4–50 days
Yield
up to 165 ml/kg (40%)
⚠️ Educational purposes only. Home distillation is illegal in many countries without a licence. Check your local laws first.

Theory & Ingredients

Ripe pineapples contain up to 15% sugar, so 1 kg of pulp gives at most 165 ml of 40° spirit. Given the price of the raw material, the yield is usually raised with sugar (1 kg adds 1.1–1.2 L of 40%), but the more sugar, the weaker the aroma — stick to the recipe proportions.

Baker's or spirit yeast finishes the wash in 4–7 days but worsens the aroma; wine yeast or a raisin starter takes 25–50 days but keeps the aroma fully. If you have time, use wine yeast or a starter.

  • Pineapples — 2 kg
  • Sugar — 0.5–1 kg (optional)
  • Water — 5 L, plus 4 L per 1 kg of sugar
  • Yeast — 20 g dry (100 g pressed) or a raisin/berry starter

Wash Preparation

1
Keep the fruit in the freezer for 3–4 days to break down acids that hinder fermentation.
2
Thaw the pineapples. Cut off the top and bottom (they make the spirit bitter), peel the skin down to smooth pulp and cut out rotten parts.
3
Slice the pulp and mince it with a meat grinder or mixer. If your still has a bubble trap, freeze 100–150 g of pulp now to aromatize the distillate during the second run.
4
Place the pulp into the fermentation vessel. Add the sugar and stir; leave for 45–60 minutes (no longer, or it will sour) for better juice release. Skip this step if making the wash without sugar.
5
Add the water and the yeast (or starter). Stir, fit an airlock (or a medical glove with a pinhole) and leave at least 25% headspace for foam.
6
Move the vessel to a dark place at 18–28 °C. Fermentation lasts 4–50 days depending on yeast, sugar and temperature. It is finished when gas release stops, the wash lightens, a sediment layer forms and the taste shows no sweetness, only light bitterness.
7
Filter the finished wash through cheesecloth or dense cloth to remove pulp particles that could scorch; press the pomace well.

Distillation

8
Pour the wash into the boiler and run the first distillation without fraction separation. Stop when the stream strength falls below 25%.
9
Measure the distillate strength and calculate the pure alcohol: volume in litres × strength % ÷ 100. Dilute to 18–20%; do not clean with carbon or other methods, or the aroma will disappear.
10
Second run: collect the first 12–15% of the pure-alcohol amount separately as "heads" — a harmful fraction. If your still has a bubble trap, place the frozen pineapple pulp from step 3 inside it (it may go in unfrozen) to aromatize the distillate.
11
Collect the "body" until the stream strength falls below 40%, then stop or collect "tails" separately.
12
Dilute the pineapple moonshine to the desired drinking strength, bottle in glass, seal tightly and rest for 2–3 days in a dark cool place before tasting.
💡 Pineapple pulp is fibrous and gives no clean juice, so the wash is always thick; filter it well before distillation to avoid scorching and bitterness.
