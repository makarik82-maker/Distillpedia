🛠️ Equipment Checklist

Everything you need to get started, prioritized by importance: distillation first, then fermentation, measuring and safety.

This checklist is ordered by priority. Buy the mandatory distillation and safety gear first; fermentation and measuring tools come next; upgrades can wait until your second or third run.

Grain-specific gear (saccharification)

  • Base (white) malt — barley preferred; never fermented "red" malt (it cannot saccharify).
  • Grain mill or pre-ground malt — correct grist raises saccharification efficiency.
  • Large cooking pot (enamel/stainless) — for boiling grain porridge; fill ≤60–70%.
  • Alternative: enzymes A & G (amylase + glucoamylase) or Koji yeast for cold saccharification.

Mandatory safety equipment

🛑 These items are non-negotiable:
  • Fire extinguisher (dry chemical or CO₂) within arm's reach of the still.
  • Ventilation: open windows, exhaust fan, or outdoor setup.
  • Heat-resistant gloves and safety glasses.
  • First aid kit for burns and cuts.
  • Never use lead solder, automotive radiators or unknown metals.

Starter kit budget

  • Pot still + condenser: $150–400
  • Fermenter + airlock + siphon: $25–100
  • Alcoholmeter + thermometer + cylinder: $25–70
  • Cheesecloth, spoon, bentonite: $15–40
  • Safety gear: $50–120
  • Total: roughly $260–730 for a safe, working starter setup.
💡 Buy the still and safety gear first; everything else can be improvised for the first run (a clean food-grade bucket, a glove airlock, a kitchen sieve). Upgrades (column, copper packing, refractometer) pay off from run #2–3.
