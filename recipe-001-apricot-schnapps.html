🍑 Apricot Schnapps (German Fruit Brandy)

A traditional German fruit spirit made by double-distilling wild-fermented apricot mash — no added sugar, no commercial yeast. The result is an aromatic brandy that captures the pure essence of the fruit.

Category
Fruit
ABV
38–45%
Difficulty
Medium
Fermentation
20–45 days
Yield
~10 L mash
⚠️ Legal & Safety Disclaimer: This recipe is provided for educational purposes only. Distilling alcohol at home is illegal in many countries without a licence. Always check your local laws. Read our Distillation Safety Guide before attempting any distillation.

Ingredients

  • 10 kg ripe apricots — unwashed, wild yeast on the skin
  • 8–10 L water — chlorine-free, non-boiled

No sugar, no commercial yeast. The fruit ferments on its own wild yeast.

🛑 Safety reminder: The first fraction of any distillation ("heads") contains methanol and must be discarded. Never consume it. See Distillation Safety Guide.

Step-by-Step Process

1
Prepare the fruit. Remove pits from unwashed ripe apricots. Mash the fruit by hand into a smooth, viscous paste. Do not wash the apricots — the natural wild yeast on the skin is essential for fermentation.
2
Start fermentation. Transfer the apricot paste into a fermentation vessel. Add 3 L of water and stir well. Cover the opening with cheesecloth and place in a dark, warm spot (18–28 °C) for 3–4 days.
3
Add remaining water. When signs of fermentation appear (foam on the surface, a yeasty smell), add another 5–7 L of water and stir thoroughly. The exact amount depends on the sugar content of the apricots — the goal is a liquid wash.
4
Ferment. Fit an airlock and move the vessel to a dark room at 18–28 °C. Since no commercial yeast is added, fermentation will be slow — typically 20 to 45 days.
5
Confirm fermentation is complete. Signs: no bubbles from the airlock for 2 days, the wash tastes dry (no sweetness), and a layer of sediment has formed at the bottom.
6
First distillation. Siphon the wash off the sediment and transfer it to the still. Run a simple pot-still distillation. Stop collecting when the ABV in the stream drops below 30%.
7
Measure and dilute. Measure the ABV of the distillate and calculate the total amount of pure alcohol. Dilute with water to 20% ABV.
8
Second distillation with cuts. Distill again. Collect the first 10–12% of pure alcohol volume as "heads" in a separate container — discard this fraction, never consume it. Continue collecting the "hearts" until the ABV in the stream drops below 45%.
9
Dilute and rest. Dilute the schnapps with water to 38–45% ABV. Transfer to a glass container, seal tightly, and rest in a dark, cool place for 3–5 days to smooth the flavour.
💡 Editor's tip: The same technique works for plums, pears, cherries, apples, and raspberries. Wild fruit often gives a more distinctive aroma than cultivated varieties.

Serving Notes

  • Serve fruit schnapps at 16–20 °C in 20–30 ml portions.
  • Inhale the aroma before sipping to appreciate the fruit character.
  • Grain-based schnapps is best served well chilled.
  • Traditional pairings: fresh fruit, Bavarian sausages, herring.
