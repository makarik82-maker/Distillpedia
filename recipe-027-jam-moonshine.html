🍓 Jam Moonshine (Proper Wash Recipe and Distillation)

The aroma and taste of jam moonshine is closer to sugar moonshine than to the original fruits or berries, but with a competent approach, some tones of the initial raw material remain (if desired). The main thing is to correctly compose wash proportions, then do double fractional distillation.

Category
Fruit
ABV
40–45%
Difficulty
Medium
Fermentation
5–50 days
Yield
~5 L jam
⚠️ Legal & Safety Disclaimer: This recipe is provided for educational purposes only. Distilling alcohol at home is illegal in many countries without a licence. Always check your local laws. Read our Distillation Safety Guide before attempting any distillation.

Choosing the Right Jam

Any jam or marmalade is suitable, with or without pits: cherry, apple, raspberry, plum, apricot, etc. If you need moonshine for tinctures, different types can be mixed in one wash. The jam itself can be very old (stored for more than 5 years), fermented, and even slightly mouldy, but not soured — necessarily without a sharp vinegar smell. There are usually no problems with homemade jam, but store-bought sometimes ferments poorly due to preservatives that suppress yeast. This problem is solved by boiling (technology described in the wash recipe).

Ingredients

  • Jam — 5 L
  • Sugar — 1 kg (optional)
  • Water — 29 L
  • Yeast — 120 g dry or 600 g pressed baker's, or spirit (wine) for 35 L of must
  • Citric acid — 24 g (optional, 4 g per 1 kg raw material)

Wash proportions: 5 L water per 1 L jam, plus another 4 L per 1 kg sugar. Spirit and wine yeast per package instructions based on must volume. Baker's yeast: 20 g dry or 100 g pressed per 1 kg raw material (jam + added sugar).

🛑 Important: The yield of jam moonshine depends on the sugar content of berries (fruits) and the amount of added sugar. Every housewife has her own recipe, so there are no unified values here. From 1 kg of sugar (sum of natural and added), up to 1.2 L of 40% moonshine is theoretically obtained, but the practical quantity is 12–20% lower due to alcohol losses during distillation. Always discard the "heads" fraction — 12–15% in this recipe. See Distillation Safety Guide.

Step-by-Step Process

1
Remove mould. Remove the mouldy layer and another 2–3 cm of jam unaffected by fungal spores so as not to spoil the moonshine aroma.
2
Place in cooking pot. Put all jam in a pot or bucket for cooking, add sugar if desired.
3
Boil water. Boil 75% of total water volume in a separate container.
4
Mix jam with water. Pour boiling water into the jam, stirring until homogeneous consistency. Thick jam should not accumulate at the bottom or stick to the walls.
5
Cook the syrup. Bring the mixture to a boil, reduce heating power to below medium, then cook the syrup for 10 minutes, stirring and removing foam. Boiling and cooking are needed to suppress all pathogenic microflora, destroy preservatives in store-bought jam, make the must liquid, and release sugars.
6
Cool the must. Turn off heating, cover the must with a lid, and cool to the recommended yeast temperature — usually 26–30 °C.
7
Activate the yeast. Dilute spirit or wine yeast per package instructions. Dry baker's yeast: sprinkle evenly over the surface of syrup (300 ml water and 50 g sugar) at about 30 °C, cover with a lid, and leave for 10–40 minutes until foam appears. Pressed yeast can be added directly from the refrigerator, crushed with clean hands.
8
Transfer to fermentation vessel. Pour the must from the pot into the fermentation vessel. Add citric acid (optional) and remaining water (25%). Stir. Fill the vessel max 75%, leaving room for carbon dioxide and foam. Fresh (unboiled) water contains oxygen, which yeast needs at the initial stage for reproduction.
9
Add yeast and ferment. Add yeast to the must, stir again. Close with an airlock, leave the wash in a dark room (cover with dense fabric) at 18–28 °C. Depending on yeast and temperature, jam wash ferments for 5–50 days. Signs of fermentation completion: gas release from airlock stopped (glove deflated); wash became lighter, and a layer of loose sediment accumulated at the bottom; foam disappeared from the surface (individual small islands remain); wash smells like alcohol and is not sweet (all sugars converted) — the most important sign.
10
Filter the wash. Siphon the finished wash off sediment (preferably through a hose or tube like gasoline from a tank), then strain through cheesecloth to remove any solid residues that could burn during distillation, making the moonshine bitter.
11
First distillation. Distill jam wash the first time, preferably with a simple apparatus (distiller) with minimal strengthening (column height). Optimal distillation speed — moonshine (low wines) comes out warm, but must be without steam.
12
Finish first run. Finish distillation when the ABV in the stream drops below 25 degrees for regular apparatuses, and at 5 degrees for columns over 50 cm high assembled in pot still mode. If the time and energy spent seem justified, you can distill the wash almost to zero ABV to "extract" as much alcohol as possible.
13
Measure and calculate. Measure the ABV of the entire distillate at 20 °C. Calculate the amount of absolute alcohol: ABV percentage multiplied by volume in litres, then divided by 100. All distillation calculations and temperature corrections can be done in a moonshine calculator.
14
Dilute and clean. Dilute the low wines (first distillation moonshine) to 18–20% ABV. To remove residual smell and taste of the original raw material, clean the moonshine with charcoal (optional).
15
Second distillation. Distill the low wines the second time: after the liquid boils, adjust power so the moonshine comes out in drops or a thin stream, and is close in temperature to the cooling water.
16
Collect the heads. Collect separately the first 12–15% of the amount of pure alcohol. This harmful fraction ("heads") contains many harmful impurities and is not suitable for consumption.
17
Collect the hearts. Collect the drinking part of the moonshine ("body") until the ABV in the stream drops below 42%. Then finish distillation (turn off heating) or collect "tails" separately to add them to a new batch of wash before the next distillation.
18
Dilute and rest. Dilute the drinking part ("body") of jam moonshine with soft water (with minimal content of alkaline earth metal salts) to 40–45% ABV. Pour into glass bottles, close tightly, and leave in a cool place for at least 3–4 days before tasting to stabilize the taste.
💡 Editor's tip: Jam moonshine is closer to sugar moonshine in taste than to the original fruit, but it's an excellent way to process old or unwanted jam. The result is a good base for tinctures and liqueurs. If you want to preserve some fruit tones, use wine yeast and skip the charcoal cleaning step.
