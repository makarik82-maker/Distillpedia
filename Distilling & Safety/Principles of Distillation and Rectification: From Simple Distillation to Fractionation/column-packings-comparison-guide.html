Introduction: The Packing Is the Heart of the Column

A rectification column without packing is just a pipe. Vapor flies through it without meeting resistance, without contacting liquid, without separating into components. At the outlet you will get the same low wines at 30–40% ABV as with simple distillation. All the magic of repeated evaporation and condensation that turns a 10% wash into a 96% rectificate happens on the surface of the packing.

The packing is the material that fills the column and creates the surface on which the rising vapor meets the descending reflux. The larger this surface, the better it is wetted by liquid, and the more evenly the flows are distributed, the more efficient the separation. The same boiler, the same dephlegmator, the same wash — but with different packings you will get 85% or 96% strength. The difference is a few centimeters of wire, ceramic, or stone inside the pipe.

In home distilling communities, arguments about packings have not subsided for years. SPN versus Raschig rings. Copper versus stainless steel. Springs versus scrubbers. Pebbles versus expanded clay. Everyone defends their choice, but rarely can anyone explain why one packing works better than another and exactly which properties determine efficiency.

This article is a systematic review of all the main packing types available to the European home distiller. We will break down the physics of the process, the measurable characteristics, the practical results, and give specific selection recommendations.

Chapter 1. What the Packing Does: The Physics of Vapor–Liquid Contact

1.1. Three Conditions for Separation

For rectification to work, the packing must provide three conditions:

📐

1. Large Contact Area

Vapor and reflux must meet on as large a surface as possible. The larger the area, the more molecules participate in mass transfer simultaneously.

⚖️

2. Even Flow Distribution

Vapor must rise through the entire column cross-section, and reflux must flow down evenly without forming streams and dry zones. Otherwise contact is minimal.

⏱️

3. Sufficient Contact Time

Vapor and liquid must stay in contact long enough for equilibrium to be established. Too fast a vapor passage reduces the efficiency of each stage.

1.2. How the Packing Creates Contact

When reflux flows down the packing, it forms a thin film of liquid on the surface of each element. Vapor, rising from below, passes through the gaps between elements and contacts this film. Heat and mass transfer occur: ethanol moves from the liquid into the vapor, water moves from the vapor into the liquid.

The thinner the film and the larger the surface area over which it is distributed, the more efficient the contact. Therefore, the best packings are those that create the maximum surface area with the minimum resistance to vapor flow.

1.3. What Happens If the Packing Is Poor

🌊 Channeling

Reflux flows down in a few streams; the rest of the surface is dry. Vapor–liquid contact happens only in these streams. Efficiency drops 2–3 times.

🌊 Flooding

With too dense packing or small free cross-section, vapor cannot pass through. Liquid accumulates, the column "chokes," boiler pressure rises.

📉 Low Surface Area

Large elements (20 mm pebbles, brick pieces) give a small contact area. Separation is inefficient; outlet strength is 80–88% instead of 95–96%.

Chapter 2. Key Characteristics: What to Measure and Compare

📏 HETP — Height Equivalent to a Theoretical Plate

The main indicator of packing efficiency. It is the height of the packing layer at which separation equivalent to one theoretical plate is achieved.

The lower the HETP, the more efficient the packing. A packing with 3 cm HETP gives twice as many theoretical plates per meter of column as one with 6 cm HETP.

Optimum: 3–5 cm

🧮 Specific Surface Area

The packing surface area per unit volume of the column (m²/m³). The larger it is, the larger the vapor–liquid contact area.

Optimum: 500–1200 m²/m³

🕳️ Void Fraction (Free Cross-Section)

The percentage of column volume not occupied by packing material. It determines vapor flow resistance and flooding risk.

High void fraction (>85%) — low resistance but less surface area. Low (<60%) — high area but flooding risk.

Optimum: 75–92%

💧 Wettability

The ability of the packing surface to hold a liquid film. Hydrophilic surfaces (ceramic, glass, oxidized metal) are wetted better than hydrophobic ones (polished metal, plastic).

Optimum: High

🔩 Material

Determines chemical inertness, durability, and additional properties (for example, copper binds sulfur compounds).

Stainless, copper, ceramic
Number of theoretical plates in a column:
Number of plates = Packing height ÷ HETP
Example: a column 150 cm tall with packing HETP of 4 cm → 150 ÷ 4 = 37.5 theoretical plates.

Chapter 3. Packing Types: A Detailed Review

🌀

3.1. SPN — Spiral Prismatic Packing "Springs" / Selivanenko springs

★★★★★

🏆 Top Pick

What it is: spirals of stainless wire 0.3–0.5 mm in diameter, coiled into springs 3–5 mm in diameter and 5–10 mm long.

HETP3–4 cm
Specific Area800–1100 m²/m³
Void Fraction85–92%
MaterialAISI 304 / 316

✅ Advantages
  • One of the best HETP values among available packings.
  • High void fraction — low vapor flow resistance.
  • Even reflux distribution.
  • Easy to fill and pour out of the column.

Verdict: one of the best packings for home rectification. Optimal combination of efficiency, availability, and simplicity.

★★★★★

What it is: hollow cylinders (rings) of ceramic, glass, or metal. Diameter 4–10 mm, height equal to diameter, wall thickness 0.5–1.5 mm. Invented by Friedrich Raschig in 1914 — the oldest type of industrial packing.

HETP5–8 cm
Specific Area400–700 m²/m³
Void Fraction65–80%
MaterialCeramic / glass / steel
WettabilityHigh (ceramic)
Cost€8–20 / L
✅ Advantages
  • A classic proven by a century of industrial use.
  • Ceramic rings are wetted excellently; the liquid film is even.

3.4. Panchenkov Packing (RPN) Regular wire packing / wire mesh

★★★★★

What it is: a woven mesh of thin stainless or copper wire, rolled into a roll and inserted into the column. Widely used in the CIS countries.

HETP5–8 cm
Specific Area600–900 m²/m³
Void Fraction90–95%
MaterialStainless / copper
WettabilityMedium
Cost€5–15 / L

3.5. Copper Mesh & Copper Scrubbers Sulfur-binding supplement

★★★★★

What it is: a woven copper mesh or copper dish scrubbers. Used as a budget packing or as an addition to the main packing for binding sulfur compounds.

HETP6–10 cm
Specific Area400–600 m²/m³
Void Fraction85–93%
MaterialCopper
WettabilityMedium
Cost€3–10 / L
✅ Advantages
  • Binds sulfur compounds. Copper reacts with hydrogen sulfide, mercaptans, and dimethyl sulfide, forming insoluble copper sulfide (a black coating). This removes the unpleasant odors typical of grain and potato washes.
  • Cheap and available (scrubbers are sold in any hardware store).
  • Easy to replace.
❌ Disadvantages
  • Low efficiency as a standalone packing (HETP 6–10 cm).
  • Copper oxidizes: the surface becomes coated with oxide and sulfide and needs regular cleaning.
  • Hardware-store scrubbers may contain impurities (zinc, tin, coatings). Use only pure copper.
  • Service life of 5–15 runs, after which the scrubbers need replacing.
Output strength: 85–92% ABV as a standalone packing with a 150 cm column.
Verdict: not recommended as the main packing for rectification. Recommended as an addition: 10–20 cm of copper mesh or 1–2 scrubbers at the top of the column above the main stainless packing. This gives sulfur cleanup without losing efficiency.

3.6. Pebbles & Expanded Clay (LECA) The budget beginner option

★★★★★

What it is: small stones (pebbles) or expanded clay granules (LECA) 5–20 mm in diameter. A budget option often used by beginners.

HETP8–15 cm
Specific Area200–400 m²/m³
Void Fraction40–55%
MaterialStone / clay
WettabilityMedium / high
Cost€1–5 / L
✅ Advantages
  • Minimal cost.
  • Availability: pebbles can be collected on a shore; LECA bought at a hardware store.

❌ Disadvantages
  • Low efficiency. HETP 8–15 cm means you need a column 2.4–4.5 meters tall for 30 theoretical plates — unrealistic at home.

Verdict: not recommended for rectification. Suitable only for experiments or as a temporary solution when nothing else is available. If you use pebbles — wash, boil, and bake them in the oven at 250 °C. LECA is preferable to pebbles but still inferior to specialized packings.

Chapter 5. How Packing Size Affects Efficiency

5.1. The Rule: Column Diameter ÷ Packing Size ≥ 8–10

For even reflux distribution and to prevent channeling, the column diameter must be at least 8–10 times the size of the packing element.

⚠️ If the packing size is too large for the column diameter, the elements pack unevenly, forming large gaps and channels. Reflux flows down the column walls, bypassing the packing. Efficiency drops.

5.2. Smaller Packing Is More Efficient but Creates Resistance

Reducing packing size increases specific surface area and lowers HETP. But at the same time, void fraction decreases and vapor flow resistance grows.

Practical optimum for a home column 38–50 mm in diameter:
  • SPN: spring diameter 3–4 mm.
  • Raschig rings: diameter 4–6 mm.
  • Berl saddles: size 4–6 mm.

Smaller packing (2 mm) gives a better HETP but creates flooding risk at high heating power. Larger (8–10 mm) reduces resistance but worsens separation.

Chapter 6. How the Material Matters

🔩 Stainless Steel (AISI 304, AISI 316)

  • Chemical inertness: does not react with wash, alcohol, or acids.
  • Durability: does not corrode or oxidize. Service life 10+ years.
  • Wettability: medium. Polished steel is wetted worse than ceramic. To improve wettability, degrease new stainless packing (wash with soda solution or alcohol) and run 1–2 blank distillations with water.
  • Does not bind sulfur compounds.

🟠 Copper

  • Sulfur binding: copper reacts with hydrogen sulfide (H₂S), mercaptans (R-SH), and dimethyl sulfide (DMS), forming insoluble copper sulfide (CuS). This removes unpleasant odors.
  • Catalytic effect: copper helps decompose some aldehydes.
  • Drawbacks: oxidizes, requires regular cleaning (citric acid solution 10 g/L). Service life 5–20 runs depending on usage intensity.
  • Recommendation: use copper as an addition to the main stainless packing, not a replacement.

🏺 Ceramic

  • Chemical inertness: does not react with anything.
  • Excellent wettability: the rough ceramic surface holds a liquid film well.
  • Fragility: breaks when filling, pouring out, or transporting.
  • Heat resistance: withstands high temperatures but cracks at sharp temperature changes.

🔮 Glass

  • Chemical inertness.
  • Good wettability.
  • Fragility.
  • Transparency (for laboratory purposes).

Chapter 7. How to Fill the Column Correctly

7.1. Preparing the Packing

A

SPN, Raschig Rings, Saddles

  • Wash the packing with hot water and detergent.
  • Degrease: soak in food-grade soda solution (2 tablespoons per liter of water) for 30 minutes.
  • Rinse with clean water.
  • For stainless steel: run 1–2 blank distillations with water to "break in" the surface.
B

LECA, Pebbles

  • Rinse under running water.
  • Boil for 30 minutes.
  • Bake in the oven at 250 °C for 1 hour (to remove organics).
  • Cool and use.
C

Copper Scrubbers

  • Wash with hot water.
  • Soak in citric acid solution (10 g/L) for 15 minutes to remove oxides.
  • Rinse with clean water.
  • Use immediately.

7.2. Filling the Column

1

Install a support mesh at the bottom of the column (above the vapor inlet from the boiler). The mesh prevents the packing from falling into the boiler. Use a stainless mesh with 1–2 mm cells.

2

Pour in the packing in small portions, periodically shaking the column for even distribution. Do not tamp — the packing should lie loosely.

3

For SPN and Raschig rings: pour in a "rain," letting the elements fall freely. They will pack most evenly.

4

For RPN: roll the mesh into a roll with a diameter slightly smaller than the column's inner diameter and insert it. The roll should fit snugly against the walls but not be crumpled.

5

Install a top support mesh or limiter so the packing does not shift during operation.

6

Leave 2–3 cm of free space at the top of the column (below the dephlegmator) for even reflux distribution.

Chapter 8. Practical Selection Recommendations

🍬 8.1. For Sugar Wash (Neutral Spirit)

Goal: maximum strength and purity; sulfur compounds are minimal.
Recommendation: stainless SPN, spring diameter 3–4 mm. Column 120–150 cm. Reflux ratio 3–5.
Alternative: Raschig rings 4 mm (ceramic or stainless). Column 150–200 cm.

🌾 8.2. For Grain Wash (Whiskey, Bourbon)

Goal: high strength + removal of sulfur compounds.
Recommendation: stainless SPN (main packing, 100–130 cm) + copper mesh or 1–2 copper scrubbers at the top of the column (10–20 cm). Copper binds the hydrogen sulfide and mercaptans typical of grain wort.
Important: if you are making an aromatic whiskey, rectification is not suitable. Use fractional distillation on a simple pot still. Rectification with packing is only for neutral grain spirit.

🥔 8.3. For Potato Wash

Goal: removal of sulfur compounds and methanol.
Recommendation: stainless SPN + copper section at the top of the column. Increased heads cut (12–15% of absolute alcohol) due to methanol.

🍎 8.4. For Fruit Wash

Goal: if the aim is neutral spirit for later infusion.
Recommendation: stainless SPN. Copper is not needed (sulfur compounds are minimal in fruit washes).
Important: if the aim is an aromatic fruit brandy (calvados, grappa, slivovitz), rectification is not suitable. Use fractional distillation.

💰 8.5. For a First Column (Budget Option)

Goal: minimal cost with acceptable quality.
Recommendation: RPN (Panchenkov packing) in stainless steel. Column 150 cm. Reflux ratio 4–6 (compensates for the low packing efficiency). Output strength: 90–94%.
Alternative: ceramic Raschig rings 4–6 mm. Cheaper than SPN, easier to buy.

Chapter 10. Frequently Asked Questions

Q Can I mix different packings in one column?

Yes, but consciously. A common scheme: bottom 2/3 of the column — SPN or Raschig rings (main separation), top 1/3 — copper mesh (binding sulfur compounds). Do not mix packings chaotically: this creates uneven flow distribution.

Q How long does the packing last?

Stainless SPN and Raschig rings — 10+ years without replacement. Ceramic — 3–5 years (depends on careful handling). RPN — 2–4 years. Copper scrubbers — 5–15 runs. LECA and pebbles — 1–2 years (they clog with deposits).

Q Can I use plastic elements as packing?

No. Plastic does not withstand 78–100 °C temperatures; it can deform and release toxic substances. Use only metal, ceramic, or glass.

Q What is better for a 38 mm column: SPN 3 mm or Raschig rings 4 mm?

SPN 3 mm gives HETP ~3.5 cm → 43 plates per 150 cm. Raschig rings 4 mm give HETP ~5 cm → 30 plates per 150 cm. SPN is more efficient. But if SPN is unavailable, Raschig rings are a worthy alternative.

Q Why does copper turn black in the column?

Copper reacts with sulfur compounds in the wash, forming copper sulfide (CuS) — a black coating. This is normal operation of copper as a sorbent. Blackened copper needs to be cleaned with citric acid solution (10 g/L, soak for 15–30 minutes).

Q Can I use pebbles from an aquarium store?

You can, but efficiency will be low (HETP 10–15 cm). Wash and boil them before use. Pebbles are suitable only for experiments or as a temporary solution.

Q How do I know the packing is clogged?

Signs of clogging: boiler pressure rises at the same heating power, output strength drops, uneven column operation (bubbling, flooding). Disassemble the column and wash the packing with hot water and soda.

Q Do I need to replace the packing after each run?

No. SPN, Raschig rings, and saddles last for years. Copper scrubbers are replaced every 5–15 runs. LECA and pebbles — once every 1–2 years. After each run, rinse the column with steam or hot water.

Q Can I make SPN myself?

Yes. Wind stainless wire 0.3–0.5 mm in diameter around a mandrel (nail, knitting needle) 3–4 mm in diameter. Cut into 5–10 mm long springs. The process is labor-intensive (1 liter of packing takes 2–4 hours of manual work), but the result is no worse than store-bought.

Q What packing is used in industrial rectification columns?

Industry uses plate columns (bubble-cap, sieve, valve plates) and structured packings (corrugated metal sheets). Random packings (Raschig rings, saddles) are used in small and medium installations. For home use, random packings are optimal for efficiency/cost/simplicity.

Chapter 11. Packing and Product Purity: Beyond Strength

11.1. Strength ≠ Purity

A strength of 96% does not mean absolute purity. Even at maximum strength, the rectificate retains traces of impurities with boiling points close to ethanol. The packing affects not only strength but also the degree of impurity separation.

11.2. Separation of Head Fractions

Methanol (64.7 °C), acetaldehyde (20.8 °C), ethyl acetate (77.1 °C) are more volatile than ethanol. They concentrate at the top of the column. An efficient packing (SPN, Berl saddles) provides clear separation: heads are collected in the first 5–10% of absolute alcohol, after which mainly ethanol remains in the collection.

A coarse packing (pebbles, LECA) does not provide sufficient separation. The head fractions are "smeared" across the whole collection, and some methanol and acetaldehyde get into the hearts.

11.3. Separation of Tail Fractions

Isoamyl alcohol (132 °C), furfural (162 °C), water (100 °C) are less volatile than ethanol. They concentrate at the bottom of the column and in the boiler. An efficient packing holds them at the bottom, preventing them from rising to the take-off node.

Chapter 12. Packing Care and Maintenance

1

Regular Rinsing

After each run, pass clean water steam through the column for 10–15 minutes. This removes wash residues, protein deposits, and yeast particles.

2

Deep Cleaning (every 5–10 runs)

  • Disassemble the column.
  • Pour the packing into a container.
  • Soak in hot food-grade soda solution (2 tablespoons per liter of water) for 1–2 hours.
  • Rinse under running water.
  • For copper elements: soak in citric acid solution (10 g/L) for 30 minutes.
  • Dry and refill.
3

When to Replace the Packing

  • SPN, Raschig rings: no replacement needed. If damaged (deformation, corrosion), replace the damaged elements.
  • RPN: replace every 2–4 years or when visibly worn.
  • Copper scrubbers: replace every 5–15 runs.
  • LECA, pebbles: replace every 1–2 years or when clogged.

Conclusion: The Packing Determines the Result

A rectification column is not the boiler and not the dephlegmator. It is the packing. It is on the surface of the packing that the heat and mass transfer happens that turns wash into rectificate. Everything else — boiler, dephlegmator, take-off node — merely provides the conditions for the packing to work.

Choosing a packing is a choice between 80% and 96% strength, between traces of methanol in the hearts and clean separation, between the smell of hydrogen sulfide and a neutral profile. The difference is determined by a few square meters of surface packed into a meter-and-a-half pipe.

Three Rules to Remember

🏆 SPN is the gold standard of home rectification HETP 3–4 cm, high void fraction, durability. If you can use SPN — use it. For grain and potato washes, add a copper section at the top of the column.
📐 Packing size must match the column diameter Column diameter ÷ packing size ≥ 8–10. Too-large packing creates channels. Too-small creates flooding.
🧼 The packing is not forever Rinse after each run. Clean copper with citric acid. Replace scrubbers and RPN when worn. A clogged packing works worse than having none at all.

Understanding how the packing affects separation turns rectification from "throw whatever you have into the pipe" into a conscious engineering process. And a conscious process gives a predictable, reproducible, and safe result.
