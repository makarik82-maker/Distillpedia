🍌 Banana Moonshine (Proper Wash Recipe and Distillation)

Banana moonshine has a pronounced fruit aroma, and the taste is memorable for slightly sweet creamy-banana tones. However, wash preparation has some features that need to be considered before adding yeast to the wash.

Category
Fruit
ABV
40–45%
Difficulty
Medium
Fermentation
5–45 days
Yield
~15 kg bananas
⚠️ Legal & Safety Disclaimer: This recipe is provided for educational purposes only. Distilling alcohol at home is illegal in many countries without a licence. Always check your local laws. Read our Distillation Safety Guide before attempting any distillation.

Ingredients

  • Bananas — 15 kg pulp
  • Water — 15 L, plus 4 L per 1 kg sugar
  • Sugar (optional) — up to 100 g per 1 kg bananas
  • Yeast — per instructions depending on volume
  • Cookies (crackers) or other foam suppressant

Baker's yeast: 20 g dry or 100 g pressed per 1 kg sugar (in bananas and added). Calculations can be approximate since exceeding the specified amount won't affect the wash.

🛑 Important: Banana wash foams very heavily, so before adding yeast, prepare a natural foam suppressant: cookies or crackers without fillings and glaze (10 g), refined vegetable oil (1 teaspoon), or kefir (2 tablespoons). Dosages are indicated per 10 L of wash. Always discard the "heads" fraction during distillation. See Distillation Safety Guide.

Step-by-Step Process

1
Prepare the bananas. Peel bananas from the skin (contains preservatives that slow fermentation and adds grassy-bitter aftertaste to the moonshine). Cut away rotten and mouldy areas. If possible, leave the pulp in the freezer for at least 12 hours (better 2–3 days) so that negative temperature destroys cell membranes, converting starch to sugar and releasing aromatic substances.
2
Grind the pulp. Grind banana pulp with a blender or meat grinder. Thaw frozen ones beforehand. Separately dissolve sugar in water. Pour the resulting syrup (or just water) into the pulp, stirring until homogeneous consistency. If the wash remained thick (hard to stir by hand), dilute more with water.
3
Check for spoilage. If during work even a slight smell of rot or mouldy mustiness appeared, you should boil the pulp together with sugar and 75% of water (watch that the wash doesn't burn), boil for a few minutes, then pour in the remaining 25% of water and cool to yeast addition temperature — below 30 °C. Boiling will destroy pathogenic microflora, minimizing the risk of contamination and souring.
4
Optional: enzyme treatment. To convert inulin and starch to sugar, you can heat the wash to 55–58 °C, sequentially add both enzymes (A and G) according to manufacturer's instructions, then maintain the specified temperature for an hour. If the temperature rises above 60 °C, inulin won't convert to fructose. In turn, starch enzymes are destroyed at 72 °C and above. Enzymes are also allowed to be added to room temperature wash (so-called "cold start") — without heating at all.
5
Activate the yeast. Activate yeast according to package instructions or dilute using the universal method.
6
Transfer to fermentation vessel. Pour the wash into the fermentation vessel, filling no more than 60% of volume due to abundant foam release. Add yeast or starter (temperature must not exceed 30 °C). Distribute the selected foam suppressant on the surface. Close the vessel with an airlock (mandatory, otherwise the wash will sour).
7
Ferment. Leave banana wash in a dark place (cover with dense fabric) with stable temperature 18–27 °C. For the first 4 days, check the condition every 8–10 hours to prevent the floating pulp residues and foam from clogging the airlock outlet tube. If necessary, open and stir slightly (increases foam), sinking the dense "cap" of pulp into the liquid, then add foam suppressant again (if needed).
8
Confirm fermentation is complete. Depending on yeast, sugar amount, and temperature, banana wash ferments for 5–45 days. The process is finished when: the surface cleared — almost no foam remains; carbon dioxide stopped coming out of the airlock (glove deflated); a layer of loose sediment appeared at the bottom (visible if you shine a flashlight); the wash itself smells like alcohol and is not sweet to taste (yeast converted all sugar).
9
Filter the wash. Siphon the fermented wash off sediment through a tube. Pulp residues can be squeezed with cheesecloth or dense fabric. Strain banana wash again through a fine sieve or cheesecloth, reliably getting rid of solid particles that could burn.
10
Assemble the equipment. Assemble equipment in simple distillation mode: column height up to 60 cm with no packing inside, disable dephlegmator and auxiliary units, leaving only the cooler (shell-and-tube or coil) and one thump keg (if provided in the design). In factory stills, the appropriate operating mode is usually called pot still.
11
First distillation. Pour banana wash into the distillation pot. Fill 35–70% of volume, leaving sufficient steam zone. Make the first distillation at maximum speed: distillate may flow warm and cloudy, but necessarily without steam. Turn off the still when the ABV in the stream drops below 20%. You can distill even "to water" if energy and time costs seem justified by a slight increase in quantity.
12
Measure and dilute. Determine the overall ABV of the first distillation moonshine at 20 °C, or correct for another temperature using a moonshine calculator. Calculate the amount of absolute (pure) alcohol: ABV percentage multiplied by volume in litres divided by 100. Dilute the distillate with water to 18–20%. Do not clean with activated charcoal or other methods, preserving the unique organoleptic properties of the drink.
13
Second distillation. Distill banana moonshine again. When the liquid boils, reduce power so the distillate flows in small drops or a thin stream, but remains cold. Collect separately the first 15% of output from the amount of pure alcohol (calculated at the previous step). This is the "heads" — the initial fraction with high content of harmful substances, so it's used only for technical purposes.
14
Collect the hearts. Collect the drinking part of the moonshine ("body") until the ABV in the stream drops below 43%. After that, turn off the heat or collect "tails" separately for future processing on a rectification column.
15
Dilute and rest. Dilute banana moonshine ("body") with softened water to 40–45% ABV. Pour into glass bottles or jars, seal tightly, and leave for at least 3 days in a cool place to stabilize. Shelf life is unlimited.
💡 Editor's tip: The darker the bananas, the better, since green fruits contain a lot of starch (20–25%) that yeast cannot process and little sugar (2–4%). Freezing for 12 hours to 3 days converts starch to fermentable sugars. Wine yeast gives the best flavor but takes up to 45 days; spirit yeast is faster (4–8 days) but less aromatic.
