⚗️ Distillation Safety Guide

Methanol and heads, fraction cuts, fire and pressure hazards, safe materials — and when to stop a run immediately.

⚠️ Legal notice: in most countries (USA, UK, Canada, Australia, most of the EU) distilling alcohol at home without a licence is illegal, even for personal use. New Zealand is a notable exception. This guide is educational: it explains hazards and harm reduction, not how to break the law.

Methanol: the invisible danger

Every fermentation produces mostly ethanol plus small amounts of methanol, which concentrates in the first fraction ("heads"). Methanol is toxic: even small ingested amounts can cause permanent blindness or death. High-pectin raw materials (unripe fruit, Isabella grapes, currants, rowan, pomace) produce more methanol — use ripe fruit and always cut heads generously.

  • Never drink or keep the heads fraction.
  • Typical heads cut: 8–15% of pure alcohol volume (12–16% for high-pectin fruit).
  • Work in a ventilated space; do not deliberately inhale vapors.

Fraction cuts: heads, hearts, tails

  1. Heads: sharp solvent smell; collect separately and discard (technical use only).
  2. Hearts: clean ethanol fraction; collect until stream ABV falls to ~40–45%.
  3. Tails: fusel oils and water; cloudy, unpleasant; redistil on a column or discard.
💡 Calculate pure alcohol first (volume × ABV% ÷ 100), then cut heads as a percentage of that number — not of total distillate volume.

Fire and explosion hazards

  • Alcohol vapor is extremely flammable and can ignite meters away from a flame or spark.
  • Ventilate the room; keep a fire extinguisher nearby; never leave a running still unattended.
  • Check all seals for vapor leaks (smell, hissing) before and during the run.
  • Prefer electric heating with controlled power over open flame where possible.

Pressure: never seal a still

  • A still must always have an open vapor path. A blocked output turns the boiler into a pressure bomb.
  • Before heating, confirm the vapor path and condenser are clear.
  • Never heat a fully closed container; never run with a clogged airlock on the wash side.

Materials matter

  • Use food-grade copper, stainless steel (304/316), borosilicate glass, food-grade silicone.
  • Never use lead solder, automotive radiators, heater cores or unknown scrap metal — lead poisoning is cumulative and irreversible.
  • Avoid ordinary plastics in contact with hot vapor or high-proof alcohol.

Stop the run immediately if…

🛑
  • You smell a vapor leak or hear hissing.
  • You feel dizzy, get a headache or nausea (vapor inhalation).
  • The distillate smells like solvent or nail-polish remover late in the run.
  • Equipment leaks, rattles or behaves unexpectedly.

Legal alternatives

  • Vapor infusion: flavoring store-bought neutral spirit with botanicals — legal almost everywhere.
  • Zero-proof distillates: botanical waters with no alcohol.
  • Water / essential oil distillation: non-food and household uses.
