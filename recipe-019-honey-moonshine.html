🍯 Honey Moonshine (with and without sugar)

Properly made honey moonshine is easy to drink and memorable with subtle honey tones in the aroma and taste. However, the wash preparation technology has several peculiarities: if you simply dilute honey in water and add yeast, fermentation will not start, or the moonshine will have a specific smell even after two distillations.

Category
Sugar
ABV
40–45%
Difficulty
Medium
Fermentation
5–50 days
Yield
~5 kg honey
⚠️ Legal & Safety Disclaimer: This recipe is provided for educational purposes only. Distilling alcohol at home is illegal in many countries without a licence. Always check your local laws. Read our Distillation Safety Guide before attempting any distillation.

Choosing the Right Honey

Any honey is suitable, even substandard: very old, with impurities, crystallized, and fermented (but not soured — without sharp vinegar tones). The type of honey (buckwheat, floral, linden) does not affect the organoleptic properties of the moonshine — the aroma and taste will be approximately the same. To get a "clean" drink without honey tones, a second distillation on a rectification or at least a mash column is required, because after a classic moonshine apparatus, an aftertaste always remains.

Honey contains 80–85% fermentable sugars (fructose, glucose, sucrose, maltose). Theoretical yield from 1 kg of raw material — up to 1 L of 40% moonshine, but in practice the quantity will be 15–20% lower, as some alcohol is lost during distillation.

Ingredients

  • 5 kg honey
  • Sugar (optional): 1–3 kg
  • Water: 25 L, plus 4 L per 1 kg sugar
  • Citric acid (recommended): 5 g per 1 kg raw material (honey + sugar)
  • Yeast: 120 g dry or 600 g pressed baker's, or other types per manufacturer's instructions depending on wash volume

Wash ratio: 5 L water per 1 kg honey, plus 4 L per 1 kg sugar. Part of the water will evaporate during boiling, so total hydromodule must be at least 1:4.

🛑 Important: Do not confuse honey moonshine with mead (medovukha) — a low-alcohol drink (3–10%) obtained by fermenting honey diluted in water with wine, beer, or "wild" yeast. Mead is closer in preparation technology to beer (sometimes even contains hops) than to moonshine. Honey wash can be considered a variety of mead if wine yeast was used for fermentation, but not spirit or baker's yeast. Always discard the "heads" fraction. See Distillation Safety Guide.

Step-by-Step Process

1
Melt crystallized honey. Heat crystallized honey to liquid state in a water bath or by any other method, e.g., place the container in a bath with 40–50 °C water.
2
Strain the honey. Strain liquid honey through cheesecloth or a fine kitchen sieve, removing all impurities: wax residues, honeycomb pieces, ash, sand, etc. Important: It is precisely the impurities that worsen the aroma and taste of honey moonshine, so filtration before boiling is an important preparation stage.
3
Calculate water. Calculate the required amount of water.
4
Make the syrup. Mix all honey and sugar in a pot or cooking bucket. Add 75% of water from the total volume (can be boiling water for faster dissolution). Stir until you get a homogeneous syrup consistency. Very important: honey should not settle at the bottom of the container, otherwise it will burn and the moonshine will be bitter. When choosing a cooking container, consider that 1 kg honey occupies 0.72 L of volume, and 1 kg sugar — 0.6 L.
5
Boil the syrup. Bring the syrup to a boil, then simmer on medium heat for 10 minutes, stirring periodically and skimming foam (if it appears). Boiling destroys pathogenic microorganisms, preventing wash contamination, and breaks down natural preservatives in honey, which accelerates fermentation.
6
Cool the syrup. Turn off the heat, cover the container with a lid, and cool the syrup to 35 °C.
7
Activate the yeast. Spirit and wine yeast per manufacturer's instructions. Dry baker's yeast: mix 300 ml water at 28–32 °C and 50 g sugar in a half-liter jar, sprinkle yeast evenly over the surface of the syrup, cover with a lid, and leave at room temperature for 10–40 minutes until foam appears. Pressed yeast does not need activation; it can be added to the wash directly from the refrigerator, crumbled by hand.
8
Transfer to fermentation vessel. Pour the honey syrup from the pot into the fermentation vessel, add citric acid (optional) and the remaining unboiled water (25%). During cooking, almost all oxygen evaporates from the water, which yeast needs for reproduction in the initial stage, and "raw" water replenishes this deficiency. Fill the container max 80% of volume to leave room for foam and CO₂.
9
Add yeast and ferment. Add yeast, stir, install an airlock, and move the wash to a dark place (or cover with dense fabric) at 18–28 °C. Depending on the yeast and temperature, honey wash ferments for 5–10 days on spirit or baker's yeast, and up to 50 days on wine yeast.
10
Confirm fermentation is complete. Signs: gas stops coming out of the airlock (glove deflates), wash clarifies (poorly visible in dark honey varieties), a layer of loose sediment appears at the bottom, foam almost disappears from the surface, wash smells like alcohol and has a slightly bitter taste without sweetness (yeast converted all fermentable sugars) — the most important sign.
11
Transfer to the still. Siphon the finished honey wash off sediment (preferably through a tube like gasoline from a tank) into the still, filling 30–75% of volume.
12
First distillation. Distill the wash at maximum apparatus speed without separating into fractions: "heads," "body," and "tails." The moonshine may come out warm, but must be without steam.
13
Finish first run. Stop collecting distillate when the ABV in the stream drops below 25% on regular apparatuses with minimal strengthening, and below 5% on columns in pot still mode. It is permissible to distill honey wash to almost zero ABV to reduce moonshine losses, but this approach requires additional time and energy costs, which is not always justified.
14
Measure and calculate. Measure the ABV of the resulting distillate at 20 °C, then calculate the amount of pure alcohol: volume in litres multiplied by ABV% divided by 100. All calculations and temperature adjustments can be done in the moonshine calculator.
15
Dilute. Dilute the moonshine with water to 18–20% ABV. To maximally remove honey tones in aroma and taste, clean the moonshine with wood activated charcoal. Charcoal cleaning is not a mandatory preparation step.
16
Second distillation. Distill a second time: bring to a boil, then adjust the power so the moonshine comes out in drops or a thin stream, and is cold like cooling water.
17
Collect the heads. Collect the first 12% of output from the amount of pure alcohol in a separate container. This part is not suitable for consumption as it contains many harmful impurities.
18
Collect the hearts. Smoothly raise the apparatus power so the moonshine comes out in a stable thin stream, but remains cold. Collect the drinking portion until the ABV in the stream drops below 42%.
19
Finish or collect tails. Finish distillation or collect "tails" separately to add them to the next batch of wash before distillation.
20
Dilute and rest. Dilute the finished honey moonshine with "soft" water (with minimal salt content) to drinking strength — 40–45%, transfer to glass containers, seal tightly, and before tasting leave for at least 2–3 days in the refrigerator or cellar to improve the taste.
💡 Editor's tip: The best honey moonshine in terms of softness and pleasant aroma is obtained on special mead yeast or regular wine yeast. However, fermentation time increases from 7–10 to 35–50 days, which is not always justified.
